# Chapter 12: Preventing the Buyer’s Reflex Resistance to Salespeople

## Core Idea
Buyers—including B2B buyers, including you—have an **automatic reflex resistance** to salespeople. You inherited poisoned water; it is not your fault, but it **is** your problem. Every touch needs a second objective: how will they perceive you? Lead with client issues, sound like a human, and assume legitimate need—not tire-kicking price shoppers.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Reflex resistance**: instinctive defense shield from a lifetime of retail lurkers, mispronounced-name telemarketers, and B2B blabber-demos. When to use: designing story, phone, VM, and call openings. How: expect it; design to keep the shield down rather than pretending you are “not like other salespeople” without changing behavior.
- **Dual objective on every interaction**:
  1. Primary: what you want (meeting, info, next step)
  2. Perception: how they will feel about you/your company; what working with you will be like; proof this is not about you; proof you are worth the time and driven to bring value
  How: write both objectives on the call card. If the primary can be hit in a way that makes you look like a pest, change the tactic.
- **Resistance filter (four levers)**:
  1. **Beliefs** — if you truly believe they will be better off, it shows. Phony is smelled fast. Problem-solver motive lowers the shield.
  2. **Sound** — prototypical “sales voice” is a skunk tail. Natural, friendly, casual, confident (Ch 9).
  3. **View of the prospect** — underperformers: tire-kickers, window-shoppers, only-price. Top reps: legitimate opportunity, stuck and seeking help. You telegraph the assumption.
  4. **Feelings** — winners: warm, even “I love these leads.” Laggards: pre-anger at people they have not met. Poker faces are rare in sales.
  Plus **words**: from sentence one they test whether you “get it.” Issue-lead (Ch 8) is the best preventer; self-focus lumps you with everyone they avoid.

## Key Concepts
- **Inherited reputation**: self-absorbed, manipulative, verbose, unreliable, high-ego, pest, time-waster, disconnected, poor listener.
- **Cheesy pitchman as qualifier**: the uncoachable rep who *liked* sounding salesy to “filter” buyers. Fired. Don’t be him.
- **Neon welcome vs. no soliciting**: issue language replaces the mental sign.

## Mental Models
- Use **“not your fault / your problem”** to skip victimhood and redesign tactics.
- Run the **perception questions** as a pre-flight on emails, VM, and openings.
- Assume **stuck and needing help** when a lead inbound-hits, unless evidence says otherwise.
- Prefer **problem-solver identity** over closer identity at first contact.
- Use **issue-first as the shield-down default** when you do not know this buyer yet; biography-first is the shield-up default.

## Anti-patterns
- “B2B buyers don’t resist *me*.” Yes they do.
- Cranking leads at breakneck speed, every word self-focused.
- Using sales-voice on purpose.
- Pre-judging leads as price-only tire-kickers.
- Bringing leftover anger from last week’s losses onto this week’s innocent prospects.
- Leading with how smart you are.

## Worked Example
Company with a strong marketing engine, weak conversion. Coach listens to outbound: zero awareness of how they sound; selfish motive obvious. Intervention: before each call, state objective + what you know about the lead + how the *prospect* should feel after 30 seconds.

Rookie refuses: he *likes* sounding like a salesperson; if they bristle, they weren’t going to buy. That qualifying theory maximizes resistance and minimizes conversions. He is released.

Contrast study, same firm: bottom group assumes window-shoppers hunting cheap; top group assumes a real situation and a request for help. Same leads, different music on the line.

**Pre-call card:** Belief: they may be better off with us. Sound: my normal voice. View: legitimate. Feeling: curiosity, not irritation. First 15 words: a client issue, not a company bio.

Run the same card on an email and a VM: would a busy buyer think “this person gets my Tuesday” or “another vendor”? If the latter, you are paying the inherited reputation instead of preventing it. Issue-first language is the practical swap that turns the mental “no soliciting” sign into a welcome.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reflex resistance is default. Plan for it.
2. You didn’t cause it; you still must sell against it.
3. Every tactic has a perception objective, not only a seller objective.
4. Beliefs, sound, view, feelings, and words all leak.
5. Issue-first language is the practical shield-down move.
6. How you mentally label leads becomes how you treat them—and how they treat you.
7. Uncoachable attachment to “sounding like a salesperson” is a firing offense against your own numbers.
