# Chapter 2: The “Not-So-Sweet 16” Reasons Salespeople Fail at New Business Development

## Core Idea
New-business failure is patterned. Scan the Not-So-Sweet 16 as a mirror, pick the three or four that are yours, and treat them as operating constraints—not personality trivia. Everyone in sales (including Weinberg) is guilty of a subset.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Not-So-Sweet 16**: a diagnostic checklist of attitudes, behaviors, and attributes that kill hunting. When to use: kickoff with a new team, quarterly self-audit, or when a “good relationship manager” misses new-logo quota. How: read the list without defending; name your top 3–4; fix those first.
  - Why it works: it levels the field before process coaching. Failure mode: treating #16 (“not built for it”) as an insult instead of a role-fit decision, or using the list to blame others.

### The 16 (exact names)
1. **Haven’t had to / don’t know how** — farming worked in boom times; “go make calls” is not training.
2. **Always waiting (on the company)** — waiting for materials, website, leads, marching orders. Top performers attack anyway.
3. **Prisoners of Hope** — (term credited in the wild to Tom Reilly / *Value-Added Selling*) they stop creating new opportunities because a few stale deals “should” close; “I’ll get a bluebird.”
4. **Can’t tell the story** — self-focused, long, confusing, no differentiation; the #1 daily weapon is dull.
5. **Awful target selection / lack of focus** — wrong list, or a decent list abandoned after one miss; constant redirection = no traction.
6. **Late to the party** — reactive to shoppers and RFPs a competitor helped write; vendor, not value creator.
7. **Negative attitude / pessimistic outlook** — winners own results; victim-excuse lists lose.
8. **Fake or pitiful phone effort** — fake = time-blocked calling hijacked by news, LinkedIn, email, lunch; pitiful = they dial but sound terrified.
9. **Not likable / don’t adapt / low EQ** — people buy from people they like; one-speed sellers mismatch buyer style.
10. **Can’t conduct an effective sales call** — no plan, no shared agenda, buyer takes the wheel, too much talk, presenting ≠ selling.
11. **Babysit existing accounts** — overserving known clients because it is easier, urgent, and paid the same. New Sales dies.
12. **Good corporate citizens** — Halloween committee, copier hero, “nicest person” who cannot say no. Top hunters are productively selfish with time.
13. **Don’t own their sales process** — default to buyer’s process; capabilities overview with zero discovery; **Premature Proposal Syndrome**.
14. **Don’t use and protect the calendar** — no written plan driving the calendar; no one finds 15 free minutes and starts calling targets.
15. **Stopped learning and growing** — “you can sell or you can’t”; unread on a craft that is getting harder (see Konrath, *SNAP Selling*).
16. **Not built for it** — some people are too conflict-averse/relational, or too analytical (analysis-paralysis), to hunt. That is a role problem, not a pep-talk problem.

## Key Concepts
- **Prisoners of Hope**: pipeline addiction to a few moldy deals; luck and bluebirds are not a process.
- **Late to the party**: you arrive after opinions, process, and maybe the RFP are already set.
- **Premature Proposal Syndrome**: proposing before decision criteria, players, and underlying issues are known.
- **EQ in sales**: manage self, emotions, relationships, and others’ perceptions of you.
- **Productively selfish time**: abrupt with time-wasters; hunting beats being voted nicest.
- **Bluebird**: a deal that “flies in.” Not a strategy.

## Mental Models
- Use the **16 as a mirror**, not a weapon: veterans pick 3–4; rookies treat the list as a hazard map.
- Use **“waiting vs. attacking”** when a rep’s next action is “after marketing finishes X.”
- Think of **focus as compounding**: one finite list, reworked, beats creative wandering.
- Use **#16 as a placement rule**: if one “no” ends the call and conflict is unbearable, put them in service, not hunting quota.

## Anti-patterns
- **One-and-done targeting**: miss once, throw away the strategic list, spray a new pile of folders.
- **Getting excited about unexpected RFPs** you did not create.
- **Fake phone block**: research, news, email, then “I’m hungry.”
- **Presenting = selling**; talking over buyer clues.
- **“If I don’t take care of it, who will?”** as cover for never prospecting.
- **Arrogance + ignorance**: “I’ve been doing this 30 years; nothing to learn.”

## Worked Example
A veteran account manager’s numbers crater after the boom. Audit against the 16:

- #1 Don’t know how to hunt (never modeled).
- #3 Two “sure thing” deals slipped 60 days; all week is spent checking on them (Prisoner of Hope).
- #8 Calendar says 8–10am prospecting; 45 minutes of news + LinkedIn + a CEO email; two timid dials.
- #11 95% of time on existing clients because commission pays the same.
- #13 Buyer asked for a capabilities deck; they went in naked—no discovery.

Intervention, in order: stop waiting (#2); write a finite target list (#5) and stay on it; time-block real phone with inbox closed (#8, #14); tell the story around *their* issues (#4); own a sales process so the first meeting is not a presentation (#10, #13). If after honest effort they still cannot push past a first “no,” that is #16—change the role.

## Key Takeaways
1. Failure is a short list of habits. Name yours.
2. Top hunters do not wait on the company.
3. Hope is not a pipeline strategy; keep creating opportunities.
4. Story + focused targets + phone + structured calls beat talent wandering.
5. Overserving current accounts and corporate niceness have a real opportunity cost: New Sales.
6. Own process, calendar, and learning—or the buyer and the inbox will own you.
7. Some people should not hunt. Admit it early.
