# Chapter 14: Planning and Executing the Attack

## Core Idea
Targets and weapons are academic until you take the field. **No one defaults to prospecting.** Time-block it, work the math, write a personal business plan that drives the calendar, pre-commit travel, and keep the pipeline healthy with **⅓ targeted / ⅓ active / ⅓ hot**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **No one defaults to prospecting**: hunting is the opposite of autopilot commuting. Existing accounts, internal meetings, fires, personal noise, and other people putting holds on your calendar will eat New Sales unless you get there first.
- **Time blocking**: appointments with yourself for priority work. When to use: always; especially if current hunting ≈ 0. How:
  1. Put blocks **in the calendar** (not in your head)
  2. **Keep** the appointment (Seinfeld rental-car rule: holding the reservation is the point)
  3. **Stay outbound** the whole block: no inbox grazing, DND, you are not a heart surgeon
  Dose: minimum ~90 minutes to find a groove; cap ~3 hours. Starting from zero: 2×2-hour blocks/week already changes the math. Heavy acquisition: 8–9 two-hour sessions/week still ≈ one-third of work time.
- **Work the math**: sales is a numbers game. High-frequency attack against the *strategic* list. You cannot control outcomes; you can control activity. Reverse-engineer from closed deals through your stages (example ratios in the worked example). Improve conversion by sharpening weapons (Ch 7–13); still put enough into the top.
- **Individual business plan** (five components):
  1. **Goals** — revenue, GP, new logos, net-new $, existing $, mix, named-account goals, private income target
  2. **Strategies** — where it comes from: focus accounts, geos/verticals, attached target list, cross-sell, how you’ll get in, team/events/referrals/social
  3. **Actions** — hours blocked, outbound calls, conversations, face-to-faces, trips, presentations, tours, proposals/$ proposed
  4. **Obstacles** — named now (product knowledge, support, tech, anti-sales noise, service burden, personal) so they can be resourced
  5. **Personal development** — seminars, books/blogs, industry training, writing/social/associations, mentor
  Present to peers (20 min + 10 Q&A) for accountability and stolen best practices, or privately with the manager. Review often: “Am I doing what I said?” Plan should match calendar.
- **Preplan travel / Sales Force One**: buy the ticket 4–6 weeks out *before* meetings exist—that forces calling to fill the trip. Change-friendly, sales-friendly airlines (Weinberg’s pick: Southwest: no change fees, last-minute affordable team trips, culture that lands you in a selling mood) beat carriers that hold your money hostage. He even biased targeting toward cities they served.
- **⅓-⅓-⅓ balanced effort** (cast in stone):
  - **Targeted** — committed to pursue; not yet in dialogue
  - **Active** — dialogue started, potential, must advance
  - **Hot** — urgency; proposal out or imminent
  Spend a third of hunting time in each. Gravity pulls everyone to Hot → Prisoners of Hope. Healthy pipeline: **full** (no one deal makes the year), **moving** (not moldy), **balanced** (all three segments). Too much Targeted = no near-term closes. Too much Hot / empty Active = future cliff.

## Key Concepts
- **Take the field / sales is a verb**.
- **Onesie-twosie prospecting**: does not work; hence blocks.
- **Activity metrics**: leading indicators you can control.
- **Prisoners of Hope** (Ch 2) as an execution disease.

## Mental Models
- Use **calendar as fortress**: you occupy prime selling slots before the company does.
- Use **backward math** when the goal is “12 new logos” but the week has two timid dials.
- Use **⅓-⅓-⅓** as a weekly time audit, not a CRM taxonomy religion.
- Think **ticket as commitment device**, not a travel perk.

## Anti-patterns
- Super Bowl pregame: endless talk about selling, no plays.
- Open email during the prospecting block “just in case.”
- Letting anyone book over hunting time.
- Obsessing Hot deals; starving Targeted.
- No written plan, or a plan that never touches the calendar.
- Change-fee airlines training you *not* to book until meetings are confirmed (so trips never happen).

## Worked Example
Goal: 12 new closed deals. Stylized conversions: win 1 of 3 proposals; 3 of 4 fits go to proposal; 2 of 3 first meetings become fits; 1 of 2 conversations become meetings.

Work backward: 12 closes → 36 proposals → 48 fit/second-meeting opportunities → 72 first meetings → **144 proactive conversations**.

Calendar: three 2-hour blocks/week, DND, list on the desk. Tuesday AM = Targeted names only; Wednesday PM = Active next-steps; Thursday AM = Hot proposals/closes. That *is* ⅓-⅓-⅓.

Plan one-pager lists the 40-name list (Ch 5), 8 hours blocked, 20 conversations/week, 8 meetings/month, 2 trips pre-booked on the sales-friendly carrier to the two densest target cities—tickets bought before invites. Weekly review: is the calendar obeying the plan? Is the pipeline full, moving, balanced?

## Key Takeaways
1. No one prospects by accident. Block it, keep it, stay outbound.
2. 90–180 minute blocks; occupy the calendar first.
3. Math works—work it from closed deals backward to conversations.
4. Write the five-part plan; make it drive the calendar; review it.
5. Pre-book travel as a forcing function; prefer sales-friendly change policies.
6. ⅓ Targeted, ⅓ Active, ⅓ Hot—on purpose.
7. Healthy pipeline = full, moving, balanced.
