# Chapter 5: Selecting Targets: First for a Reason

## Core Idea
You cannot prospect if you do not know who the prospects are. Target selection is first in the New Sales Driver because it is one of the few truly *strategic* acts in sales: you are choosing where scarce time goes. A pile of folders is not a list.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Finite, Focused, Written, Workable list** (all four required):
  - **Finite**: lock a defined set; work and rework it until you get noticed. Do not discard after one miss.
  - **Focused**: vertical or account *type* so you learn the language, questions, and case studies; replicate wins. No prize for selling the widest variety. Path of least resistance, then focus like mad.
  - **Written**: one-page printout or color-coded whiteboard you can point to *now*. Scrolling CRM or thumbing a directory is not the same. Writing/posting increases activity.
  - **Workable**: sized to the sale, role, and cycle—enough to work thoroughly in a defined period, not so many they starve, not so few you surf the web. Dozen enterprise accounts vs. ~400 inside accounts can both be “right.”
  When to use: before any phone block or campaign. How: name names; print; commit a period; only then load weapons.
- **Existing-account segmentation (intentional imbalance)**: all accounts are not equal. Sort current customers into:
  1. **Largest** (dollars spent, not org size)
  2. **Most Growable** (best incremental revenue)
  3. **Most At-Risk** (highest chance of losing some/all)
  4. **Other** (everyone else—minimal mind share)
  Accounts can sit in more than one bucket. Overserve 1–3; starve Other. Kill the **milk run** (same route by habit). Inside reps: do not let the CRM daily task list replace this macro view.
- **Who/Why questions** (build the list from answers, not hope):
  - Who are best customers (industry, size, model, location)? Why did they start / why do they stay?
  - Who do we compete with? Why/when do they beat us? Why do prospects choose us?
  - Who used to buy? Why did we lose?
  - Who almost bought? Who has referred / who *should* refer?
  First pursuit: prospects that look, feel, and smell like best clients (softball down the middle).
- **Referral / indirect selling list**: when influencers *are* the path (Realtors → mortgage; construction managers → GCs; branch bankers → premier; architects specifying flooring), build a separate finite/focused/written/workable influencer list, track conversations and referrals by source type, then imbalance toward sources that send the best deals.
- **Dream targets**: 4–5 monster accounts that would make the year (language from Chet Holmes / Anthony Iannarino). Unique mini-attack each; small weekly time to advance. **Caution:** high risk / low probability—do not become a Prisoner of Hope; keep working normal targets.
- **Aim higher in the org**: executives are often nicer, smarter, bigger-thinking, less nickel-obsessed. Speak issues/results, not product minutiae. Worst case of starting high: ignore/no—you can still go down. Worst case of starting low: they say no, then going over their head creates enemies. Best consolation prize: executive refers you internally (“Susan asked me to connect with you”).

## Key Concepts
- **Strategic targeting**: rare chance to step back from repetitive execution and choose the battlefield. Leadership should weigh in; time is the equalizer.
- **Lookalike / best-customer clone**: highest-confidence hunt.
- **Book of Lists / Hoover’s / LinkedIn / trade shows / association directories**: identification tools *after* strategy, not a substitute for a finite list.
- **Scale down, don’t climb over**: start high, descend if needed.

## Mental Models
- Use **finite + rework** when a hunter “tries an account once” then changes industries.
- Use **intentional imbalance** when the calendar is a milk run or a CRM task hamster wheel.
- Use **dream targets as a side quest**, never the whole pipeline.
- Prefer **starting in the suite** when you can talk business issues; product pitches belong lower and later.

## Anti-patterns
- Reactive mode: no list because nothing is being targeted.
- Pointing at a pile of journals and database printouts: “that’s my list.”
- Creative wandering across customer types.
- Equal energy to every account on the route.
- Falling in love with one whale and stopping the rest of the process.
- Assuming executives are scarier; staying forever with purchasing.

## Worked Example
Direct-mail hunter lands a few large ad agencies. Pattern: agency production generalists botch DM; his expert team is the fix. Strategic decision: *only* agencies. Same story, same questions, growing case studies, reputation. Record year.

Rebuild that: Who/Why answers say best clients = mid-market agencies, 50–200 people, in three Southwest-served cities, who outsource production. Print 40 names (workable for the quarter). Add 5 dream holding-company logos with a 30-minute weekly mini-plan each. Segment current book: 8 Largest, 6 Most Growable, 4 At-Risk, rest Other. For each agency, first call the CMO or managing partner with issue language; if they point to print production, that internal referral is a win. Do not swap the 40 for a new vertical after two unanswered weeks.

## Key Takeaways
1. Targets first: no names, no attack.
2. List must be finite, focused, written, *and* workable.
3. Clone best customers before you get clever.
4. Segment existing accounts; practice intentional imbalance.
5. Influencer lists deserve the same rigor as end-user lists.
6. A handful of dream clients, plus the regular list—never instead of it.
7. Aim higher; you can always walk down the ladder, not safely up over a “no.”
