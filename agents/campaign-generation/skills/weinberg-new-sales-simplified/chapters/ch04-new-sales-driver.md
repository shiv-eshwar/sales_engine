# Chapter 4: A Simple Framework for Developing New Business (New Sales Driver)

## Core Idea
If you are not closing new business, the cause is in one of three places: targets, weapons, or attack. That is the New Sales Driver. It was born when Weinberg was forbidden to use his own process (wait for the partner, then “present and demo”) and promised himself: never again let someone else dictate your sales process.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The New Sales Driver** (exact three-part model):
  - **A. Select targets**
  - **B. Create and deploy weapons**
  - **C. Plan and execute the attack**
  When to use: any new-business initiative, individual or team; any post-mortem of missed new-logo goals. How: diagnose which of the three is broken, then work them in order. Targeting is first because you cannot attack what you have not named.
  - Why it works: it is a war-planning metaphor salespeople can run without mystique. Failure mode: skipping A (spray), skipping B (water pistol), or treating C as optional (all academic until you take the field). “Sales is a verb.”
- **Bold declaration (failure locator)**: if an individual or team is not acquiring new business, the cause is one or more of:
  - Poor target selection **or** lack of focus on selected targets
  - Lame weapons **or** lack of proficiency deploying them
  - Inadequate planning **or** lack of execution
  Most teams fail in more than one.
- **Assumptions before you call it “a sales problem”** (otherwise you are treating a strategy/comp/talent issue):
  1. Clear strategy, defined market place, demand for the offering
  2. Comp plan is not working *against* the desired effort
  3. Talent at least “average” (B- or better)
  Plus: pricing matches value delivered.

## Key Concepts
- **Personal process vs. borrowed process**: the Driver was intuitive hunting, then documented because coaching clients needed teachable modules (first public outing: Bank of America Premier Banking, St. Louis).
- **Never again**: after a learning-management startup assigned “wait, then present and demo,” Weinberg refused to outsource process ownership (see also Ch 13, Frank in the boardroom).
- **Present and demo**: two of his least-favorite sales words in one sentence.
- **Take the field**: targets + weapons are meaningless without calendared attack, declared “which weapons at which targets when,” then monitor activity.

## Mental Models
- Use the **three-box diagnosis** when pipeline is empty: wrong/unfocused targets, dull/unfired weapons, or no execution.
- Think of **prospecting as campaign planning**, not a mood: name the enemy (accounts), arm the jet, fly the mission.
- Use **“sales is a verb”** when the team loves theory decks more than Tuesday morning call blocks.
- Prefer **owning process** over partner-led or buyer-led theater, even when the partner “owns the relationship.”

## Anti-patterns
- **Call your friends and family** as enterprise targeting advice.
- **“It’s hot—every decent-size company could use it.”** That is not a target list.
- **Wait until the channel partner summons you.**
- **Show up passionate, present, demo**—discovery optional.
- **Making the model complicated** to hide lame effort (Intro / Ch 16).
- **Declaring a sales problem** when strategy, pricing, or a counter-incenting comp plan is the actual problem (Ch 3).

## Worked Example
New hire at a web LMS company brings a one-page attack plan and a dozen targeting questions (verticals, size, geography). Answers: start with family and friends; then “everyone with training content.” Assigned to a giant channel partner: *wait* to be invited; the partner will lead; your job is presentation + demo.

Outcome: company fails; hunter is fired for the first time. Lesson extracted: the formula he used at plastics and direct mail *was* the asset—select lookalike targets, sharpen weapons, execute. Being barred from it proved its value.

Codify it for a coaching firm. First delivery of the New Sales Driver: a Premier Banking team that needed to hunt new lines of business *inside* an existing portfolio. Same three moves: name the targets, arm the story/phone/call, put the attack on a calendar.

**Apply now:** write one page with three headings—Targets / Weapons / Attack. Under failure, tick which clause of the bold declaration is true (selection vs. focus; quality vs. proficiency; plan vs. execution). Do not skip to closing tricks (Ch 16).

## Key Takeaways
1. New Sales Driver: Select targets → Create/deploy weapons → Plan and execute the attack.
2. Diagnose missed new business into those three boxes; most teams fail in more than one.
3. Own your sales process. Never again outsource it to a partner, a buyer, or a “present and demo” ritual.
4. Confirm it is actually a sales problem (strategy, comp, talent) before coaching tactics.
5. Simplicity is the point. Complication is often a smokescreen.
6. Nothing happens until you take the field and measure activity.
7. Mid-book (Ch 5–14) is the operating manual for A, then B, then C.
