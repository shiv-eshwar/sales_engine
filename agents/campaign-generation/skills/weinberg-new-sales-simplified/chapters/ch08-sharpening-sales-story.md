# Chapter 8: Sharpening Your Sales Story

## Core Idea
A compelling story has three building blocks in a **fixed sequence**: client issues, then offerings, then differentiators. Combine them under a headline and transitional phrase to form the **Power Statement**—a one-page, two-to-three-minute internal source document. Lead with offerings and you scream commodity. No issues = no sale.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **“So What?” test**: on every sentence a seller says, ask “so what?” When to use: ride-alongs, call recordings, website copy. How: if the line is about you and the buyer could think “we already have a ___,” cut or reframe as an issue you address.
- **Three building blocks (sequence is the weapon)**:
  1. **Client issues addressed** — pains removed, problems solved, opportunities captured, results achieved (the spear tip).
  2. **Offerings** — simply what you bill for (short, unsold, sandwiched).
  3. **Differentiators** — why you are the best choice for *those* issues.
  Leading with offerings = commodity. Leading with differentiators = still about you. Leading with issues = attention, problem-solver posture, foreshadows discovery questions, and qualifies (if they feel none of the issues, move on).
- **Power Statement construction**:
  1. **Headline** — 1–2 sentences of category context.
  2. **Transitional phrase** — third-person testimonial shape: “[Buyer type] turn to [You] when…” or “[Role] look to us when they…”
  3. **3–7 client issues** — conversational bullets, provocative/emotional wording (frustrated, pressure, had it with…).
  4. **Offerings** — few direct sentences, no embellishment.
  5. **Differentiators** — intriguing lead-in + at least five reasons you win.
  When to use: live 2–3 minute delivery on a sales call; excerpt everywhere else. Internal document, **not a leave-behind**.
- **Sales Story Exercise** (messy, worth it): three blank pages—issues, offerings, differentiators. Brain-dump why best customers came and stay; pains of the wrong provider; cost of self-performing; opportunity cost of not using you. *Call* (don’t email) raving fans for their words. Mine marketing copy for customer-result phrases, not self-praise. Argue the issues list down to ~7; wordsmith to follow the transitional phrase. Differentiators next (include *you* if you are part of the difference). Offerings in under five minutes. Tinker for weeks; some teams need ~five revisions.
- **Excerpt operating system**: intro letter ≈ trimmed full statement; drip email = 1–2 issues (+ maybe one offering/differentiator); phone = mini (Ch 9); discovery questions = issues rewritten as open probes.
- **Commodity antidote**: even “same product” sellers can shift the conversation from “in stock / best price?” to issues (e.g., tired of automated attendants, want a dedicated team) plus differentiators (easy setup, low minimums, won’t oversell). Few things are true commodities; jumping to price makes them so.

## Key Concepts
- **Power Statement vs. elevator pitch / value prop**: he rejects elevator-length cram jobs; this is a two-to-three-minute encapsulation plus a source doc.
- **Third-person “they turn to us when”**: strongest claims feel like customer testimony, not brag.
- **No Issues = No Sale**: businesses do not change for no reason.
- **Internal only**: you pull copy *from* it; you do not hand it out.

## Mental Models
- Use **issues-first** whenever you need attention from a crazy-busy prospect (Konrath).
- Think **appetizer vs. entrée**: phone/email get two issue bullets; the call gets the full statement.
- Use **customer language** as raw material—voice inflection on a phone call beats a survey.
- Prefer **qualification by issues** over qualifying by demographics on the first touch.

## Anti-patterns
- Opening with “we make / we supply / we do.”
- Leading with how great you are.
- Inflating the offerings section (the weakest block).
- Treating the Power Statement as a brochure.
- Emailing favorite customers for story input (you miss emotion).
- Stopping at a first draft; skipping live use and revision.

## Worked Example
Reconstructed **premium security-services** Power Statement (paraphrased from Weinberg’s client pattern—do not paste book copy):

- **Headline**: Premier integrated security for owners, property managers, and corporates.
- **Transition**: Building owners look to us when…
- **Issues**: want security as a tenant-winning advantage; current system never did what was sold; liability and life-safety fear; embarrassed by officer image; unreliable, untrained, high-turnover guards; need real integration of people + monitoring + CCTV; no peace of mind in a crisis.
- **Offerings**: manpower, access, monitoring, mobile response, CCTV—stated plainly.
- **Differentiators**: true one-stop integration; in-house financing; vendor-agnostic gear; crisis specialty; officers clients treat as their own; clients don’t leave (and the few who did came back).

Notice: the issue block contains *zero* seller biography. A property manager hears their Tuesday, not your footprint.

**Exercise in an hour:** three pages → call two fans → cut to seven issues with emotional verbs → five differentiators that justify premium → two offering sentences. Then write three discovery questions from issue bullets (Ch 11).

## Key Takeaways
1. Sequence: issues → offerings → differentiators. Sequence is not optional.
2. Power Statement = headline + “they turn to us when” + 3–7 issues + short offerings + ≥5 differentiators.
3. So What? every line. No Issues = No Sale.
4. Internal source document; excerpt into letters, email, phone, questions.
5. Draft with the team and with customers’ spoken words; revise for weeks.
6. Commodity businesses still have non-price issues and differentiators—use them before price.
7. A loved story keeps you on message: your story is your story.
