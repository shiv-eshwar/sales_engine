# Patterns — Smart Calling

## Pattern: Smart Call Opening
**When to use**: First 10–20 seconds with a decision maker; also as voicemail body.
**How**: Identify yourself → one intel fact (assistant, news, site, initiative) → hint PVP in their world → weasel into questions (“I’d like to ask a few questions to see if I could provide some information”).
**Trade-offs**: Longer if relevant; one irrelevant sentence is already too long. Skip product names without results.

## Pattern: Positive Social Engineering
**When to use**: Before the buyer call and on every live non-buyer reach.
**How**: Name + company; ask for help; because (right person / prepare for VP / not waste their time); then questions. Salvage intel even when the buyer is out.
**Trade-offs**: Some refuse. Never misrepresent. If the boss bristles, reframe as respect for their time.

## Pattern: Screener-as-Buyer
**When to use**: Any assistant, operator, or executive admin.
**How**: Same four beats; results (not products) when asked “what’s this about?”; get their name; learn buyer preferences and names dropped; send “pretty ugly” material *to them* if literature is required.
**Trade-offs**: Takes seconds you used to “blow past.” Those seconds are how you get through later. Intimidation scripts bolt the door.

## Pattern: Never-Rejected Secondary
**When to use**: Every dial, especially after a no.
**How**: Primary = their *do*. Secondary = your minimum attempt (goodwill, current vendor, permission to return, planted future-condition + where to file your card). Score the day on both.
**Trade-offs**: Secondary is not an excuse to skip a brave primary. It is mental health plus later harvest.

## Pattern: RRR Interrupt
**When to use**: Instant “not interested,” “all set,” “happy with supplier.”
**How**: Soft tone + Oh/I see/That’s not a problem + a content question (where do you get X; when is the next project; what would better-than-satisfied look like; second source; next eval date). Do not rebut the reflex.
**Trade-offs**: Sometimes they repeat the no. Then secondary objective. Never the “I haven’t given you anything to be interested in” line.

## Pattern: Benefit → Question Line
**When to use**: Instead of reciting a benefit sheet.
**How**: Possible benefit → circumstances in which it is real → assumptive or loaded questions that make them describe the scene → iceberg below the first answer → write their words.
**Trade-offs**: You already may know from social engineering; still ask so *they* say it. Avoid “any needs?” and “what do you love about the incumbent?”

## Pattern: Recommend, Don’t Pitch
**When to use**: When they are receptive after questions.
**How**: Transition → paraphrase their words → results only (what that means to you) + proof + trial closes → commitment ask. Social proof and consistency frames.
**Trade-offs**: You will skip features they did not mention. That is the point. Pitching earlier creates objections.

## Pattern: Will-You Wrap
**When to use**: End of any call that will continue.
**How**: Why callback / you do / they do. Summarize. They pick a date tied to homework. Specific clock time; you call. ACTION notes + summary email. “If I send X, *will you* Y?” not “May I send X?”
**Trade-offs**: Feels “formal” on the phone — that differentiation is the asset. If they refuse all homework, stop chasing.

## Pattern: Voicemail Imprint
**When to use**: Almost every unreached Smart Call.
**How**: Same opening; you will call back; number twice slowly; email. Repeat the *same* PVP. Listen to the whole greeting. Last-resort “I’ll assume you don’t want to discuss it” before you punt.
**Trade-offs**: Do not expect return calls. Mystery-name messages and fake cutoffs poison trust if they do call.

## Pattern: Trigger-Timed Pursuit
**When to use**: Prioritizing who to call now.
**How**: Hunt bad experience, change/transition, awareness (Elias). Alerts on competitors and dream accounts; CRM current-vendor field; call with value when the incumbent changes — never gloat.
**Trade-offs**: Thin deals do not get 16 research tabs. Huge deals do not get zero.

## Pattern: High and Big
**When to use**: Pipeline filled with stalls and tiny logos.
**How**: Clone top-customer traits; start a level above typical buyers; be ready for ROI talk (VITO). Ritualize hours so big thinking has a calendar.
**Trade-offs**: Unprepared altitude dies faster than a low start. Combine with intel and PVP.
