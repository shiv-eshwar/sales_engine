# Chapter 7: The Three Ps That Are Holding You Back

## Core Idea
Three mindsets block prospecting: **Procrastination**, **Perfectionism**, and **Paralysis from Analysis**. Fanatical prospecting is a little every day. Messy success beats perfect mediocrity. When stuck, make one call, then the next.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Ps (3Ps)**:
  1. **Procrastination** — putting prospecting off with “tomorrow / Friday / Monday”; trying to eat the month in one bite.
  2. **Perfectionism** — research, desk ritual, and “perfect time” as a shield against rejection.
  3. **Paralysis from Analysis** — what-if binges that look like call reluctance but are overthinking.
  - When to use: Anytime blocks are skipped, call rates are tiny, or prep consumes Golden Hours.
  - How: Daily small bites; research in Platinum Hours; disrupt by starting the first call (sometimes a hard push). Let what-ifs take care of themselves on the line.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Failure is the cumulative impact of skipped little things. Perfectionism correlates with fear of failure and self-defeating delay. The mind abhors the unknown and invents dire outcomes — until you jump.

- **One bite at a time**: You cannot do a month of prospecting in one day; daily discipline is the only scale that works.
- **Messy success > perfect mediocrity** (Zig Ziglar: anything worth doing is worth doing poorly): A targeted list plus dials beats a three-hour research ritual.
- **Disrupting the 3Ps**: Focus on just one call. Coach sits and dials beside you. Or a direct “just do it” push — like being ordered off the diving board.

## Key Concepts
- **Procrastination disease**: Universal; promises to catch up never cash. No reward; cost shows up as Law of Need plus a pile of shoulda-dones.
- **Research-in-advance vs. research-as-avoidance**: For C-level or complex/expensive deals, relevant research is good — **before and after Golden Hours**, not inside the prospecting block.
- **Call reluctance (true)**: If you hate the work so much you cannot dial, get out of bed, or go to work — this book will not help; quit and do something else. That is job mismatch, not a technique gap.
- **Call reluctance (mislabel)**: Most “reluctance” is 3Ps, especially analysis paralysis, and is fixable.
- **Self-talk of perfection**: “When every duck is in a row, they’ll be putty” — produces busy-not-doing.
- **Coach Poss lesson**: Skills in the shallow end do not count until you jump into deep water.

## Mental Models
- Use **daily bites** when the elephant (monthly quota of touches) feels impossible — never batch a month into a hero day.
- Use **Platinum Hour research** when selling high and complex; use **just dial** when perfection is clearly a rejection shield.
- Use **one-call-then-next** when what-ifs start: the unknown is resolved only in the conversation.
- Think of **3Ps cost** as both emotional and financial — they extract a high price even when they feel like “being thorough.”

## Anti-patterns
- **Catch-up fantasy**: “I’ll make up today’s prospecting on Friday.”
- **Jeremy pattern**: Perfect desk, Google + LinkedIn + website + full CRM history, then seven calls in three hours, then escape to existing customers. Voice mail used as proof that “no one answers.”
- **What-if binge**: “What if they say no / this / that?” instead of dialing.
- **More training / more facts / wrong timing** as delay tactics.
- **Labeling all inaction as call reluctance** without sorting hate-the-job vs. 3Ps.
- **Golden Hour research** for prospects who do not require C-level customization.

## Worked Example
**Jeremy vs. Valarie (same morning).** Jeremy spends hours perfecting the station and researching one name at a time. First calls dump to voice mail. He rearranges the desk, then leaves to visit current customers. Result: ~7 prospecting calls in ~3 hours, nothing gained.

Valarie dumps a CRM list and dials immediately. In one hour: **53 calls, 14 decision makers, 2 appointments** with qualified prospects, then **39 prospecting emails**. Imperfect — a few calls would have been better with research — but she out-earns Jeremy by almost **$100,000** in commissions and ranks #1 in the division.

**Disrupted paralysis.** A rep is stuck on what-ifs. Coach: make one call. Then the next. Sometimes the coach dials the same list in parallel so the rep sees they are not “blown out of the water.” Parallel to the diving-board jump: panic on entry, then remembered strokes (messy dog paddle still reaches shore). After the first real interrupt, the block becomes possible.

**Rule of thumb from the chapter:** On any day, a targeted list plus calling beats the rep who spends the whole block on meticulous research. Misses happen; they do not close the activity gap.

## Key Takeaways
1. 3Ps — Procrastination, Perfectionism, Paralysis from Analysis — are the mindsets that empty pipes.
2. Prospect a little every day; you cannot eat the elephant in one sitting.
3. Messy success beats perfect mediocrity; research belongs in Platinum Hours, not as a Golden Hour shield.
4. True job-hate “reluctance” is a career decision; typical paralysis is fixable by starting.
5. Disrupt with one call at a time — sometimes you need a push off the board.
6. Failure is accumulated skipped disciplines, often topped with a desperate scramble.
