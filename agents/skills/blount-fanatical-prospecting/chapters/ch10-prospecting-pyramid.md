# Chapter 10: Leveraging the Prospecting Pyramid

## Core Idea
Random CRM dialing treats the database as a square — every name equal — so you mostly hit people not in the buying window. Top performers treat it as a **Prospecting Pyramid**: sort by qualification, potential, and probability; start each morning at the tip. List quality impacts block success more than any factor except mindset.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Prospecting Pyramid** (walk like an Egyptian):
  - **Bottom**: Thousands with little more than a name/contact; data often wrong. *Action:* gather/correct data, start qualifying, move them up.
  - **Higher**: Solid contacts, email, competitors, usage, budget, some DMs/influencers. *Action:* identify buying window and all stakeholders.
  - **Higher**: Windows identified; complete DM/influencer records including social. *Action:* nurture in front of confirmed DMs toward the future window.
  - **Conquest layer**: Limited list of best/largest accounts (10, 25, 50, 100). *Action:* nurture, regular touches, stakeholder map, window qualify, trigger-event watch, familiarity.
  - **Near top**: Hot inbound and referrals. *Action:* immediate follow-up to qualify and/or enter pipe.
  - **Tip**: Highly qualified and entering the window (need, contract expiry, trigger, budget period). *Action:* first names on today’s list; move into pipe.
  - When to use: Designing every prospecting block and CRM views.
  - How: Daily qualifying work that moves names up. Start fresh, motivated mornings on tip-top (easier conversions → early wins → confidence). Then conquest qualify/nurture. Then systematically qualify the mass below.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Few names are in-window at once; random first-record dialing is statistically inefficient, high-rejection, desperation appointments with LPPs.

- **Powerful lists**: A list is the track for the prospecting train. Filter/combine:
  - Objective (four from Ch 9)
  - Channel (phone, email, social, text, in person, networking)
  - Qualification (highest at top)
  - Potential (largest at top)
  - Probability of hitting this block’s objective
  - Territory plan (day, zip, street, grid, city)
  - Inbound, conquest, DM role, vertical, product type, seasonal, inactive, trade-show leads
  - Automate CRM filters/views/reports; some sorts stay manual.

## Key Concepts
- **Square vs. pyramid**: Sameness vs. segmentation.
- **Trigger event / window of dissatisfaction**: Short; get in front before it dissipates or they buy elsewhere.
- **Company-provided lists**: Often missing or poor; you still own list construction.
- **“Is this the best prospect to call?”**: Ask of the first name every morning.

## Mental Models
- Use **highest-probability first** when energy is best — tip of pyramid, not zip-code roulette.
- Use **list as strategy**: one objective + one channel + ranked names.
- Think of qualifying as **pyramid climbing**, not a side task — that is how tomorrow’s tip gets populated.
- Prefer **organized army** over **mob** (Coolidge): same CRM, different order, different results.

## Anti-patterns
- **Login and call the first record**: Rudimentary geo filter, no objective, no qualify method.
- **Desperation demos**: Any win with poorly qualified names just to feel activity.
- **Skipping list-building** because opening the CRM feels faster — then hunting and pecking all hour.
- **Starting at the bottom** when in-window names exist (they buy or cool off while you “get through the list”).
- **Treating inbound/referrals as later**: they belong near the tip with immediate follow-up.

## Worked Example
A team has a packed, prevetted CRM plus intel tools. Director wants phones at 8 AM. Observation: nearest rep has no rule for first call — “I just log in and start calling.” Others pick a city/zip and call top of that dump. Result: miserable blocks, weak pipe, junk appointments.

Whiteboard pyramid. Prompt: who would you *want* on the list? They surface the real ranking: budget, expiring competitor contracts, referrals, larger headcount, inbound/web forms, trade-show booth, fully qualified but not yet appointed.

New morning design: (1) tip-top in-window and hot inbound/referrals, (2) conquest nurture/qualify, (3) information-gathering on the lower mass. Same database, ordered by probability and potential. Early appointments from the tip fund confidence for the harder qualifying work below.

## Key Takeaways
1. Do not treat the database as a square; pyramid by qualify × potential × probability.
2. Daily mission is moving names up by gathering windows and stakeholders.
3. Start the day at the tip while you are fresh; in-window names are perishable.
4. List quality (except mindset) is the biggest driver of block success.
5. Build lists from stacked filters; automate what you can in CRM.
6. Ask every morning: is this first name the best call?
