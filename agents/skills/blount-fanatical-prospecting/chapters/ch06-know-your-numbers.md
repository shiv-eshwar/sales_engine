# Chapter 6: Know Your Numbers: Managing Your Ratios

## Core Idea
Sales is a numbers game: **what** (quality) you put in the pipe and **how much** (quantity) determines what you get out. Elite sellers track everything like elite athletes track stats, then adjust. You cannot be delusional and successful at the same time.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Quality × Quantity → Output**: Formula for pipeline results. Quantity is science; quality (size, qualification, penetration, relationships with decision makers, influencers, coaches) is science plus art.
  - When to use: Forecasting, coaching, personal scorecards, diagnosing a miss.
  - How: Track calls, contacts, emails, responses, appointments, sales, social touches, texts, and new data added to the database. Review and adjust.

- **Efficiency + Effectiveness = Performance (E + E = P)**:
  - **Efficiency**: How much activity you generate in the time block allotted.
  - **Effectiveness**: Ratio of activity to outcome (and quality of those outcomes).
  - When to use: After every prospecting block; when “I made a ton of calls” does not match results.
  - How: Optimize the balance. 100 dials/hour with zero info, zero appointments, or only LPPs = efficient but ineffective waste. 10 calls/hour with one HQ appointment and two record updates = more effective but inefficient (wasted block time). Aim for both.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Without tracking, emotions invent a story. With tracking, you can change list, time of day, message, channel, or objective.

- **Manual tracking vs. system-only tracking**: Superstars keep a visible personal count, not only CRM/phone logs.
  - When to use: Daily. Especially after harsh rejection that can warp perception of volume.

## Key Concepts
- **LPPs (Low Potential Prospects)**: Appointments that look like activity but waste the pipe.
- **Variables that move E and E**: List quality, vertical, time of day/year, day of week, contact’s decision role, product, complex vs transactional, call objective, channel, approach/methodology/message, confidence, mindset.
- **Directional corrections**: Stats exist so you can change course, not to decorate a dashboard.
- **Visual tracking sheet**: Paper or equivalent in view — “felt like 50, was 12.”
- **Peak performance**: You will not reach it until you know numbers and use them.

## Mental Models
- Use **athlete stats** as the standard: if you cannot recite today’s calls, contacts, appointments, and conversions, you are flying blind.
- Use **E + E = P** when volume looks high but pipe is junk, or when quality looks high but you only made a handful of touches.
- Prefer **cold reality of a tally** over **warm delusion** after a bad call — rejection early in the day commonly shrinks actual dials while the story says “I was crushing it.”
- Think of counting as **honesty technology**: it keeps daily goals and standards from sliding.

## Anti-patterns
- **“Sales is not a numbers game”**: Usually a cover for not tracking.
- **CRM-only fog**: “Salesforce is tracking it” while the rep has no idea where they stand in the moment.
- **Emotional volume**: “A lot of calls” that audit to 12 in seven hours.
- **Efficiency theater**: Speed-dialing a bad list of LPPs.
- **Effectiveness theater**: Over-researching a tiny number of “perfect” calls (pairs with Ch 7 perfectionism).
- **Lowering standards via delusion**: Lying to yourself about activity so you can feel productive.

## Worked Example
Midafternoon coaching: rep says nobody is buying, “easily 50 calls,” brick wall. No tracking sheet; claims CRM has it. Walk the log against the phone system. Tally: **12 calls in seven hours**. Early-day harsh rejection tripped confidence and mindset; without a visible count, he invented productivity.

Fix: restore a daily tracking sheet (dials, contacts, outcomes) in view. After any emotional hit, the sheet — not the feeling — governs whether the block continues. Then inspect ratios: if 50 real dials still yield nothing, change list, time, message, or targeting (effectiveness), not just “try harder.” If 12 quality conversations happened by accident in a sea of wasted minutes, the issue is efficiency — Power Hour design (Ch 8), not the market.

Contrast: 100 dials/hour, no new info and no HQ appointments = wasted efficiency. 10 dials, one HQ appointment, two database updates = better effectiveness, still a failed block because the hour was not filled. Target both.

## Key Takeaways
1. What you put in (quality) and how much (quantity) determine what comes out.
2. Track every channel’s activity and outcomes; elites know their numbers in the moment.
3. Efficiency is rate inside the block; effectiveness is outcome ratio and prospect quality; performance needs both.
4. Manual, visual tracking prevents post-rejection delusion.
5. Small, stats-driven adjustments (list, timing, message, channel) can double results.
6. You cannot be delusional and successful at the same time.
