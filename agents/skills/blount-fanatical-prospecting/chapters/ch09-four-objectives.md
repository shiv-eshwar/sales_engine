# Chapter 9: The Four Objectives of Prospecting

## Core Idea
Every prospecting touch needs a defined **primary objective**. Without one you wander and sell nothing. Prospecting is a contact sport: know what you want, ask for it, skip small talk. The four core objectives are **set an appointment**, **gather information and qualify**, **close a sale**, and **build familiarity**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four Objectives of Prospecting**: Primary outcome of the touch.
  1. Set an appointment
  2. Gather information and qualify
  3. Close a sale
  4. Build familiarity
  - When to use: Before every block and every touch. Group touches by objective so blocks run faster (quantity) and you know exactly what to ask (quality).

- **Objective selection rules of thumb**:
  - Complex, high-risk, high-cost: primary = appointment with qualified DM/influencer/stakeholder; secondary = information; tertiary = familiarity.
  - Transactional, low-risk, low-cost, **inside**: primary = close; secondary = information.
  - Same product, **outside**, remote channels (phone/email/text/social): primary = appointment; secondary = information. **In-person knock**: primary = close.
  - Highly qualified CRM: primary = appointment as buying window opens; secondary = familiarity.
  - Purchase only in defined windows (contract expiry, budget cycle): primary = qualify the window + familiarity; do not book meetings with people who cannot buy. Once the window is known, switch primary to appointment.
  - New territory / start-up / new division: primary = information (DMs, windows, budgets); secondary = familiarity.

- **Appointment = dual-calendar commitment**: On your calendar **and** theirs; they expect you at a specific time, date, and place (physical or virtual). “Call me maybe,” “stop by anytime,” “mornings are usually fine” are brush-offs, not appointments. Send the meeting request.

- **Don’t swing at nothin’ ugly (strike zone)**: Qualify so you do not spend Golden Hours on unprofitable, unwindowed, no-budget, no-DM, contract-locked deals.
  - When to use: Complex, contractual, exclusive, long-cycle, high-level, defined budget period.
  - When **not** to over-qualify before meeting: noncontractual products most people buy continuously, no set budget period, single consistent DM — then set the appointment and qualify in the meeting (avoids qualifying-as-call-avoidance).

- **Familiarity touch counts** (averages; shift by brand, geo, channel, cycle, vertical):
  - 1–3 to reengage inactive customer
  - 1–5 if in buying window and familiar with you/brand
  - 3–10 if highly familiar but not in window
  - 5–12 warm inbound
  - 5–20 some familiarity (window-dependent)
  - 20–50 cold, unknown you and brand

- **Strategic Prospecting Campaign (SPC)**: Multi-channel, long-horizon plan whose primary objective can be familiarity (voice mail, email, LinkedIn, likes/comments, conference face) so later interrupts convert.

## Key Concepts
- **Buying window**: Period when they can actually purchase; qualify it before burning appointment slots on complex deals.
- **Bell curve of the database**: Few fully qualified + in window; more qualified but locked; more semi-qualified; more unknown/outdated; few never-buyers/bogus. Spend time at the top; nurture locked-qualified; gather on holes; delete bogus.
- **One-call close path**: On phone/in person for transactional: beat RBO, 1–2 qualify questions, convert to an on-the-spot sales conversation, then close (full close technique is out of scope; RBOs in Ch 15–16, 18).
- **Contact sport vs. relationship theater**: Prospecting is not for chatting, selling the whole vision, or marketing novels. Get to the point.

## Mental Models
- Use **objective-first list design** (Ch 10) so a Power Hour has one ask.
- Use **strike zone profile** (ideal customer + how early to engage before the window) as the walk-away standard; calculated risk ≠ chasing ugly.
- Use **familiarity as lubricant** when the list is cold: SPC first, appointment later.
- Prefer **firm calendar** over **hopeful verbal**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Wing-and-a-prayer touches**: No ask, long scripts, marketing-written emails.
- **Fake appointments**: 80% no-show rates because “anytime mornings” was logged as 10 AM.
- **Ugly-deal swinging**: Pipe and forecast stuffed with unqualified work.
- **Always-set-then-qualify** on complex/contractual sales (wastes both calendars).
- **Always-qualify-first** on simple transactional (used as call avoidance).
- **Treating prospecting as relationship time**.

## Worked Example
**Armando’s 80% no-shows.** He “had” a 10 AM with Jessica, a never-bought buyer. She didn’t answer; assistant said she was traveling. No calendar invite. What she actually said last week: super busy, happy to meet another time, usually available mornings, “call anytime.” He suggested 10 AM; she said fine. That was a brush-off. Audit of his next seven days: almost all “call me maybe.” Rule applied: it is only an appointment when it is on both calendars and they expect you.

**Database actions vs. objectives.** Top of bell: book the appointment. Qualified but contract-locked: nurture + familiarity. Holes in data: information-gathering block. Bogus/too small/never: eliminate. Cold 100 ops managers who don’t know you: SPC of calls/VMs, email, social, trade shows — primary objective familiarity until they will take a meeting.

**Transactional inside close:** Skip the appointment theater. Interrupt, handle reflex, two qualify questions, ask for the sales conversation now.

## Key Takeaways
1. Name the primary objective before the touch; group blocks by objective.
2. An appointment is a mutual, specific-time commitment — not a maybe.
3. Match objective to complexity, channel, tenure, and buying-window constraints.
4. Define the strike zone; don’t swing at ugly; systematically qualify the database.
5. Familiarity is usually secondary/tertiary; make it primary only via an SPC on high-value cold lists.
6. Touch-count ranges explain why cold unknown names need far more persistence than inactive customers.
