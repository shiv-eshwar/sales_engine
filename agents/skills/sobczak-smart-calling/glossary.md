# Glossary — Smart Calling (Art Sobczak)

**ACTION notes** — Post-call log: Authority map, Commitment, Timing, Interest/need, Overview, Notes plus next-call ideas. (Ch 16)

**All-about-them mind-set** — Prep and talk about the prospect’s world, not your quota or product love. (Ch 2)

**Asking attitude** — Bias to ask more and larger; techniques are secondary. (Ch 15)

**Assumptive problem question** — Assumes the pain exists and asks them to describe the scene, not “any problems?” (Ch 12)

**Because / justification statement** — The reason you give when social-engineering or briefing a screener; unlocks answers (Cialdini/Langer). (Ch 4, Ch 8)

**Cold call** — Same pitch to strangers with little or no information. Ban it; keep telephone prospecting. (Ch 1)

**Commitment (not close)** — The do they agree to; closing implies an end. (Ch 15)

**Consistency principle** — People stick to what they already said; recommend and ask in their words. (Ch 14)

**Differential advantage** — The felt difference versus alternatives, used inside a PVP. (Ch 2)

**Fuzzy phrase** — Vague stall (“let’s stay in touch,” “still evaluating”); clarify clock, criteria, or personal yes. (Ch 12, Ch 16)

**HAYT** — “How are you today?” Optional icebreaker; listen if you use it. (Ch 9)

**Iceberg theory of questioning** — First answer is the tip; buy-reasons sit below; keep asking. (Ch 12)

**Intelligence gathering** — Facts plus situational/trigger information used on the call, not hoarded. (Ch 3)

**Interest-creating opening** — You/company + intel + PVP hint + request to question. Also the voicemail body. (Ch 10, Ch 7)

**Jim Furyk Theory** — Delivery may be unconventional if impact conditions are met: relevance, possible value, questions, low resistance. (Ch 10)

**Loaded benefit question** — Third-party problem you have solved, then “what’s your experience?” (Ch 12)

**Pattern interrupt** — Softener + new question that breaks an automatic blow-off. (Ch 11)

**Possible Value Proposition (PVP)** — Outcome language that *might* matter; confirmed with questions, never assumed. (Ch 2)

**Primary objective** — What you want them to *do* on this call. (Ch 5)

**Quality game** — Successes matter, not dial count; prior no does not create the next yes. (Ch 1)

**Recommendation vs. pitch** — After questions, results they asked for; pitch is one-way spray. (Ch 14)

**Resistant Reflex Response (RRR)** — Instant “not interested / we’re happy” with little thought. (Ch 11)

**Ritualized phone time** — Calendar-blocked calling with no admin inside the block. (Ch 6)

**Screener-as-buyer** — Treat assistants as buyers; they can influence, buy, or kill. (Ch 1, Ch 8)

**Secondary objective** — Minimum attempt that makes every call a win; kills the rejection story. (Ch 5, Ch 18)

**Smart Calling** — (1) Acquire intel before the decision maker, (2) use it in a consultative process, (3) help them take a valued action. (Ch 1)

**Social engineering (positive)** — Identify, ask for help, justify, question people other than the buyer. (Ch 4)

**Social proof** — Others’ results and words persuade more than your claims. (Ch 14)

**Telephone appointment** — Follow-up booked like a meeting: specific time, who calls, homework. (Ch 16)

**Trial close** — Micro-agreement during a recommendation (“sound good so far?”). (Ch 14, Ch 20)

**Trigger event / window of dissatisfaction** — Bad experience, change/transition, or new awareness that makes a non-buyer likely now. (Ch 3)

**Verbal nods / phone eye contact** — Uh-huh, I see, tell me more — replacements for visible listening. (Ch 13)

**VITO** — Very Important Top Officer; call high for speed, value, and price. (Ch 18)

**Weasel words** — Might, maybe, perhaps, depending on — keep openings consultative. (Ch 10)

**Will you vs. May I** — Obligation vs. permission; always aim at Will you. (Ch 15)
