# Chapter 17: How to Sound Smart: Effective Telephone Communication

## Core Idea
Prospects form a picture of you in seconds from sound. You would not visit in tattered clothes; do not call sounding like you crawled out of bed. Easier to fix than appearance: knowledge, practice, recordings — and stop sounding like a “salesperson.”

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Friend-and-passion voice**: Use as the default calling sound.
  - How: A recorded rep sounded like a telemarketer on sales calls and like Kathleen Turner on a personal call about the gym. Pretend you are talking to a good friend about something you care about. Picture that person. You sell more by not sounding like a salesperson.
  - Failure mode: Assembly-line game face. Humans become ticks on a contact counter.

- **Record and review (highest-ROI skill drill)**: Use weekly, and before important follow-ups.
  - How: Hear speed, monotone, volume, nasal/whine, filler (uh/ah/um amplified on phone), pet phrases (“you know what I mean?”). Catch missed intel and buying signals the way a second movie viewing reveals details. Swap recordings with a peer at lunch. Replay a prior call as pre-call planning so their exact words are fresh.
  - Legal: Some states require two-party consent; others one-party. Look up current law; do not treat this as legal advice. Company systems vs. personal USB recorders.
  - Failure mode: Never listening to yourself, so the AFLAC-duck problem persists.

- **Stand for sales**: Use on important calls and post-lunch fog.
  - How: UC research cited: standing processes information 5–20% faster; heart rate up ~10 bpm stimulates brain (Buffington). Headset required for productivity; invest in one that is comfortable (Sobczak: OfficeRunner-class).

## Key Concepts
- **Sound is appearance**: Grooming analogy. First impression is vocal.
- **Filler is louder on phone** because there is no body language to mask it.
- **You don’t know what you don’t know**: Peers hear sins you cannot.
- **Voice craft**: Berkley, *Speak to Influence*, as a self-study pointer.
- **Match their greeting energy** (Ch 7) without becoming a fake announcer.

## Mental Models
- Use **the personal-call version of you** on business calls.
- Think of recordings as **game film** — every serious performer reviews.
- Prefer **standing + headset** as physiology, not motivation poster.
- Treat filler and loop-phrases as **visual stains** the listener cannot unsee.

## Anti-patterns
- **Script-reader affect** that disappears in real conversation.
- **Refusing a headset** because a cheap one hurt.
- **Checking recordings only for “content,”** ignoring how you sound.
- **Assuming one-party recording is always legal.**
- **Sitting slumped through the money conversation.**

## Worked Example
Same person, two voices: friend-call emotion vs. sales-call flatness. Intervention: before the next prospect, picture the friend, stand, record. Review: count ums, mark one missed “our challenge is…” you talked over, rewrite the next question. Peer lunch: they flag a rising terminal inflection that makes statements sound like begging.

Pre-call: play last week’s 22-minute discovery before today’s recommendation so paraphrase (Ch 14) is verbatim.

## Key Takeaways
1. Your phone “look” is your voice; make it the passionate-friend version.
2. Record legally, review regularly, exchange tapes with a peer.
3. Kill filler and repeated crutches; they are magnified without video.
4. Use recordings to catch content you missed live and to prep the next call.
5. Stand on important calls; wear a serious headset.
6. Match energy without putting on a telemarketer mask.
7. Voice skill is trainable; treat it as part of Smart Calling, not vanity.
