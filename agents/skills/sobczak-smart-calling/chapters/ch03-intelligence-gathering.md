# Chapter 3: Intelligence Gathering: Making Your Calls Smart

## Core Idea
What separates a dumb cold call from a Smart Call is what you know before you speak — and power comes from *using* that knowledge, not collecting it. Gather facts plus situational “trigger” information that places the buyer in a window of dissatisfaction.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Factual vs. situational intelligence**: Use on every pre-call.
  - How: Facts = what they sell, locations, headcount, financials. Situational = needs, pain, problems, desires, initiatives. The jackpot is situational: new branches, lawsuits, promotions, interviews revealing this year’s initiatives, regulation changes.
  - Failure mode: Calling Business By Phone Inc. as if it were a phone-system reseller. Wrong company model = dumb call.

- **Trigger events / window of dissatisfaction** (Craig Elias): Use to prioritize who is likely to buy *now*.
  - How: Look for (1) Bad Experience with people, product, or provider; (2) Change/Transition in people, places, priorities; (3) Awareness of legal, risk, or economic reasons to change. Analyze *won* sales to see which triggers actually create your deals.

- **Person + company intel**: Use to connect without stalking.
  - How: Collect what is available (Mackay 66 as a model: background, interests, lifestyle, needs, relationship). Mention a relevant article or shared context later; never “I liked your smile in the photo.”

- **Source stack**: Use in this order of effort vs. payoff.
  - How: CRM/notes first (past purchases — avoid calling existing customers as new). Then their website (About, press, mission language, job postings, site: search). Then LinkedIn, news alerts, blogs, Twitter/Facebook/YouTube as relevant. Paid intel (InsideView, OneSource iSell, Hoovers, Factiva, LexisNexis) when rainmaking is the job.

## Key Concepts
- **Knowledge is not power**: Coolidge’s educated derelicts. Power is application in opening, questions, and recommendations.
- **Compelling events**: Contracts awarded, expansion/downsizing, legal action, restructuring, cost-cut or sales pushes, new people/products, deadlines.
- **Google News Alerts**: Free assistant. Minimum: competitors, top customers, coveted prospect companies and named buyers, plus topical queries.
- **Competitor-change hunt**: Alert on competitor names; store Current Vendor in CRM; call with value when the vendor is acquired or renamed — never “I bet it’s a mess, switch to us.”
- **LinkedIn for intel**: Groups, Answers/discussions, company pages, Advanced People Search with saved alerts, who viewed you, InMail as last-resort.
- **Filetype and street-view searches**: Public PDFs/PPT/XLS; ethics: do not use files they clearly did not mean to publish.
- **RFP search**: “Requests for Proposal” + keywords for government buyers.
- **Social not-working**: Time toilets. You are not your customer — be where *they* are, not where you prefer. High-level buyers may not live on social; still check.
- **Match depth to deal**: Community-magazine ad ≠ seven-figure consulting. Same process, different time budget.

## Mental Models
- Use **“what you know about who you want to know”** as the real networking rule.
- Think of intel as **ammunition for the first 10 seconds**, not a biography to dump.
- Prefer **trigger-timed calls** over generic cadences: change creates permission to reopen a closed door.
- Treat **social media as a communication channel**, one spoke, never a wonder-solution that replaces the phone.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling without knowing what they sell**.
- **Ignoring CRM notes** and prospecting existing customers.
- **Creepy personal comments** from photos.
- **Sixteen browser tabs for a transactional sale** — or zero research for a career-making account.
- **Leading with competitor misfortune**.
- **InMail as the plan** (Schaffer’s “Hail Mary”).
- **Assuming Fortune 100 CEOs tweet** — or assuming nobody important is online. Check the actual buyer.

## Worked Example
Won-sales trigger: A telecom rep notices big deals clustered after a new buyer, a service failure, or a risk realization. He runs a Won Sales Analysis, then sets alerts and CRM vendor fields so the next similar event produces a call — not a hope.

Competitor-acquisition call (paraphrase): “We spoke six months ago about cutting component costs; you were with AB and timing wasn’t right. Since the acquisition, some of my customers have seen slower order delivery. If that’s an issue, we have options worth a look…” Value first, change as context, questions next.

Website-to-question: A construction-info rep reads the prospect’s “brag site,” infers project type and web savvy, notes other office principals, then asks “Since this impacts Project Management, does Doug need to be involved?” instead of “Does anyone else need to be involved?”

## Key Takeaways
1. Gather facts and situational triggers; use them — do not hoard them.
2. Identify your trigger events; hunt the window of dissatisfaction.
3. Start with CRM, then the prospect’s site, then LinkedIn/news/social/paid tools.
4. Set Google Alerts on competitors, customers, and dream accounts.
5. Store current vendor so you can sort and call when that vendor changes.
6. Use personal intel surgically; never stalk.
7. Invest research time in proportion to deal size — but never dial blind.
