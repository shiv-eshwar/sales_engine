# Chapter 5: Setting Smart Call Objectives and Never Being Rejected Again

## Core Idea
Begin every call with the end in mind: a primary objective is what you want the *other person to do*. Pair it with a secondary objective you can attempt even if they will not do that. Rejection is your reaction; design a win on every call.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Primary objective (the do)**: Use before every dial.
  - How: Ask, “What do I want this person to *do* as a result of this call?” Action-oriented: buy, agree to take a proposal to the board and recommend it, run a side-by-side, attend a web demo, meet with influencers. Qualifying, “see if they have needs,” and “send info” are activities — not primary objectives.
  - Why it works: Unfocused calls meander. Covey’s begin-with-the-end-in-mind plus athlete-level expectation raises the average result even when you miss the top of the range.
  - Failure mode: Aiming to “see if there’s interest,” then bailing to a brochure when they stay on the line.

- **Think big / highest conceivable end result**: Use when setting the primary.
  - How: Expect the furthest the call could honestly go (including a sale on a prospecting call if it has ever happened in your world). You will not hit it most times; your median will still be higher, and you shorten cycles by forcing decisions.

- **Secondary objective (never-be-rejected-again)**: Use on every call, especially after a no.
  - How: Minimum attempt *you* will make if the primary fails. It need not be a do: leave a good feeling, learn current vendor, ask permission to recontact, plant a future-condition seed. A win — however small — means you did not consent to inferiority (Roosevelt).
  - Why it works: Two reps hear the same no. One visits the vending machine as a victim. The other logs a secondary win and stays in the game.
  - Failure mode: No fallback, so every no feels like an identity wound.

- **Planted-seed secondary**: Use when there is no fit today but there could be later.
  - How: Name the future unfavorable circumstance you solve, then tell them where to file your card (under Widgets, in the project file, in the mailing-planning folder). Ask the brilliant follow-up: “What would have to happen that would be so severe you would even consider someone else?”

## Key Concepts
- **Joyride vs. destination**: You do not start the car “to see what happens.” Neither should a call.
- **Rejection ≠ the words you hear**: “Happy with what we have,” “not interested,” “no” are events. Rejection is how you interpret them. No one rejects you without your consent.
- **“Oh?” as a tool**: After a no, a curious “Oh?” can open the real story (agency example).
- **Future-condition reminders**: Volume thresholds, customer requests you currently refer out, rising bad-address rates — specific tripwires.

## Mental Models
- Use a **primary do** the way a driver uses a destination and a route.
- Think of **secondary objectives as mental health infrastructure**, not a consolation prize.
- Prefer **one planted sentence** that harvests a year later over “keep us in mind.”
- Judge the day as: primary hit N times; otherwise secondary. That is a good day.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fake primaries**: “See who they buy from,” “qualify and send info,” “see if they have needs.”
- **Low aim that caps the call**: Jumping off as soon as interest appears because you did not expect to go further.
- **Taking “no” as “I am inferior.”**
- **Alternate-choice appointment close with no value** (“Thursday or Friday?”) as the whole strategy — then failing to salvage a secondary.
- **Leaving without a fileable reminder** of when they would need you.

## Worked Example
Travel-agency rep uses a weak alternate-choice close. Buyer (Sobczak) says he is not a prospect. Rep asks “Oh?” — learns history with the agency, size mismatch then, current satisfaction. Secondary: if anything happens, first in line. Then: “What would have to happen that would be so severe you would consider someone else?” Buyer names indifference and unfixed money-costing errors. Rep plants two proofs: you would be a big account with a personal plus cross-trained agents; president’s philosophy is business with us will never cost clients money.

A year later a trainee tickets error costs $600; old agency will not make it right. Buyer finds the card. Seed harvested.

Call-end seed (paraphrase): “It doesn’t look like a fit today. When you notice more direct mail returned for bad addresses, we have software that can cut that and save printing and postage. I’ll send my card — please keep it in the file you work from when you plan mailings.”

## Key Takeaways
1. Primary objective = the action you want *them* to take; write it before you dial.
2. Aim at the highest conceivable honest result.
3. Secondary objective = the attempt that guarantees a win and kills the rejection story.
4. Rejection is a reaction you can refuse.
5. Plant specific future conditions and a place to store your name.
6. “Oh?” and “what would have to happen?” turn a no into intelligence.
7. Score the day on primaries plus secondaries, not on an unbroken chain of yes.
