# Chapter 18: Getting and Staying Motivated

## Core Idea
Process without the head game is worthless. The mental side — desire, self-motivation, confidence, attitude — is a choice. Beliefs are filters (Zimmerman): if you do not like the show, change the channel. Mechanics from this book fail in the hands of someone who cannot handle no’s; clumsy talkers with huge desire still win.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Never-rejected-again (motivation edition)**: Use when no’s pile up (pairs with Ch 5).
  - How: Rejected rep burns calls, slams the phone, takes no as identity, mopes, skips the next dial, relives every no. Successful rep logs a secondary win, accepts many no’s, judges attempts not just yeses, does not take it personally (no need *right now*), knows the last call does not determine the next, may be one keypad punch from the biggest sale.
  - Shop rule: cannot leave until the last call is positive (objective met). People go home upbeat and start tomorrow willing.

- **Absurdity dump for call fear** (Frankl / Boyan-Enright): Use when you scroll past the Big Fish for weeks.
  - How: Dwelling encodes the fear. Exaggerate it to cartoon impossibility (demon buyer traveling back through the headset to turn brains to jelly). The mind rejects absurdity; ridicule strips power. Allport: people who laugh at problems are on the way to solving them.

- **“So what?” mode**: Use as a one-line interrupt. “So what if I get a no. Big deal.” Worst-case is rarely what the body is acting out.

- **Size of thinking**: Use when choosing accounts and titles.
  - How: Success magnitude tracks thought size (Disraeli: life is too short to be little). Call IBM-class opportunities, not only tiny shops that cannot buy. Call high (Peoples *Selling to the Top*; Parinello VITO): faster, less work, more value, less competition, more price. Middle managers stall (“still in committee”). Caution: be ready to discuss big-picture impact.
  - Action: clone attributes of your top 10–20% customers; start several levels above the usual entry title.

- **Go for the big jump**: Use when the safe fourth-place call is tempting.
  - How: Bergoust risked last place vs. conservative fourth; no regrets on the risk. The risks you regret are the ones you do not take. When hot, keep going (Ch 6).

- **Be a kid**: Use as a behavior checklist.
  - How: Kids persist past no, take risks, imagine, aim at the majors, laugh ~400 times a day vs. adults <20, move fast, stay curious. Do not grow out of those sales traits. Written goals; aim higher; smile vs. scowl; dart, don’t shuffle.

## Key Concepts
- **Cynicals and skepticals**: They mock training and motivational work; they are usually miserable. Avoid their pity party — fatal for Smart Calling.
- **Only you motivate you**: Books, seminars, Pivot (Zimmerman) as fuel when you slump. Fill the mind on purpose.
- **Practice talking to strangers** in lines and airports to shrink fear.
- **VITO vs. turf-protectors**: Top officers care about revenue, cost, efficiency — not impressing the committee.

## Mental Models
- Use **filters/channels**: attitude is selectable, not weather.
- Think of **no as “not this need, this minute,”** not “I am rejected.”
- Prefer **laughably exaggerated fear** over rumination.
- Think **big accounts, high titles, big asks** — then ritualize (Ch 6) so the thought has a calendar.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reliving every no** while forgetting your best friend’s number.
- **Avoiding the intimidating account** until it becomes a superstition.
- **Small-think**: “They’d never buy from little me.”
- **Starting low and living in molasses** of non-deciders.
- **Hanging out with office cynics** during ritual call time.
- **Conservative fourth place** when gold required a risk.

## Worked Example
Fear: You skip Mr. Mackerel at Big Fish for a month. Absurdity: he is a supernatural demon who digitizes himself into headsets. Smile, then dial with a Smart opening. “So what if he says not interested?” Secondary objective ready (Ch 5).

Thinking size: Stop farming $200 accounts that consume service. List traits of your best customers; build a lookalike list; open at VP/finance, not the user who cannot sign.

Kid persistence: First no on Raffles (Ch 11) is not the end. Next ask, next level in the hierarchy, next day — without taking it as a verdict on the self.

## Key Takeaways
1. Head game is a choice; change the filter.
2. Secondary wins and “last call positive” prevent rejection stories.
3. Ridicule fear into absurdity; use “so what?”
4. Think and call bigger — accounts and titles — with prep to match.
5. When you are hot, keep jumping; take the risks you would regret skipping.
6. Borrow kids’ persistence, curiosity, motion, and laugh rate.
7. Avoid cynics; feed your mind on purpose when you slump.
