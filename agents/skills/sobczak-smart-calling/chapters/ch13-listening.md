# Chapter 13: The More Important Side of the Question: Listening

## Core Idea
The best salespeople are not the smoothest talkers. They listen more than they talk and know when to shut up. Speak less, sell more. If you allow them, people will explain why they think, feel, and act — and that is what helps them buy.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **The pause (most powerful listening tool)**: Use at two points on every call.
  1. After you ask a question (2–3 seconds).
  2. After you hear an answer (2–3 seconds).
  - Why it works: You won’t fill the hole yourself; they get time to think; answers get longer; unsolicited extra appears; you understand before you formulate the next line. Picture them on camera stroking their chin — they are doing that on the phone too.
  - Failure mode: Edgy silence-filling. The “orrrrr what did you want to do, orrrrr just let it run out?” that talks people into no.

- **Key-terminology capture**: Use whenever they mark a problem in their words.
  - How: Write exactly “Here’s our challenge…” “Our biggest problem is…” “Where we need work…” Then: “Tell me more about that.” They sell themselves. Replay those words in the recommendation (Ch 14) — they will not disagree with themselves.

- **Phone eye contact**: Use to replace unseen nods.
  - How: Mix verbal nods (uh-huh, I see, interesting) and encouragement (go on; tell me more; expand on that). Lack of this feels like the waiter who only looks at his shoes.

- **Listen-when-they-whisper**: Use when volume drops.
  - How: Lowered voice usually means sensitive, high-value truth. Lean in; note it.

## Key Concepts
- **Latex-gloves lesson**: A silent clerk elicited a full confession of why the buyer had gloves and a funnel. People feel compelled to explain. You do not have to drag it — stop interrupting.
- **Talk-time study** (Conklin): Bottom 10% of a sales force talked ~30 minutes per presentation; top 10% talked ~12.
- **Don’t talk yourself out of a sale**: State price or next step, then pause. Discounting into the void (“$799… we could do $150 off… and another $80”) trains them to wait.
- **No negative recommendations**: Don’t invite the no. Make a positive suggestion and live with the silence.
- **Listening:talking ratio**: Measure it. If you are always the soundtrack, you are not selling.

## Mental Models
- Use silence as **a question you already asked**.
- Think of extra talking as **nullifying the communication** (Greenleaf).
- Prefer **their phrase file** over your clever paraphrase on the first pass.
- Treat a whisper as **the real meeting inside the meeting**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Listening to yourself talk** (the manager’s complaint).
- **Jumping in the instant they stop** — you cut the iceberg below the waterline (Ch 12).
- **Nervous “orrrr”** after a good suggestion.
- **Stacking discounts into silence.**
- **Monotone uh-huhs** that sound like a hold message.
- **Missing buying signals** because you were loading the next speech.

## Worked Example
Magazine renewal: “Your subscription runs out this month, I was calling to see if you wanted to keep that going… orrrrr what you wanted to do… orrrrr if you just wanted to let it run out?” The invitation to no completes the decision. Better: state the renewal as the next step, pause, let inertia and their own reasons work.

Price: “It’s $799.” [pause] Prospect was ready to buy. Rep hears silence as objection and talks $230 off the table. Pause would have held margin.

Training inquiry: Four different buyers each marked pain with a signature phrase. Rep wrote the phrase, said “tell me more,” and let them pour. Later recommendation used their words.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pause 2–3 seconds after questions and after answers.
2. Speak less; top performers talk a fraction of what poor ones do.
3. Capture and replay their exact problem language.
4. Use verbal nods and “tell me more” as eye contact.
5. When they drop volume, that content is usually the deal.
6. After a positive ask or a price, shut up — do not invite no or discount into the void.
7. Review recordings (Ch 17) to hear what you missed live.
