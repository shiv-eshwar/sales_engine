# Chapter 16: Wrapping Up Calls and Setting Up the Next Action

## Core Idea
The next call is won or lost at the end of this one. If you hang up with “I’ll send some literature and call in a couple of weeks,” you created amnesia and a “just checking in” follow-up. Qualify who deserves a callback; assign homework; summarize; set a real phone appointment.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Three questions before you promise a callback**:
  1. Why is a follow-up necessary? (Interest, need, authority, money — and a *specific* remaining action: demo review, proposal facts, real committee date — not “still evaluating.”)
  2. What will *you* do before then? Tell them and do it.
  3. What will *they* do? If they will not agree to anything, they do not see enough value — do not chase shadows.

- **Call-ending summary** (no sale):
  - Need/problem and interest (why they care).
  - What they will do (read with questions prepared, test sample against agreed criteria, take proposal with their recommendation).
  - What you will do.
  - When you next talk — *they* give the date tied to their homework: “By what date will you have the inventory figures?”
  - Next-call agenda (plant expectations).

- **Specific telephone appointment**: Use for important follow-ups.
  - How: As serious as a face-to-face. Not “sometime Thursday afternoon.” “You will have tested the sample by Thursday; does Friday work? Calendar? Morning better? Put me down for 11:15 *your time*; I’ll call you; I’ll send an invite if they use them. Unless I hear otherwise, Friday 11:15. Have the sample and notes ready.”
  - Why it works: Signals your time is valuable; you are not a typical rep. If an assistant owns the calendar, book through them.

- **ACTION notes model**: Use immediately after the call (it saves prep time later).
  - **A**uthority — full decision map, not one title.
  - **C**ommitment — what they pledged.
  - **T**iming — when they buy; when you speak.
  - **I**nterest/Need — what and why.
  - **O**verview — did you summarize?
  - **N**otes — plus: questions for next time, possible next objectives, opening-statement ideas while fresh.

- **Last-resort before giving up**: Use when they insist they are not a prospect.
  - How: Not “I’ll send a kit anyway.” “Could you ever see situations changing where this would be a possibility?” If yes: “What would those situations be?” Then work those scenarios — or learn you should stop.

- **Graduate degree in sales**: After every call: (1) What did I like? (2) What would I have done differently? Not “what did I blow?” Positive replay.

## Key Concepts
- **Not everyone is a prospect**: Fast disqualify is a success skill. Do not waste cycles.
- **Two emails between calls**: Summary right after; reminder before the next contact — two extra touches so they did the homework.
- **Literature quota without qualification** burns postage and goodwill (ad-kit story).
- **Get them to send you something** (current invoice, sample, brochure) to deepen involvement.

## Mental Models
- Use **this call’s last two minutes** as **next call’s first two minutes**.
- Think of a vague callback as **an office visit you scheduled as ‘I’ll pop in sometime.’**
- Prefer **their date tied to their task** over your round number of weeks.
- If they will not do homework, **you do not have a prospect; you have a polite stall.**

## Anti-patterns
- **“Just checking if you got my brochure.”**
- **Calling back because you already invested**, not because the three questions have answers.
- **You pick “in a couple of weeks”** with no agenda.
- **Skipping post-call notes** because you are “too busy” — you will pay in weak next openings.
- **Sending kits to people who told you they are not buyers.**

## Worked Example
End: “To review: you believe we can beat availability and you like the service policy; you want the new inventory program but must wait for budget, which you will suggest. I’ll email the comparison Friday with the page to study. Monday you meet your boss with your recommendation. We talk May 5. What else would help that meeting?” Then ACTION notes and a summary email.

Appointment tighten: They name Friday. You narrow to 11:15, confirm who calls, send invite, restate homework.

Last-resort: After a clear not-a-fit, “Could you ever see that changing?” opens a real maybe or a clean kill — better than a ghost kit.

## Key Takeaways
1. Follow-up success is built at wrap-up, not at the next dial.
2. Answer why / you do / they do before you agree to call again.
3. Summarize need, both to-dos, date they choose, and next agenda.
4. Book phone appointments like office visits.
5. Log ACTION notes and next-opening ideas immediately.
6. Email the summary (and a reminder) between calls.
7. Review every call with like / do-differently; use last-resort questions instead of leftover literature.
