# Glossary — New Sales. Simplified.

**⅓-⅓-⅓** — Cast-in-stone time split across Targeted, Active, and Hot opportunities so the pipeline stays full, moving, and balanced. (Ch 14)

**Active (pipeline)** — Dialogue has started; potential exists; work it toward Hot. (Ch 14)

**A-player hunter** — The scarce 10–15% who can be counted on for new logos year after year. (Ch 3)

**Annuity book** — Existing portfolio that pays the same whether you hunt or not. (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Attack** — Planned, calendared, measured deployment of weapons against named targets. New Sales Driver part C. (Ch 4, Ch 14)

**Babysitting accounts** — Overserving current customers at the expense of prospecting. (Ch 2)

**Bluebird** — A deal that “flies in.” Luck, not a process. (Ch 2)

**Bold declaration** — Missed new business traces to targets, weapons, or attack (selection/focus, quality/proficiency, plan/execution). (Ch 4)

**Book of Lists** — Local-journal ranked company lists used to name targets after strategy is set. (Ch 5)

**Capabilities overview** — First-meeting monologue about you. Usually malpractice. (Ch 10, Ch 13)

**Clean up their issues** — Existing-customer call phase: clear junk before your agenda. (Ch 11)

**Client issues** — Pains removed, problems solved, opportunities captured, results achieved. First building block of the story. (Ch 8)

**Commodity antidote** — Using issues + differentiators so a price-sensitive product is not sold as a true commodity. (Ch 8)

**Default to the buyer’s process** — No plan, so they drive. (Ch 2, Ch 10)

**Differentiators** — Why you are the best choice for the issues you just named. Third story block. (Ch 8)

**Discovery precedes presentation** — Sales law. Exam before prescription. (Ch 10–13)

**Dream targets / dream clients** — 4–5 monster accounts with a mini-plan; never the whole process. (Ch 5)

**Fake phone effort** — Time-blocked calling hijacked by news, email, research, lunch. (Ch 2, Ch 9)

**Finite, focused, written, workable** — The four tests of a target list. (Ch 5)

**Fit** — Magic word: non-desperate test of whether you should work together. (Ch 9, Ch 11)

**Good corporate citizen** — Helpful everywhere except on the hunting quota. (Ch 2)

**Hot (pipeline)** — Real opportunity with urgency; proposal out or imminent. (Ch 14)

**Hybrid hunter-farmer** — One person hunting and farming; usually starves hunting. (Ch 3)

**I head up…** — Phone positioning line that frames you as a peer, not a telemarketer. (Ch 9)

**Intentional imbalance** — Extra time on Largest / Most Growable / Most At-Risk; starve Other. (Ch 5)

**Late to the party** — Arriving after the buyer’s process (or a competitor’s RFP) is set. (Ch 2, Ch 15)

**Let me steal a minute** — Preferred proactive-call opening; honest about interruption. (Ch 9)

**Lookalike prospect** — Looks/feels/smells like a best customer. (Ch 1, Ch 5)

**Milk run** — Calling the same route by habit, not potential. (Ch 5)

**Mini Power Statement** — Two issue bullets (+ optional one differentiator) for the phone. (Ch 9)

**New Sales Driver** — Select targets → Create and deploy weapons → Plan and execute the attack. (Ch 4)

**No one defaults to prospecting** — Hunting never happens by leftover minutes. (Ch 1, Ch 14)

**Not-So-Sweet 16** — Diagnostic list of why hunters fail. (Ch 2)

**Offerings** — What you bill for. Short middle of the Power Statement. (Ch 8)

**Organic call** — Unstructured meeting that feels “natural” and usually fails. (Ch 10)

**Pad and pen** — First-meeting tools; projector stays home. (Ch 10)

**Pitiful phone effort** — They dial but sound scared. (Ch 2)

**Power Statement** — One-page, 2–3 minute internal story: headline, transitional phrase, issues, offerings, differentiators. Not a leave-behind. (Ch 8)

**Premature Proposal Syndrome** — Proposing before players, criteria, and issues are known. (Ch 2, Ch 11)

**Prisoners of Hope** — Stop creating opportunities because a few stale deals “should” close. (Ch 2, Ch 14)

**Proactive telephone call** — Weinberg’s term instead of “cold call.” (Ch 9)

**Productively selfish time** — Protecting hunting time from time-wasters. (Ch 2)

**Puffery** — Passionate packaging of value—not dishonesty. Lost art. (Ch 7)

**Reflex resistance** — Buyer’s automatic defense shield against salespeople. (Ch 12)

**Sales Force One** — Travel treated as attack infrastructure (Weinberg: Southwest). (Ch 1, Ch 14)

**Sales is a verb** — Activity in front of lookalikes beats methodology shopping. (Ch 1, Ch 4)

**Sales law (first meeting)** — A first meeting shall never be a presentation. (Ch 13)

**Sales story** — Client-focused talking points used all day across every weapon. (Ch 7–8)

**Same side of the table** — Ally seating (90° / beside), not adversarial across. (Ch 10)

**Show up and throw up / spray and pray** — Dump and hope something hits. (Ch 7, Ch 10)

**So What? test** — If a sentence is about you and the buyer could say “we already have a ___,” reframe. (Ch 8)

**Targeted (pipeline)** — Named, committed, not yet in dialogue. (Ch 14)

**Three magic words** — Visit, fit, value. (Ch 9)

**Time blocking** — Calendar appointments with yourself for outbound work. (Ch 14)

**Transitional phrase** — “[Buyer type] turn to us when…” (Ch 8)

**Two ears, one mouth** — Talk/listen proportion (Abraham). (Ch 10)

**Value** — Magic word: they get ideas even if you are not a fit. (Ch 9)

**Visit** — Magic word instead of appointment/meeting. (Ch 9)

**Weapons** — Story, phone, call, plus the wider arsenal. New Sales Driver part B. (Ch 6)

**Windshield time** — Field mentoring before/after calls. (Ch 1)
