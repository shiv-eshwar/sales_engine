# Chapter 1: Sales Simplified and a Dose of Blunt Truth

## Core Idea
Sales is connecting with customers and prospects to test whether your solution meets their needs. New-business failure is not a mystery of “Sales 2.0”; it is hunters who stopped hunting, managers who stopped mentoring, and gurus who gave passive reps permission not to prospect.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Sales is simple (needs → solutions → connection)**: People and companies have needs; you represent a potential solution; your job is to connect and determine fit. Use when a team is drowning in methodology, CRM theater, or inbound mythology. How: keep asking “Did I get in front of enough lookalike prospects and discover whether we can help?”
  - Why it works: complexity becomes a smokescreen for lame effort. Failure mode: treating simplicity as an excuse to skip targeting, story, and attack planning (those come in Ch 4–14).
- **Sales is a verb**: The most successful new-business people are the most *active*. Use when someone wants a trick instead of face time. How: measure activity that produces meetings with strategically chosen targets; treat social/inbound as a supplement, never a replacement.
- **Sales Force One (travel as attack infrastructure)**: Treat cheap, change-friendly air travel as part of the hunting system, not a perk. Use when geography is the bottleneck to getting in front of targets. How: pre-commit trips, then fill them with customer *and* prospect calls.

## Key Concepts
- **Prospect**: a company you have never done business with that still has needs.
- **Hunting vs. farming**: opening new relationships vs. growing existing ones. Boom years hid how many “salespeople” were only farmers.
- **Reactive account manager**: thrived when inbound demand was high; lost when demand dried up.
- **No one defaults to prospecting**: calendars fill with urgent non-selling work unless you force hunting onto them.
- **Sales mentor vs. sales manager**: the mentor rides along, quizzes pre-call, debriefs after; the desk-jockey manager audits CRM instead of selling skills.
- **Windshield time**: the field classroom—product quizzes, back-door facility reads, role clarity, Achilles’ heel, competitor map.
- **Lookalike prospect**: a prospective customer who “looks and smells” like your best customers. Good things happen when talent sits in front of those.

## Mental Models
- Use **“sales is a verb”** when a rep wants inbound, Twitter, or a new methodology to replace outbound. Activity in front of lookalikes beats cleverness.
- Think of **prosperity as a trap**: long boom cycles produce farmers in hunter seats. When inbound slows, they have no process.
- Use **technology as supplement, not replacement**: social, SEO, and blogs can create inbound; they do not cancel the need to pick targets and call them.
- Think of **the CRM-proficient amateur**: fluent in Salesforce tasks, unable to telephone a prospect for a meeting.

## Anti-patterns
- **Sales 2.0 death-of-prospecting**: “Don’t cold-call; buyers will come when ready.” It is candy for passive-reactive reps. Inbound is not a fitness plan.
- **Waiting for fear to disappear before hunting**: Weinberg’s early hunting worked *because* he did not know he was supposed to be afraid.
- **Calling only purchasing**: owners and senior executives are more fun and more useful (expanded in Ch 5).
- **Manager as CRM cop**: updating the system valued above executing basics (phone, call structure).
- **Pitching a similar product at a lower price**: especially fatal when you are the premium-priced smaller player. Lead with issues you can solve.

## Worked Example
A tiny, higher-priced plastics maker vs. giants. Map existing and potential customers with colored pins. Add a third color from an industry directory. Plan quarterly Midwest drives and a few long-haul trips. On every customer trip, block time to call and visit prospects—same meeting style as customer calls: questions, plant tours, flexibility, eagerness, not a cheaper commodity pitch.

After harvesting competitor accounts, pick one new vertical (~30 named prospects). Sequence: industry data → trade pubs → the major show → phone, mail, samples, more phone → engineers/plant managers → a plant trial → eventually the category leader’s founder/CEO. Result: roughly doubled a forty-year-old company’s revenue in three years.

Replay at a direct-marketing firm with *zero* inherited accounts: choose lookalike targets, learn which issues earn a first meeting, time-block proactive calls, out-call peers ~3:1, surround the hunter with account managers so hunting stays the job. Southwest becomes Sales Force One. Year three: #1 of fifteen, record months.

**Decision rule:** if you are smaller and priced higher, you still hunt lookalikes with a problem-solver posture. Tools are optional; a finite target list, a story about their issues, and high-frequency face time are not.

## Key Takeaways
1. Sales has not changed: connect, discover fit, help them win.
2. Most “salespeople” never had to hunt; prosperity hid it. Do not wait for inbound to return.
3. Social/inbound supplements prospecting; it does not replace selecting targets and picking up the phone.
4. No one defaults to prospecting. Block it or it dies.
5. Managers who mentor in the field beat managers who police CRM.
6. Good things happen when talent gets in front of lookalike prospects; more meetings → more good things.
7. Hunt up the org chart; purchasing is a worse default than owners and executives.
