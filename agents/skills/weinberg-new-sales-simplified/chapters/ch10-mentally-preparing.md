# Chapter 10: Mentally Preparing for the Face-to-Face Sales Call

## Core Idea
The first face-to-face meeting is the pinnacle of outside new-business work. It is **your** call even if they requested it. Walk in with a plan. Discovery always precedes presentation. Bring a pad and pen; leave the projector at home. Two ears, one mouth. Sit as an ally, not an adversary.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **It’s your call; you need a plan**: “organic” as a strategy is amateur hour. When to use: every initial meeting. How: know who is there, why they think you are there, what a win is, *and* the flow (Ch 11). Sharing the agenda next chapter is how you keep ownership. If you cannot say the first ten minutes without a deck, you do not have a plan.
  - Why it works: buyers are tired of wasted time; a visible plan lets them relax and let you fly. Failure mode: confusing “mechanical” with “professional.”
- **Avoid defaulting to the buyer’s process**: unplanned calls become “What do you have for me?” / “You’ve got 30 minutes. Go.” / interrogation. You play defense and learn nothing. How: the moment they try to make you present, time-check, share *your* agenda, and ask what they wanted from the hour.
- **Pad and pen > projector (initial meeting)**: presenting ≠ selling. **Sales law: discovery always precedes presentation.** Capabilities overviews on first meetings are malpractice. Consultative sellers gather, connect, relate, then later propose. How: walk in with notebook only; keep any one-pager in a folder until their issues earn it.
- **Talk-listen law (Danny Abraham / Slim-Fast)**: God gave you two ears and one mouth—use them in that proportion. When talking you are not learning. Track actual talk ratio; reps who feel “balanced” often spoke 80–90%. How: after the call, estimate then compare to a coach’s tally or a recording.
- **Same side of the table**: seller-buyer as allies. Sit perpendicular (90°) at a rectangle, beside at a round. Physical setup fights the adversarial default. How: pause in the doorway of a large room and choose the ally seat before you sit.

## Key Concepts
- **Organic meeting**: no groove; buyer controls or seller babbles.
- **Show up and throw up / spray and pray**: dump everything, hope something hits.
- **Capabilities overview**: usually a request you should not blindly obey (Ch 2, Ch 13).
- **Pre-call questions** (mentor drill from Ch 1, reused here): who, styles, why they think we’re here, win walking out, plan, vulnerabilities, competitors.

## Mental Models
- Use **pilot-in-command** (fully in Ch 11): you file the flight plan before taxi.
- Think **doctor, not pitchman**: exam before prescription.
- Use **two ears / one mouth** as a live scoreboard on every call.
- Prefer **notebook as brand**: the person with the pen is there to learn their business.

## Anti-patterns
- Refusing to plan because planning “feels mechanical.”
- Projector as security blanket on a first visit.
- Equating a slick deck with selling.
- Sitting across the table, reinforcing opposition.
- Talking 80%+ while believing you “asked enough questions.”
- Veteran teams who have *never* had a manager model call structure.

## Worked Example
Field coaching: before the call, standard questions get decent answers. “What’s your plan?” → “I don’t plan; it comes off mechanical. I like meetings organic.” Result: no groove, buyer drives, seller nervous-babbles, coach withholds the life preserver so the lesson sticks.

Counter-example: insurance producer with a week of renewals + two large prospects, strong story, no visualized flow. Thirty minutes of structure work. Friday VM: she felt in control.

Weinberg walking into a referred senior-team meeting: sales guy panics that there is no projector. Notebook + expensive pen. One-pager stays in the folder until discovery earns connecting his framework to *their* issues.

Abraham debrief after a “perfect” product show at Target: nice job on our babies—but you talked too much; we didn’t learn their business. Two ears, one mouth.

**Prep checklist before you park:** styles of attendees; their expected agenda; your win; first 10 minutes (rapport → agenda, not deck); sit 90°; talk-ratio intention ⅓ / ⅔; one probing question already written from each Power Statement issue.

If they booked you for a “capabilities overview,” still run this prep. The request does not cancel discovery-first (Ch 13). Your job in the parking lot is to decide how you will take the left seat without a fight—time-check, agenda, pad out.

## Key Takeaways
1. Own the call; unplanned calls default to the buyer.
2. Organic is not a strategy.
3. Discovery before presentation—always. First meeting ≠ capabilities show.
4. Pad and pen beat projector on the initial visit.
5. Measure talk time; target roughly one-third talking.
6. Sit as an ally (perpendicular / beside).
7. Even veterans often have never been taught call flow—install it (Ch 11).
