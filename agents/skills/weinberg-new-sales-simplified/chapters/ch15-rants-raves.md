# Chapter 15: Rants, Raves, and Reflections

## Core Idea
Thin-but-useful operating norms that did not fit earlier chapters: manners with gatekeepers, contagious attitude, appearance as a quality signal, competing with Goliath, owning the morning and the early innings of deals, real vacations, team selling, and ignoring people who tell you not to prospect.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Manners as access strategy**: receptionists and assistants have feelings and influence. When to use: every inbound screen. How: befriend, smile (even on the phone), let them grab the other line, humor not anger, ask for help/advice, persist *and* stay nice, visit them in person. **Parking**: don’t take the customer spots by the door—buyers notice the walk to the back lot.
- **Attitude is contagious**: stop hanging with professional whiners. Sales is not accounting—you cannot phone in a dead heart. The company looks to sales for pace and hope. If the culture is truly anti-sales, leave (Ch 3). If it isn’t, bring the A-game or find a company that wants hunters.
- **Appearance / image**: look fresh, successful, confident. Condition and contemporariness beat tribal “always a suit” vs. “come as you are.” Dress **one level up** from the customer. Don’t cheap out on shoes or bags. **Lose the embroidered logo shirt**—it is a uniform and screams “we are about us.”
- **Play Goliath to win**: bigger ≠ better; bigger is often slower, clumsy, inflexible, arrogant, complacent. You compete against *their local team*, not the whole org. Ask who is more committed. Historical David/Goliath: Goliath wins ~70–80%, David ~20–30%. Ten battles with two or three huge wins can make the year.
- **Winners: office early + deal early**: mornings are for high-payoff hunting, not coworker chat, news, or inbox (inbox = reactive third of the day). Work the target list *before* opportunities materialize so you lead, not chase RFPs (Ch 2 late-to-the-party). Ignore voices that say don’t call until perfectly qualified.
- **Real vacation / off the grid**: unplug so the brain can do work it cannot do while thumb-scrolling dinner email. Checking a few times a day destroys the asset. Auto-reply that you cannot receive until return. Clients respect a recharged hunter; families notice.
- **Team selling / use your resources**: lone-ranger pride loses. Bring CEO (you still run the call), SMEs for technical buyers who dismiss “the sales guy,” network intro when a key player hates you. No extra credit for flying solo. Know blind spots before you are stuck.
- **Beware who tells you not to prospect**: naysayers usually don’t do it, didn’t have a real list/weapons/story, and barely dialed. Same-family illustration: sister is a calling machine and wins; brother declares cold-calling dead and misses. He doesn’t want to prospect, so he doesn’t want *you* to either.

## Key Concepts
- **Productive niceness**: persistent + kind, not doormat (contrast Ch 2 “good corporate citizen”).
- **Position A**: already inside the account when they get ready to look.
- **Off the grid**: truly unable to receive, not “checking twice a day.”

## Mental Models
- Use **gatekeepers as allies**, not obstacles.
- Think **you vs. their rep**, not you vs. their Fortune ranking.
- Use **morning as hunting prime**; inbox is farming bait.
- Treat **anti-prospecting advice as a confession**, not a market insight.

## Anti-patterns
- Rudeness to assistants; parking in the best stall.
- Mentoring rookies via complaint at dinner.
- Logo shirts, dead shoes, dated “tube TV” look that signals corner-cutting.
- Towel-throwing when the competitor is big.
- Starting the day in email; waiting to be perfectly qualified.
- Fake vacation; maverick selling past your blind spots.
- Listening to failing reps about whether calling works.

## Worked Example
Two remote siblings, same company: sister runs a finite list and proactive calls; brother evangelizes “cold calling is dead” and has monthly excuses. Interrogate the brother with Weinberg’s questions (list? focus hours? story? how many dials?). His answers are the tell.

Goliath week: you will not beat the giant 10/10. Plan ten serious fights; need two or three defining wins. Compete on discovery quality, relationships, tailored solution, speed—against their local crew.

Morning: first 90 minutes = Targeted calls (Ch 14 block). Email after. Vacation: auto-reply off-grid; outline next quarter’s attack on a beach, like the author outlining this book.

## Key Takeaways
1. Be persistently nice to the people who can open or kill access; park considerately.
2. Attitude leaks to the buyer and the plant floor—choose inputs.
3. Look like a professional, not a walking billboard.
4. Hunt giants on purpose; you need a David batting average, not a monopoly.
5. Own the morning and get in deals early.
6. Real time off improves selling; fake time off doesn’t.
7. Use the company; ignore failed prospectors’ theology.
