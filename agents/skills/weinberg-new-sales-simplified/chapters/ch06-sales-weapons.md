# Chapter 6: Our Sales Weapons: What’s in the Arsenal?

## Core Idea
Never bring a water pistol to a gunfight. After targets are locked, load the jet and become proficient at firing. Ill-equipped reps will not engage; armed-but-inaccurate reps pull the trigger and miss. Only a handful of weapons are fully *owned* by sales—and three of those dominate results: **sales story, proactive telephone call, face-to-face sales call**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Arsenal audit**: list applicable weapons, missing weapons, loaded-but-unfired weapons, and the few that would change results if mastered. When to use: after the target list exists, before the attack calendar. How: stockpile → pick mission-critical → drill proficiency.
- **Mission-critical trio (sales-owned)**:
  1. Sales story (Ch 7–8)—foundation of every other weapon
  2. Proactive telephone call (Ch 9)—still the most accurate weapon for a meeting
  3. Face-to-face sales call (Ch 10–11)—pinnacle the other weapons exist to create
- **Full cache (know it; do not equally prioritize it)**:
  - **Sales story** — language/talking points for “what do you do?”
  - **Networking** — relationships that open doors
  - **Social media** — research and engage (LinkedIn, Twitter); supplement, not replacement
  - **Email** — less disruptive first touch; useful entrée if not a hiding place from the phone
  - **Proactive telephone call** — still deadly for face-to-face
  - **Voice mail** — majority of attempts; treat as a chance to drop story and start a relationship
  - **Print** — sell sheets, brochures, intro letters
  - **Digital** — blogs, podcasts, video, webinars as low-cost appetizers
  - **White papers / industry experts** — third-party issues framing
  - **Initial face-to-face** (inside reps: the full call *is* on the phone)
  - **Probing questions** — more selling than great presentations
  - **Case studies** — proof it works
  - **Samples and demos** — powerful if not boring/self-focused
  - **Trade shows** — costly but one major account pays for a lifetime of fandom
  - **Facility tours** — orchestrate culture/process as a weapon; rehearse roles
  - **Team selling** — drag the CEO on high-level calls; SMEs for technical buyers
  - **Entertainment** — real business gets done off-site when used appropriately
  - **Presentations** — obligatory in some worlds; redeem or they backfire (Ch 13)
  - **Proposals** — last weapon they can say yes to; customize
  - **References** — third-party proof; cultivate a *variety*; coach them when the relationship allows

## Key Concepts
- **Owned vs. borrowed weapons**: marketing owns many assets; sales merely fires them. Story, phone, and the call are yours to sharpen.
- **Reluctant vs. ineffective**: won’t shoot vs. terrible shots. Neither wins.
- **Proficiency**: the sniper rifle only matters if you can use it. Practice changes confidence.
- **Pinnacle call**: everything before the first meeting exists to create it (outside role).

## Mental Models
- Think **fighter pilot**: targets locked (Ch 5) → load weapons → fly the mission (Ch 14).
- Use **questions > presentations** whenever you are tempted to demo first.
- Prefer **third-party issue content** when the buyer resists a vendor pitch but will read an industry problem.
- Use **team selling** when the buyer sees you only as “the relationship person.”

## Anti-patterns
- Engaging prospects unarmed (no story, no talk track, no questions).
- Firing every weapon equally instead of mastering the trio.
- Hiding in email/social because the phone is scary (Ch 1, Ch 9).
- Self-focused demos that blow up in your hands.
- Unrehearsed plant tours and accidental SME pile-ons that steal call control.
- Treating voice mail as a failure instead of a planned touch.

## Worked Example
A 401(k) hunter treats the prospect HQ tour as a designed weapon: every stop planned, key employees rehearsed, culture and process visible. That is “facility tour as weapon,” not a walk-and-talk.

Apply an arsenal audit to a premium B2B hunter with a 40-name list:

| Weapon | Status | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Story / power statement | Vague, seller-focused | Rebuild (Ch 8) — first |
| Phone + VM | Avoided | Drill mini-statement (Ch 9) |
| Face-to-face structure | Organic | Install 8-phase call (Ch 11) |
| LinkedIn | Profile stale | Research + connect only |
| Case studies | Three buried in a PDF | Weave two into discovery |
| CEO on calls | Never | Book for top 5 dream targets |
| Demo | Default first meeting | Ban until after discovery |

Do not add webinars until the trio is live. Weapons without proficiency are still water pistols.

## Key Takeaways
1. Arm, then become accurate. Reluctant and ineffective both lose.
2. Audit the cache; master the sales-owned trio first.
3. Story underpins letters, email, VM, calls, decks, and proposals.
4. Phone remains the meeting weapon; inbound/social are supplements.
5. Questions, cases, tours, team, and references move deals; demos and presentations easily become self-harm.
6. Borrowed marketing assets still need a salesperson who can fire them at *named* targets.
7. Next chapters deep-dive the three weapons you fully control.
