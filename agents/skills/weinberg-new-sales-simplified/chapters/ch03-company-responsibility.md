# Chapter 3: The Company’s Responsibility for Sales Success

## Core Idea
Salespeople do not operate in a vacuum. If strategy is muddy, sales is treated as the company dumpster, hunters are chained to farming, pay rewards the annuity book, and culture treats reps like children, coaching the New Sales Driver will not save you. Sales follows strategy; the CEO must do that job first.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Sales follows strategy**: the sales team executes a *clear* strategy; it does not invent one in the field. When to use: new-logo shortfall plus confused messaging. How: demand answers to the clarity checklist below. If they do not exist, 100% of the shortfall sits with the CEO, not the VP of sales.
  - Why it works: hunting without a defined place in the market is wilderness wandering. Failure mode: blaming “the team can’t sell” when no one can say what you sell, to whom, and why the price is fair.
- **Clarity checklist (non-negotiable briefing for sales)**:
  1. Reason for existence
  2. Direction and why it is correct
  3. What we sell and why
  4. Which markets and our position in them
  5. Competitive landscape and why we are better/different
  6. Why pricing matches the value we create
- **Hybrid hunter-farmer problem**: the dominant SMB model loads account-management work on the few people who can actually hunt. When to use: leadership wants more new logos *and* the same people quoting, firefighting, and shepherding production. How: if true A-player hunters are ~10–15% of the team and farmers are abundant, stop making the fisherman also clean, cook, serve, and do the dishes.
- **Comp that drives hunting**: smart salespeople work the plan. When the company wants net-new but pays every dollar the same, the rational move is to babysit the annuity. How: shrink the attractiveness of the existing-book annuity over time; over-pay year-one new business.
- **Sales culture (heart + head)**: fun, energized, competitive, results-focused, heart-engaged. Sales is connecting; pride and belief are not optional. If you cannot improve the client’s condition, stop taking the money (Alan Weiss).

## Key Concepts
- **Low view of sales**: samples, dinners, travel, and tips policed; salespeople treated as the enemy; sales manager’s desk as garbage dump for every problem.
- **Service burden**: quoting, orders, fires, production shepherding—time that cannot also be hunting time. Leadership cannot have both without staffing both.
- **A-player hunter scarcity**: few can be counted on for new business year after year; wasting their fishing time is idiocy.
- **Fixed vs. variable pay**: when results can swing wildly, too much base overpays underachievers and underpays stars (they leave).
- **Annuity book**: existing portfolio that pays the same whether you hunt or not.
- **Anti-sales culture**: critique, disdain, no decision rights; coaching the story is wasted if hearts are disengaged.

## Mental Models
- Use **“Mr. CEO, do your job so I can do mine”** when sales is asked to set strategy.
- Think of the **star fisherman** who only fishes 25% of the time: you will not catch more marlin by adding dishwashing to the quota.
- Use **“a dollar is not a dollar”** when finance commissions all revenue equally but the goal is net-new logos.
- Think of **sales vs. accounting**: you cannot trudge through with a dead heart and still connect, build trust, and show pride.

## Anti-patterns
- **Coaching-as-fig-leaf**: hire a coach to “fix selling” while strategy, service model, and culture stay broken. Coaching morphs into consulting for a reason.
- **All problems go to the sales manager** (including day-two cell-phone technical support for account #248).
- **Hybrid role + new-logo quota** with no freed calendar.
- **Comp that retains B-minus farmers and loses A hunters**.
- **Micromanagement / treating reps like children**: no territory decisions; fear, humiliation, commission deductions without explanation.
- **CEO as amateur sales director**: dictating exact call tactics from the corner office.

## Worked Example
A $90M distributor in a downturn. Sales exec agrees to also manage six reps. Day two: angry customer calls *his cell* with technical quality questions. CS had a counter, a tech, and six more-qualified reps. Root cause: “all problems go to the sales manager.” That policy is why the team cannot hunt.

Same pattern at three other clients: leadership hires hunting coaching, then leaves quoting/order-entry/firefighting on the hunter. Observation: little proactive target work → little new business. The fix is structural (free fishing time), not another pitch workshop.

Comp illustration: a fat book pays the same commission on last year’s accounts as on a brutal new logo. Rational hunter-farmer overserves the book. Align pay: decaying commission on old revenue + rich year-one new-business bonus.

Culture illustration: a president restaffs sales repeatedly, critiques every sample and objection, withholds decision rights. Coach fires the client: next hires will fail until culture and structure change. Sales coaching is wasted without passion for the job.

## Key Takeaways
1. Demand strategic clarity before blaming sales talent.
2. The hybrid hunter-farmer load is the #1 structural killer of New Sales.
3. Protect hunters’ fishing time; farmers are easier to hire.
4. Pay for the behavior you claim to want—especially net-new vs. annuity.
5. Too much base flattens the difference between stars and laggards.
6. A low view of sales and a dumpster manager desk destroy hunting capacity.
7. Heart-engaged culture is a company responsibility, not a pep talk.
