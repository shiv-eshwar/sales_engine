# Chapter 9: Your Friend the Phone

## Core Idea
Rename the “cold call” to the **proactive telephone call**. Inbound/SEO/social is a supplement, not a replacement, for the meeting-getting weapon. Mindset, voice, a call *outline* (not a theatrical script), laser objective, a mini Power Statement, asking **three times**, and treating voice mail as a campaign turn the phone from terror into a friend.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Keys to calling — mindset first**: you are not a dinner-hour telemarketer. You are a businessperson who can likely help a *strategically chosen* target. When to use: before any dial block. How: erase the tape that you are an irrelevant intrusion; if you believe they may be better off with you, you should *want* to call.
- **Voice: lose the sales voice**: buyers judge on sound + first words only. Casual, comfortable, slightly informal, equal-to-equal—not subservient-formal, not “salesy.” Professional ≠ pedestal. Have peers listen when you don’t know they’re listening.
- **Script vs. outline**: no to memorized long canned scripts (they sound read). Yes to logical structure + a few verbatim talking points so you can test what works and hit story phrases.
- **Laser objective**:
  - **Outside**: get the face-to-face meeting. Period.
  - **Inside**: get the full sales conversation (now or scheduled).
- **Stop overqualifying**: if they are on the strategic list, you already decided you want to see them. Underperformance is almost never “too many unqualified meetings with lookalikes”; it is not enough activity. Do not relitigate targeting mid-call.
- **Opening phrases**:
  - Name + your name + company + **“Let me steal a minute.”** Casual, honest about interruption, unlike “How are *you* today?”, “Did I catch you at a good time?”, “Can I have a minute?” (easy no; sounds like every other rep).
  - If they are truly slammed: let them go, *tell* (don’t ask) you’ll call back. Second call: “I’m the one who caught you running to FedEx.” Human points.
  - **“I head up…”** — position yourself (west region, education book, client relationships). Title-optional; everyone heads up something. Kills telemarketer self-image.
- **Telephone mini Power Statement**: two favorite client-issue bullets (one provocative/unique, one broadly applicable) + at most one differentiator. Conversational. Then **stop**. Let them process. If useful, add one more issue/diff, then ask for the meeting.
- **Inside: build a bridge**: after mini-statement, probes, pre-call research, or “how should I build a relationship with you?” Then listen and log.
- **Ask three times**: first no is programmed, not personal. Relational conflict-avoiders fail here. Push without becoming a jerk.
- **Three magic words: visit, fit, value**:
  - Visit (not appointment/meeting)
  - Fit (non-desperate: see if we might be a fit)
  - Value (bring value; on the third ask: visit anyway—you’ll get value/ideas even if we’re not a fit)
- **Winning with voice mail** (~3/4 of attempts):
  1. Positive: a touch, not a failure
  2. Expect it; prepare (no pound-key panic)
  3. One or two issue snippets; short
  4. Campaign, not a novel; vary snippets
  5. Ask for callback **and** say you will call again (don’t be one-and-done)
  6. Human: humor or light guilt on ~third message; never anger

## Key Concepts
- **Proactive telephone call**: Weinberg’s replacement term for cold call.
- **Fake vs. pitiful effort** (Ch 2): this chapter is the fix.
- **Practice ladder**: start on smaller accounts; confidence compounds.

## Mental Models
- Use **steal-a-minute + I head up + mini-story + ask** as the default outside-call skeleton.
- Think **appetizer**: phone creates hunger for the meeting entrée.
- Use **third ask + visit/fit/value** when the programmed no arrives.
- Treat **VM as episode 2 of 5**, not a failed live connect.

## Anti-patterns
- Believing Sales 2.0: you no longer need outbound calls.
- Sales voice; “did I catch you at a good time?”
- Reading a long script.
- Overqualifying lookalike targets on the first call.
- Filling silence after the mini-statement.
- One VM and quit; angry VM; 90-second ramble.
- Caving after the first no to avoid sounding pushy.

## Worked Example
Reconstructed outside call (security hunter, property manager Steve):

“Hi Steve, Rob Thompson, Allsafe. I’m getting back with you—I caught you in a tenant fire drill last week.” [He laughs.] “I head up client service for the downtown corridor. A lot of property managers are looking to us because of liability and life-safety pressure for tenants and guests. We’re also helping managers who worry the current security setup isn’t what was promised.” [Stop.]

Steve: “We’re fine / send an email / we have a vendor.”

Ask 1: “I’d like to visit for 30 minutes Tuesday or Wednesday to see if we might be a fit.”
Ask 2: (after objection) “Fair. Still worth a visit to compare how you’re approaching emergency response.”
Ask 3: “Steve, I understand you’re slammed. Visit with me anyway. You’ll get ideas even if we’re not a fit to help.”

VM if no connect: 20 seconds, one issue, number slowly, “I’ll call again Thursday.” Third VM: human + perseverance, no irritation.

Inside variant: same mini-statement, then “How are you handling after-hours incidents today?” and run Ch 11 on the phone.

## Key Takeaways
1. Proactive calling is still required; inbound is not a substitute.
2. Mindset and natural voice outperform scripts and sales-speak.
3. Outside objective = meeting; stop overqualifying the strategic list.
4. Open: steal a minute → I head up → mini Power Statement → stop.
5. Ask three times using visit, fit, and value.
6. Voice mail is a planned campaign; say you’ll call again.
7. Practice on smaller names until the motion is boring.
