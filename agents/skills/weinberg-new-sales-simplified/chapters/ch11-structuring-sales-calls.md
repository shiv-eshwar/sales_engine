# Chapter 11: Structuring Winning Sales Calls

## Core Idea
Professionals **conduct** sales calls; they do not “go on” them. Think pilot-in-command: sequence matters as much as skill. A shared agenda differentiates you, keeps you from selling too soon, and earns the right to discover. Discovery precedes presentation. Leave with a scheduled next step or the call failed.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Phases of a winning sales call** (7 for a prospect, 8 for an existing customer):
  1. Build rapport and identify the buyer’s style
  2. Share the agenda (get buy-in, seek input) — **most important phase**
  3. Clean up their issues (**existing customers only**)
  4. Deliver the Power Statement (≤ 3 minutes)
  5. Ask probing questions (discovery)
  6. Sell
  7. Determine fit and seek out objections
  8. Define and schedule the next step
  When to use: every initial and most subsequent calls. How: fly the stages in order; do not skip to 6.

### Phase notes
- **Rapport / style**: lasts as long as *they* want. Not marlin-on-the-wall cheese. News, sport, a LinkedIn detail. Chatty relational → stay; hard-charger one-word answers → move. Cheat sheet: slightly adapt toward them (smile with the expressive; speed up with the driver; slow down and bring detail for analytical). Avoid “mirroring” as acting; adapt so they can *hear* you.
- **Agenda** (three jobs): (1) differentiator—almost no one does this well; ask what *they* want, even “why did you invite me?” (2) roadmap so they aren’t taken on a ride; (3) announces a dialogue, shocking the buyer out of “here comes the puke.” Confirm time. Preview: 2–3 min story → questions on their QRS/XYZ → maybe cases/options → discuss fit/next step.
- **Clean up junk up front**: they cannot hear new products while seething about a miss. Existing accounts only.
- **Power Statement**: full form; watch wince/nod/notes; answer a clarifying question *briefly*; keep powder dry for discovery.
- **Doctor analogy**: you would not trust a physician who lectures an hour then writes a script. Three-minute credentials/philosophy, then exam.
- **Four question categories**:
  1. **Personal** — how they’re measured, bonus, what would make life easier
  2. **Strategic / directional** — market, competitors, initiatives, grow vs. cut (do homework; don’t ask Googleable questions)
  3. **Issue- and opportunity-seeking** — rewrite Power Statement issues as open probes; camp on blood; ask why they asked *that*; consequences of inaction; salt the wound; **do not sell yet**
  4. **Sales process** — authority/influencers, timeline, budget, willingness to change, stage, **decision criteria** (how, not only who), alternatives, competition. Slow down to speed up. Deals go dark from skipped data. Best influencer question: “Along with yourself, who else really cares about this?”
- **Sell**: now, and only now, tailor with *their* words. Discovery precedes presentation.
- **Fit + objections**: “It looks like we might be a fit… What do you think?” Seek obstacles while face-to-face. “I get the feeling you have concerns. Tell me now.”
- **Next step**: “What do you suggest as an appropriate next step?” Then **mutual** “I’ll do EFG, you’ll do JKL,” **calendar it**. Undefined next step = fail, even after a standing ovation. Don’t grovel thanks; you brought value too.

## Key Concepts
- **Pilot-in-command**: checklists, flight plan, you fly.
- **Pick the scab**: follow pain; don’t spray the question list.
- **Premature proposal** (Ch 2): 15-hour proposals after meeting one, because you skipped process questions.
- **Talk-listen** carried from Ch 10.

## Mental Models
- Use **agenda-as-shock** when buyers expect a monologue.
- Use **issues → questions** so discovery is not random.
- Think **scheduled handshake > brilliant meeting**.
- Prefer **earning questions with a 3-minute story** over pitching first to “earn the right” to ask.

## Anti-patterns
- Skipping agenda; buyer in the left seat.
- Pitching when they flinch during the Power Statement.
- Interrogation energy; obvious/stupid questions; not listening because you’re marching the list.
- Fast-forward to proposal excitement; prospect later goes dark.
- Traditional hard close after a no-pressure dialogue.
- Leaving without a date.

## Worked Example
Reconstructed 30-minute agenda (paraphrased):

“Ron, we set 30 minutes—how are you on time? I’ll have you out before 11:30. I’d like two or three minutes on the issues we solve for HR and why they bring us in, plus how we’re different. Then I’ll turn it over and ask how you’re approaching QRS. Depending on what I hear, I can share a relevant case or options. Then we can see if we’re a fit and whether a next step makes sense. That’s my plan—what did you want to walk away with?”

Then: full Power Statement. They nod on “managers stuck with a farming team.” Discovery camps there. Process: “Who else really cares?” + budget source + change likelihood. Only then sell the hunter-coaching offer in their words. “Looks like a fit—what do you think?” Surface “legal has to see vendors.” Next: you send a one-pager Friday; they intro legal; meeting holds Tuesday 10:00—both calendars, now.

## Key Takeaways
1. Conduct the call in phases; order is a differentiator.
2. Sharing the agenda is the highest-leverage setup move.
3. Clean up existing-customer junk before your agenda.
4. ≤3-minute Power Statement, then questions—not the reverse.
5. Four question buckets; listen and dig; process questions prevent dark deals.
6. Sell only after discovery; use their language.
7. Fit, objections, and a **scheduled** mutual next step—or you failed.
