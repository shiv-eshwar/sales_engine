# Chapter 16: New Business Development Selling Is Not Complicated

## Core Idea
Prospecting is not a mystery and there is **no magic bullet**. New customers are the lifeblood; inbound will not arrive in a steady warm stream. Success is executing the New Sales Driver basics at high frequency, with weapons that work early in the process, discovery before presentation, a calendar you actually own, and every behavior filtered through buyer perception.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **No magic bullet**: when teams ask for guaranteed closes, gender-committee tricks, first-vs-last Friday presentation slots, or “secret sauce,” redirect to the basics inspection (below). Trick plays without blocking and tackling fail.
- **Basics inspection (ask these before technique theater)**:
  1. Show the *actual* finite target list and the strategic thinking behind it
  2. How much effort has gone into *that* list?
  3. Let me hear the sales story
  4. How much proactive phone? How’s it working?
  5. Outline how you conduct a sales call
  6. Time split: existing service vs. proactive selling
  7. Pipeline volume and time across stages
  8. Written business plan: strategies + committed activities
  Discomfort answering these *is* the diagnosis. Use this as a coaching opener, a self-audit Friday, or a hiring filter for “hunters.”
  - Why it works: trick-play questions hide missing blocking and tackling. Failure mode: skipping to slideware because the inspection is embarrassing.
- **Nail the New Sales Driver cold**:
  - **A. Select targets** (Ch 5) — finite, focused, written, workable
  - **B. Create and deploy weapons** (Ch 6–13) — especially story, phone, face-to-face
  - **C. Plan and execute the attack** (Ch 14) — sales is a verb; fly the mission
- **Early fundamentals beat glamorous late stages**: phone and first-call structure improve win rates downstream more than polishing the boardroom show.
- **Be Jerry, not Frank** (Ch 13): discovery before presentation; first meeting is never a presentation; break the mold.
- **Guard selling time**: gravitational pull of account management and corporate citizenship (Ch 2). Treat prospecting blocks like a CEO meeting.
- **Resistance filter daily** (Ch 12): approach, tone, words, phone and call method—shield up or down.

## Key Concepts
- **Simple job restated**: prospects have needs; you have potential solutions; engage to test alignment.
- **Oversupply of farmers, shortage of hunters** (Intro/Ch 1): becoming a reliable hunter is scarce, valuable work.
- **No secret sales moves**.

## Mental Models
- Use the **basics inspection** whenever someone wants a closing gimmick.
- Prefer **high-frequency execution of A-B-C** over new methodology shopping.
- Think **blocking and tackling**: story, list, dials, structured calls, calendar.
- Run **perception** as a standing filter, not a chapter you finished.
- Use **“be Jerry, not Frank”** as the presentation test: did you look inside their situation before you showed your binder?

## Anti-patterns
- Hunting for guaranteed techniques while the list is a pile of folders.
- Polishing presentations while skipping phone and first-call craft.
- Allowing service gravity to erase hunting blocks.
- Treating inbound as if it were 100% of the funnel.
- Complicating sales to hide lame effort (Intro).

## Worked Example
Kickoff with a mid-market team that wants “the close that always works.”

Do not answer. Run the inspection. They cannot print a list (CRM soup). Story starts with “we’ve been in business 29 years.” Phone time is “when I can.” Calls are organic. 90% of the week is existing-account firefighting. Pipeline is two stale Hot deals (Prisoners of Hope). No written plan.

Prescribe nothing exotic: 40 lookalike names, Power Statement exercise this week, two 2-hour DND call blocks, Ch 11 agenda on every first meeting, ⅓-⅓-⅓, blocks treated as CEO-level. Revisit magic-bullet questions only after those are live. They won’t need them.

Order of operations from this chapter’s close: (1) targets, (2) story via the Ch 8 exercise, (3) phone + first-call craft before boardroom polish, (4) discovery always before presentation, (5) calendar fortress, (6) daily resistance filter. That is the whole book, compressed.

## Key Takeaways
1. New-business selling is simple: engage lookalike prospects and test fit.
2. There is no magic bullet; there is the Driver.
3. If you cannot show list, story, phone, call structure, time split, pipeline, and plan, you do not have a tactics problem.
4. Sharpen story; master phone and first meetings before boardroom theater.
5. Discovery always precedes presentation; first meeting is not a pitch.
6. Own the calendar; high-frequency attack; selfishly guard selling time.
7. Sell against reflex resistance every day. Now go forth and sell.
