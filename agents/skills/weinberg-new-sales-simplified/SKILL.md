# New Sales. Simplified.
**Author**: Mike Weinberg | **Year**: 2012, AMACOM | **Chapters**: 16 | **Generated**: 2026-09-12

How-to for the individual hunter or sales leader charged with net-new business. Demystify prospecting; veterans re-meet timeless truths; rookies get a path. Sales is simple: needs exist, you have a potential solution, your job is to connect and test fit. People who complicate it are often hiding lame effort. There is an oversupply of farmers in sales seats and a shortage of hunters.

## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**New Sales Driver** — use whenever the job is net-new accounts or new lines of business:
1. **Select targets**
2. **Create and deploy weapons**
3. **Plan and execute the attack**

Work them in that order. No names → nothing to fire. Great weapons + no calendar → academic. **Bold declaration:** if new business is missing, the cause is poor selection *or* lack of focus; lame weapons *or* lack of proficiency; inadequate planning *or* lack of execution (usually more than one). Before coaching tactics, confirm it is a *sales* problem: clear strategy and demand, pricing matches value, comp not fighting hunting, talent at least B-. Prefer owning process over partner-led or buyer-led theater. **Sales is a verb.** Social, SEO, and inbound are supplements, not replacements. **No one defaults to prospecting**—calendars fill with farming and “urgent” work unless you occupy them first.

**Use Driver diagnosis when…**
- Empty pipeline + busy week → almost always C (no protected attack) plus maybe A (no printed list)
- Lots of activity, no traction → A (wrong/unfocused names) or B (seller-focused story, pitiful phone, organic calls)
- Team wants a closer trick → refuse; run the basics inspection (list, effort, story, phone, call structure, time split, pipeline, written plan)

**Select targets.** First because you cannot prospect unnamed companies. Targeting is one of the few *strategic* acts in sales: you choose where scarce time goes. The list must be **finite, focused, written, and workable**. Clone **lookalikes** of best customers (look, feel, smell). Build from Who/Why: who buys, why they started/stay, who beats you and why, who left, who almost bought, who refers / should refer. A pile of journals and CRM screens is not a list—print one page you can point to. Focus a vertical or account *type* until you know the language and have cases; there is no prize for the widest mix. Size to the cycle (a dozen enterprise names or hundreds inside can both be “workable”). Segment current customers into **Largest / Most Growable / Most At-Risk / Other**; overserve 1–3 (**intentional imbalance**); kill the milk run. When influencers *are* the path (Realtors, specifiers, branch bankers), they get their own four-test list and referral math. Cap **dream targets** at 4–5 with mini-plans and a little weekly time; keep working the regular list or you become a **Prisoner of Hope**. Aim **higher in the org** with issue/result language; an executive bounce-down is a win. You can walk down after a high no; climbing over a low-level no makes enemies.

**Weapons.** After names exist, load the jet and get accurate. Ill-equipped reps won’t engage; armed-but-wild reps miss. Sales fully owns three mission-critical weapons: **sales story**, **proactive telephone call**, **face-to-face sales call**. Story fragments live in every other weapon, so sharpen it first. Supporting cache: networking, social, email, voice mail, print, digital appetizers, white papers, probing questions, cases, samples/demos, trade shows, facility tours, team selling, entertainment, presentations, proposals, references. Audit: applicable / missing / loaded-but-unfired / highest-leverage if mastered. Do not hide in email because the phone is scary.

**Sales story / Power Statement.** The story is not about you. Buyers want what’s in it for them. **A premium price requires a premium story**—articulating the delta vs. alternatives is the job; lowest price does not need a salesperson. **Sequence is the weapon:** client issues (pains removed, problems solved, opportunities captured, results achieved) → short offerings (what you bill for) → differentiators (why you are the best choice for *those* issues). Lead with offerings and you scream commodity. Lead with how great you are and you still sound like every other vendor. **No Issues = No Sale**—if they feel none of the issues, move on.

Power Statement (internal, 2–3 minutes live, not a leave-behind): headline (1–2 sentence category) + transitional phrase (“[Buyer type / role] turn to us when…”) + 3–7 provocative issue bullets + few offering sentences + intriguing lead-in + ≥5 differentiators. **So What?** every line; if they could reply “we already have a ___,” reframe. Draft on three pages with the team; *call* raving fans for their words; tinker for weeks. Excerpt: letters ≈ trimmed full statement; drip email = 1–2 issues; phone = mini (two issues ± one differentiator); discovery questions = issues rewritten as open probes. Even “commodity” sellers can shift “in stock / best price?” to issues (human access, dedicated team) before price.

**Proactive telephone call** (never “cold call”—the label poisons the motion). Inbound is not a meeting factory. Mindset: you are a businessperson who can likely help a *chosen* target. Lose the **sales voice**; casual, equal, slightly informal. No long theatrical script; yes to a call outline plus a few verbatim story lines. **Outside objective = face-to-face.** Stop overqualifying lookalikes on the first call—you already decided they belong on the list. Inside objective = full sales conversation now or scheduled.

Open: name + yours + company + **“Let me steal a minute”** (not “did I catch you at a good time?”). If they are truly slammed, let them go and the callback is human. **“I head up…”** positions you as a peer. Mini Power Statement, then **stop**. Ask **three times**—first no is programmed, not personal. Magic words: **visit** (not appointment), **fit** (non-desperate), **value** (ideas even if not a fit). Voice mail ~3/4 of the time: treat as a campaign touch, one or two issue snippets, leave the number slowly, ask for a callback *and* say you will call again, humor/light guilt around message three, never anger. Practice on smaller names first.

**Face-to-face.** Pinnacle of outside hunting. It is **your** call even if they requested it. Unplanned (“organic”) calls default to “what do you have for me?” / “you’ve got 30 minutes, go.” **Discovery always precedes presentation.** First meeting: pad and pen, projector at home. Track talk time; target roughly one-third talking (**two ears, one mouth**). Sit perpendicular or beside—**same side of the table**. Conduct the call as **pilot-in-command**:

1. Rapport + style (lasts as long as *they* want; adapt speed/detail; not marlin-on-the-wall)
2. **Share the agenda** (biggest differentiator) + “what did you want from this?”
3. Clean up junk first (existing customers only)
4. Full Power Statement ≤ 3 minutes; note flinches; keep powder dry
5. Discovery: personal; strategic; issue/opportunity (from the statement); process (authority, money, timeline, **who else really cares?**, decision *criteria*, alternatives)
6. Sell in their words
7. “Looks like we might be a fit… what do you think?” Hunt objections now
8. Mutual next step **on both calendars**—or the call failed, standing ovation included

**Reflex resistance.** Everyone puts up a shield; you inherited it. Dual objective on every touch: what you want *and* how they will perceive you. Filter: beliefs (they may be better off), sound (human), view (legitimate need, not tire-kicker), feelings (warm, not leftover anger), words (issue-first). Sales-voice and self-biography slam the shield up.

**Presenting ≠ selling.** By sales law, a **first meeting shall never be a presentation**. Cannot fill “our understanding of your situation”? You have no business pitching—even if asked. Insist on discovery first. If procurement forbids it, steal the first 15 minutes of the hour for why-us / issues / success, then customize live. Four-slide open: title, agenda, they-turn-to-us-when, their situation—**stop and talk**. Be **Jerry** (look inside the soffit, ask the vision) not **Frank** (campus photos, logos, flowcharts, zero questions). Never cede process to a President’s Club partner.

**Execute the attack.** Time-block 90–180 minutes on the calendar first; keep the appointment; DND; no inbox (fake phone effort lives there). From near-zero, two 2-hour blocks/week already changes math; heavy hunters might run 8–9 sessions and still only spend ~⅓ of the week hunting. Reverse-engineer: closes ← proposals ← fits ← meetings ← conversations. Five-part **personal business plan**: goals, strategies (attach the list), actions, obstacles, development—present it; calendar must match. Pre-book travel 4–6 weeks out before meetings exist (**Sales Force One** / change-friendly fares). Pipeline: **Targeted / Active / Hot**. Spend **⅓-⅓-⅓**. Healthy = full, moving, balanced. Gravity to Hot = Prisoners of Hope.

**Not-So-Sweet 16** (mirror; pick 3–4): haven’t had to / don’t know how; waiting on the company; Prisoners of Hope; can’t tell the story; awful targets/focus; late to the party; negative; fake or pitiful phone; unlikeable / don’t adapt / low EQ; weak calls; babysit accounts; good corporate citizen; don’t own process / **Premature Proposal Syndrome**; unprotected calendar; stopped learning; not built to hunt (too conflict-averse or analysis-paralysis—role fit, not a pep talk).

**Company side.** Sales follows strategy: CEO owes existence, direction, offering, markets, competitive stance, price-to-value. Low view of sales + manager-as-dumpster kills hunting time. Hybrid hunter-farmer is the #1 structural killer—free the scarce A-player fisherman to fish. Comp: too much base overpays laggards and underpays stars; equal commission on the annuity book makes babysitting rational—decay old-book payout, bonus year-one new. Heart-engaged culture is required; if anti-sales, leave.

**No magic bullet.** Blocking and tackling beat trick plays. Go forth and sell.
