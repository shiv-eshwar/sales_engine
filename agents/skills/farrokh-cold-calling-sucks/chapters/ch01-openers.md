# Chapter 1: Openers

## Core Idea
Most cold calls die in the first 30 seconds because the opener marks you as a telemarketer. Lead with context about *them* before your name, your product, or any pleasantry — the opener’s only job is to earn the next 60 seconds.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Context-First Opener**: First words must prove you are not a stranger. Mutual investor, peer in the same office, same industry, same geo+persona, same situation.
  - When to use: Every live connect. Specificity beats “we work with other professionals who breathe.”
  - How: Context → name/company → question or permission. Never name first.
  - Why it works: Creates a sit-up moment (they might know your peers), cannot be canned (context is unique), snaps the telemarketer stigma in ~5 seconds.
  - Failure mode: Vague context (“other companies like yours”) sounds like every other spam dial.

- **Heard The Name Tossed Around Opener**: Context, then “It’s [Name] from [Company]. Heard the name tossed around?”
  - When to use: You can name a peer group they respect (office, investor, industry logos, neighborhood). Gong-tracked ~11.24% meeting success vs 2.15% for “bad time.”
  - How: (1) Lead with the common thread. (2) Introduce yourself. (3) Ask the question as if they *should* know you. (4) Stay in character on “no.”
  - Tone: **Feet-Up** — assumptive, downtone, casual, as if calling a referral. Ban over-enunciation and upward inflection.
  - Transitions: On “no,” joke and stay loose (“Guess I’m not as popular as I thought… reason for my call is…”). On “yes,” skip the pitch: “What’d you hear?” — they still usually need the Problem Proposition.

- **Tailored Permission Opener**: Context tied to a problem you solve → own that it is a cold call → ask for 30 seconds, then they decide.
  - When to use: You found a triggering event (case win, new office, product launch, practice group) you can attach to the pitch.
  - How: Problem-relevant context (not alma mater trivia) → honest interruption (“this is a well-researched cold call”) → “Can I get 30 seconds… then you tell me if it makes sense?”
  - Tone: **Arms-Up** — nothing to hide, slightly cheeky, not chipper and not ashamed.
  - If they deny permission: They are swatting the interruption, not the product. Incentivize a reason (“so I don’t call again”), then humanize and re-ask for 30 seconds.

- **Untailored Permission-Based Opener**: Same honesty + 30-second ask, no research hook.
  - When to use: No observable context, or no research time. Gong ~11.18%. Weaker than tailored: they don’t know why you called and may interrupt mid-pitch.

## Key Concepts
- **Sit-up moment**: Instant realization you might work with their peers, so they cannot treat you like trash with no social cost.
- **Telemarketer stigma**: Chipper tone, tremble, canned openers, robotic unresearched pitch. Disavow in the first 5 seconds.
- **Banned Opener #1 — “How’s your day going?”**: False empathy; they hear “what are you selling?” Gong ~7.60%.
- **Banned Opener #2 — “Did I catch you at a bad time?”**: Of course it is. Lowers status; they say yes and vanish. Gong ~2.15%.
- **Bonus ban — “Is this Bob?”**: Confirms you are a stranger. Assume the name; they will correct you if wrong.
- **Feet-Up tone / Arms-Up tone**: Physical posture that produces peer-level delivery.
- **First 60 seconds**: Goal is to *earn* the next 60, not book the meeting yet.

## Mental Models
- Use **context before name** when you need them to listen at all.
- Think of the opener as **gaining access**, not selling.
- Use **Heard The Name** when peer proof is your strongest card; use **Tailored Permission** when a triggering event is stronger.
- Practice openers **off the phone**: 100 commute reps beat 100 live connects. Live-only practice means ~2,000 dials for 100 reps.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pleasantries to hide the cold call**: They already know. Masking it invites spite.
- **Timid “this is a cold call”**: Sounds low-status. Lean in with humor.
- **Startling or stiffening when they haven’t heard your name**: Drops the “peer” mask.
- **Alma-mater / trivia context**: Cannot attach to the Problem Proposition.
- **Practicing only on prospects**: Opener stays canned.

## Worked Example
*Heard The Name, law-firm partners (reconstructed)*

You: “Hey Bill — we work with a few other partners in the Skadden LA office. Armand from Northwestern. Heard the name tossed around?”
Bill: “No, I haven’t.”
You: “Ha — guess I’m not as popular as I thought. Reason I called you is…” *(into Problem Proposition)*

If Bill says yes: “Oh good — we’d normally be working with someone like you by now. What’d you hear?” Then pitch anyway; they still don’t know what you do.

*Tailored Permission, same world*: “I just saw the case win on the site. Gonna be honest — this is a cold call, but a well-researched one. Can I get 30 seconds on why that prompted me to call you specifically, then you tell me if it makes sense?”

## Key Takeaways
1. Ban “how’s your day” and “bad time.” They classify you as spam in five seconds.
2. Lead with specific context about *them* before anything else, including your name.
3. Heard The Name: context → intro → “tossed around?” → stay in character either way.
4. Tailored Permission: problem-tied context → own the cold call → 30-second opt-in.
5. Tone is the opener: Feet-Up (assumptive) or Arms-Up (disarmingly honest). Same words, wrong tone = telemarketer.
6. Solo-practice the opener 100 times until stutters sit in the same places on purpose.
