## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Never place a cold call. Prospect by phone with intelligence.** A cold call is a stranger, zero context, same pitch. Telephone prospecting is still how you replace lost customers and create opportunities inbound will not produce on your clock. Sales is a verb: do not wait.

**Smart Calling is three steps.** (1) Acquire intelligence on person, company, and industry *before* the decision maker. (2) Use it in a conversational, consultative process. (3) Help them take an action they feel good about and gain value from. Quality of attempts beats dial count. Take every live call as far as interest allows — the phone is not “just for appointments.”

**Possible Value Proposition (PVP).** Value is the outcome of owning/using you, with the buyer in the picture — not a slogan, product name, or “free eval.” Keep *possible* until questions confirm it for *this* person *now*. Draft from wants and avoids by title (assistant vs user vs executive). Strengthen with ease, specific cost-cuts, a felt differential advantage, numbered customer results, and inadequacy-fixes. Kill mottos that fail “what does this do for me?”

**Intelligence = facts + triggers.** Facts: what they actually sell, size, locations. Jackpot: situational pain — expansion, lawsuits, new VP, regulation, failed inspections. Elias triggers that open a window of dissatisfaction: bad experience, change/transition, new awareness (legal/risk/economic). CRM first (do not prospect existing customers). Then their site, LinkedIn, news alerts, competitor-change fields. Use intel; do not dump a biography. No creepy photo comments.

**Positive social engineering.** Talk to people other than the buyer. Identify yourself (no hiding) → ask for help → give a *because* (right person / prepare for the VP / not waste their time) → ask. Do this before the first buyer call and on every live miss. Ask anyone; do not assume a secretary “wouldn’t know.” Salvage intel from every live voice. Never misrepresent. If the boss objects, you were protecting their time from ignorant pitches.

**Screener-as-buyer.** Do not get past gatekeepers. Treat them as you would the buyer. They can buy, influence, research vendors, or make sure nobody buys from you. Who gets through? People they like. When they ask what it is about, answer immediately with results (not “postage meters”). Get their name, buyer preferences, names they drop, how the boss likes messages. Literature goes to *them*, highlighted and ugly, with a confirmed call time.

**Primary + secondary objectives (never rejected again).** Primary: what you want *them to do* (demo, board recommendation, side-by-side, order). “See if there’s interest” is not a primary. Aim at the highest conceivable honest result. Secondary: the attempt you will still make — goodwill, current vendor, permission to return, a planted future tripwire plus where to file your card. Rejection is your reaction to a no, not the no. No one rejects you without your consent. Score days on primaries plus secondaries.

**Opening: two jobs only.** Receptive mind + move to questions. Script it (a script is planned conversational words, not a robot). Four beats: you/company → one intel fact → PVP hint → weasel words (*might, depending on*) into “I’d like to ask a few questions…” Length follows relevance. Optional time mention *after* PVP, as a statement. Voicemail *is this script* plus “I’ll call Thursday” and the number twice, slowly. Do not expect a callback; repeat the same PVP imprint.

**Never in the first 10 seconds:** just; introducing yourself as the point; product without result; “hard to reach”; “as you know we’re #1”; “if I could show you a way”; “are you the person who…”; “can you help me?” to a *buyer*; apologizing for calling; thanks-for-taking-my-call; asking for a meeting or “do you have a few minutes?” before value.

**Resistant Reflex Responses.** “Not interested / all set / happy” is a duck reflex. Soften (Oh / I see / that’s not a problem) + pattern-interrupt question. Do not argue. Incumbent: better-than-satisfied, next eval, second source, tailored stay-in-touch — never “what do you like best about them?” Early price, literature, “why you,” two-minute clocks → defer into questions. Fail → secondary.

**Smart Questions, then shut up.** Do not present after the opening. Convert each possible benefit into circumstances, then assumptive scene questions (“What do you do when a customer needs it tomorrow and you don’t have it?”). Iceberg: first answer is the tip. Capture their exact words. Clarify fuzzy stalls. Pause 2–3 seconds after questions *and* after answers. Speak less. When they whisper, that is the real data. After price or an ask, do not discount or “orrrr” into the void.

**Recommend; do not pitch.** Pitching is one-way and creates objections. Transition → paraphrase their words → results they asked for (“what that means to you”) with proof and trial closes → ask. Social proof (peers’ words beat yours). Consistency: frame in what they already said. Precondition positively before price.

**Commitment = Will you?** Not “close,” not “always be closing,” not alternate-choice from the opening. Movement homework before any callback. Confirm internal champions are *personally sold* and will recommend. Ask for the order; do not describe stock and hope. Future: not “keep us in mind.” Wrap: why / you do / they do / *they* pick the date / specific phone appointment / ACTION notes / summary email. No homework = not a prospect.

**Call-flow (keep this order).** Intel → primary/secondary objective → screener or voicemail → opening → RRR if needed → Smart Questions + pause → recommend in their words → Will-you commitment → wrap with homework and a clock time. Skipping intel or asking for a meeting in sentence four is how calls die. Presenting before questions is how objections get *created*.

**Voicemail vs screener choice.** Box: leave the opening-as-message, you will call back, number twice, listen to the *entire* greeting (other office, assistant, pronunciation). Live assistant: do not hang up to “save time”; social-engineer, get the direct line, optional written echo of the voicemail. Repeat the *same* PVP on later messages. Last-resort when the pool is exhausted: if I don’t hear back I’ll assume you don’t want to discuss it. Never mystery-name-and-number or fake-cutoff teasers.

**Use X when Y (fast rules).**
- Use a **role-specific PVP** when users, purchasing, and executives share one company — they do not share one value. Users may hear “efficiency” as job loss.
- Use **trigger timing** when a vendor is acquired, a buyer changes, or a regulation hits; call with possible value, never “I bet it’s a mess.”
- Use **“Oh?”** after a no to open the real story; use **“what would have to happen so severe you’d consider someone else?”** to plant a harvestable seed.
- Use **second source / next eval / better-than-satisfied** when they are happy with an incumbent; do not ask what they love about them.
- Use **range or “it depends”** when they demand price in the opening; quote after needs.
- Use **Will you review X by Friday 11:15 when I call?** instead of May I send information. If that ask feels wrong, interest is not built yet.
- Use **ACTION notes** in the two minutes after hang-up (Authority map, Commitment, Timing, Interest, Overview) or the next call starts with “just checking in.”
- Use **direct numbers** (ask confidently, wildcards, premium intel) when trees and assistants eat the day.
- Use **landline + permission** for mobile; do not trick an assistant into handing over a cell.
- Use **ritual blocks with email off** when you are “too busy to prospect”; busyness is often the inbox chime.

**Tells you are doing it dumb.** Winging the first 10 seconds. Same pitch to every title. “Are you the person who handles…?” “Can you help me?” to the buyer. Alternate-choice close before a reason to meet. Hanging up at voicemail. Changing the PVP every touch because you are bored. Sending a kit to someone who said they are not a buyer. Discounting into silence. Reliving every no as identity.

**Head game.** Ritualize calling hours; stay in a hot zone; end the day on a win. Think big accounts and high titles (VITO) with matching prep. Ridicule call-fear into absurdity; “so what if they say no?” Avoid office cynics. Process without desire is inert.
