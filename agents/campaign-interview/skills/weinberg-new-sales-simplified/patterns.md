# Patterns — New Sales. Simplified.

## New Sales Driver diagnosis
**When to use**: New-logo miss, empty pipeline, “we need better closers.”
**How**: Map failure to A (wrong or unfocused targets), B (lame or unfired weapons), C (no plan or no execution). Confirm it is a sales problem (strategy, comp, B- talent) before coaching tactics.
**Trade-offs**: Brutal clarity; will not fix a CEO who will not choose a market.

## Finite list, then rework
**When to use**: Reps “have lots of prospects” (piles, CRMs, journals).
**How**: Print a period-sized list. Stay on it until traction. Do not redirect after one miss.
**Trade-offs**: Feels smaller than the universe; compounds expertise.

## Who/Why → lookalike list
**When to use**: Greenfield targeting or a new hire.
**How**: Best customers / why they buy / competitors / losses / near-misses / referrers. Clone what already works. Add a separate influencer list if that is the path.
**Trade-offs**: Less “creative”; higher batting average.

## Segment existing book (intentional imbalance)
**When to use**: Milk-run territories; CRM-task hamster wheels.
**How**: Largest / Most Growable / Most At-Risk / Other. Overserve 1–3.
**Trade-offs**: Some customers get less love on purpose.

## Dream-target side quest
**When to use**: You want a career-making logo.
**How**: Cap at 4–5; mini-plan; small weekly time. Keep the regular list so you are not a Prisoner of Hope.
**Trade-offs**: Low probability; high payoff if fenced.

## Aim higher, then walk down
**When to use**: Stuck with purchasing; scared of the suite.
**How**: Issue language to executives. Internal referral is a win. Never climb over a lower-level “no.”
**Trade-offs**: Requires a real story; product pitches fail upstairs.

## Arsenal audit → trio first
**When to use**: After names exist.
**How**: List weapons; master story, proactive phone, face-to-face. Everything else is supporting fire.
**Trade-offs**: Marketing toys wait until the trio works.

## Power Statement (issues → offerings → differentiators)
**When to use**: Any “what do you do?”; source copy for all weapons.
**How**: Headline; “[They] turn to us when…”; 3–7 emotional issue bullets; short offerings; ≥5 differentiators. Internal doc. So What? every line. No Issues = No Sale.
**Trade-offs**: Hours of drafting; kills generic elevator pitches.

## Sales Story Exercise
**When to use**: Story is seller-focused or inconsistent.
**How**: Three pages; call fans; cut to ~7 issues; revise for weeks; excerpt into letters/email/phone/questions.
**Trade-offs**: Needs facilitation; first draft is not done.

## Commodity antidote
**When to use**: “Do you have it / what’s your best price?”
**How**: Lead with non-price issues (access, dedicated team, ease) before price.
**Trade-offs**: Will not save a true lowest-price strategy.

## Proactive call skeleton
**When to use**: Outside hunting block.
**How**: Mindset (helper, not telemarketer) → natural voice → steal a minute → I head up → mini Power Statement → stop → ask for the visit three times (visit/fit/value). Objective = meeting. Do not overqualify lookalikes.
**Trade-offs**: Feels pushy to conflict-avoiders; that is the job.

## Voice-mail campaign
**When to use**: ~3/4 of dials.
**How**: Short issue snippet; callback + “I’ll call again”; vary; human/humor ~third; never anger.
**Trade-offs**: Multiple touches; one-and-done looks unserious.

## Pilot-in-command call
**When to use**: Every first meeting (and most later ones).
**How**: Rapport/style → share agenda + ask theirs → (existing: clean up junk) → ≤3-min Power Statement → four buckets of questions → sell in their words → fit + objections → schedule mutual next step.
**Trade-offs**: Feels structured; “organic” loses control.

## Question buckets
**When to use**: Discovery.
**How**: Personal; strategic; issue/opportunity (from the Power Statement); process (who else cares, money, criteria, alternatives). Camp on pain; don’t sell yet.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than pitching; prevents dark deals.

## Dual-objective resistance filter
**When to use**: Any outbound touch.
**How**: Primary goal + how they will perceive you. Beliefs, sound, view, feelings, issue-first words.
**Trade-offs**: Requires killing the sales voice and tire-kicker story.

## Four-slide then talk
**When to use**: You must present.
**How**: Title, agenda, “they turn to us when,” “our understanding of you.” Stop. Rank/correct. Then tailor. If you cannot write slide 4, refuse or convert the first 15 minutes to discovery.
**Trade-offs**: Guts vs. procurement theater; higher differentiation.

## Time block + fortress calendar
**When to use**: Hunting is dying of urgency.
**How**: 90–180 min blocks on the calendar first; keep them; DND; no inbox. Treat like a CEO meeting.
**Trade-offs**: You will disappoint internal time-shoppers.

## Reverse sales math
**When to use**: Goal exists, activity does not.
**How**: Closed deals ← proposals ← fits ← meetings ← conversations. Set controllable leading metrics.
**Trade-offs**: Crude averages; still beats vibes.

## Five-part personal business plan
**When to use**: Annual (and quarterly review).
**How**: Goals, strategies (attach the list), actions, obstacles, development. Present for accountability. Calendar must match.
**Trade-offs**: Useless if it lives in a drawer.

## Pre-book Sales Force One
**When to use**: Multi-city hunting.
**How**: Buy the ticket before meetings exist; fill it. Prefer change-friendly fares so last-minute second calls are possible.
**Trade-offs**: Cash committed early; forces phone work.

## Team selling without ceding process
**When to use**: Technical buyer, dream target, stuck relationship.
**How**: You still fly the plane; CEO/SME as weapons (Ch 6, Ch 15). Never repeat Frank (Ch 13).
**Trade-offs**: Prep required; unbriefed executives can wreck the call.
