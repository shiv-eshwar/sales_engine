# Patterns

## Context-First Open
**When to use**: Every live connect; also GK bypass #2 and VM #1.
**How**: Name a specific common thread (investor, office peers, geo+persona, triggering event) before your name. Specificity is the differentiator.
**Trade-offs**: Research cost vs canned openers. Untailored permission is the fallback when you cannot find a thread.

## Heard The Name Tossed Around
**When to use**: You can credibly cite their peer group. Strongest Gong opener in the book (~11.24%).
**How**: Context → “It’s [Name] from [Company]. Heard the name tossed around?” Feet-Up tone. On no: stay in character and joke into the prop. On yes: “What’d you hear?” then prop anyway.
**Trade-offs**: Feels like a flex if tone is stiff. “No” is the common path — the question is theater for *should have*, not a quiz.

## Tailored Permission Open
**When to use**: You have a problem-relevant event (launch, case win, new office).
**How**: Event → own the cold call with humor → ask 30 seconds, then they decide. Arms-Up. Denied? Incentive for why, then re-ask 30s.
**Trade-offs**: Higher research burden. Untailored permission (~11.18%) if no event — they may interrupt because they still don’t know why you called.

## Problem Proposition (80/20)
**When to use**: Immediately after any opener.
**How**: Movie-scene problem (persona/annoyance/scenery/emotion, stolen from customers) → one-sentence “we do X so it goes away” → interest CTA with softener, no-based ask, mini push-away.
**Trade-offs**: Vague “save time/money” drowns. Too much solution mechanism creates objections. Skip categories and accolades.

## One-Decision Calendar Close
**When to use**: After they validate interest.
**How**: Time *ranges* not exact slots; 1–2 week horizon; confirm email live; ask them to accept; if they want to email times, throw a dart + backups and do not reopen “whether.”
**Trade-offs**: Sending the invite live is ideal but messy mid-rush. Email ping-pong ghosts.

## Mr. Miyagi (Agree → Incentive → Test Drive)
**When to use**: Any post-pitch objection; chain until they question you.
**How**: Specific agree (micro-agree while thinking) → “so no one calls again” + multiple-choice → why meet if they never buy. Pause 2s, slow down, laugh, shrug.
**Trade-offs**: Agree-then-instant-repitch nukes trust. You will not win budget/competitor wars on the dial — only the test drive.

## Disarmingly Blunt Dismissive
**When to use**: Fake swats and pre-pitch rage (not interested, send info, “is this a cold call,” “where’d you get my number”).
**How**: Opposite energy: more honest/humorous than they expected (database screenshot, name the bad call, call the PDF lie). Pre-pitch: return to permission opener, do not test-drive.
**Trade-offs**: Timid apology escalates. Over-aggression without humor also fails.

## Hang-Up Delayed Context Callback
**When to use**: They click off.
**How**: Do not instant-redial “disconnected.” Log date, time, exact last words. ~1 week later, Tailored Permission using those three + research. Second hang-up → email/LinkedIn.
**Trade-offs**: Delayed vs zero success on the fake-disconnect redial.

## Wrong-Person Name-Drop
**When to use**: “Not my job.”
**How**: Name the title that should own it. If they were lying, real objection appears. If true, ask permission for *you* to mention them; you send “Spoke with [Name].”
**Trade-offs**: Asking a stranger to make the intro themselves usually dies.

## Trap + Social Proof (Incumbent)
**When to use**: In-house, known/unknown competitor, stuck in contract.
**How**: Agree the current way works → curious trap on the gap your differentiator hits → peers who switched. Unknown name: multiple-choice the field with two *different* incentives. Contract: no rip-and-replace today.
**Trade-offs**: Gotcha tone makes them defend the diet. Badmouthing competitors is worse than silence.

## Gatekeeper Triple Bypass
**When to use**: EA/front desk.
**How**: Slide-by (first name, gruff, question back) → context not product → peer proof + “I sent a note.” No lies, no pitch, no schmooze. 1–2 full shutdowns → mobile/off-hours/other channel (GK ≈ −39% success).
**Trade-offs**: Under-revealing can feel rude if tone is wrong; over-revealing trips the solicitor alarm.

## Double Tap VM → Email
**When to use**: Sequence. Not every NA.
**How**: Two VMs max, name last, never ask for a callback, never pitch. VM1 context; VM2 + proof. Email 1 = full Problem Prop; later emails are pointers (“left a VM”). Rotate numbers (VM ≈ −28% future connect, ≈2× email reply).
**Trade-offs**: Time vs 25% more dials if you skip VMs. Third VM is wasted.

## Golden 3 List Hygiene
**When to use**: Before adding volume.
**How**: Mobiles/directs; tag numbers; 5-in-4-weeks; two VMs; spam registry/rotation/business hours. DQ in 30s; A/B/C with timing on top; whole-account, call high; extra dials on A/B; intent jumps the queue. Confirms assume-on; no-show = punt invite, don’t delete.
**Trade-offs**: Research time vs Kenny-style 400 empty dials. Parallel dialers trade context for connects — OK only in huge territories.

## Machine Block (10 / 40 / Green)
**When to use**: Daily, especially full-cycle calendars.
**How**: 10 dials before other morning rituals. Green 8–12 prospecting (calls at 9, then email, flex at 1). Inbox 8:30/12/3. Never delete a Green dial block — move it inside Green. 60 prepped tasks, 60 minutes, 40 dials, DND, no linger. AE ~150–200/week; SDR ~300–400; never lower. Public consequence.
**Trade-offs**: Morning customer demand exists — gaps only, then relocate the block. 30-minute blitzes never exit warm-up.

## Reluctance Contract
**When to use**: Sweat, hoping for VM, cutting after one win, excuse-making.
**How**: Confidence = input→output belief. MV tracks + ~50 verbal reps, then stop preparing. Calls first, daily, weekly number + stick. When it sucks, that’s the moat.
**Trade-offs**: More books/scripts/research feel like work and delay evidence.
