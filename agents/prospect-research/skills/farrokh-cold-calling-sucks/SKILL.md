## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

Success in sales is the number of uncomfortable conversations you will have. Cold calling sucks, and that’s why it works: few reps will do it consistently, so the field is thin. Emails can make quota; calls make President’s Club. Formula: **Opener → Problem Proposition → Objections → Meeting.** Most calls are won or lost in the first 60 seconds — that minute’s only job is to *earn* the next 60.

**Gong stakes (800 dials):** average ~43 connects / 2 meetings vs top quartile ~106 / 18. Top 10% ~1 meeting per 3 connects. Calls also roughly double later email replies even without a live connect. Do not use this skill as an excuse to delay dialing — MV talk tracks + live reps beat more theory.

### Openers — context before name (Ch 1)
Use a **context-first opener** when they pick up. First words prove you are not a stranger (investor, office peers, geo+persona, triggering event). That creates a **sit-up moment**, cannot be canned, and snaps telemarketer stigma in ~5 seconds.

- Use **Heard The Name Tossed Around** when you can name a peer group: context → intro → “Heard the name tossed around?” **Feet-Up** tone (assumptive, downtone, as if a referral). Gong ~11.24%. On “no,” stay in character and joke into the prop. On “yes,” “What’d you hear?” — they still need the prop.
- Use **Tailored Permission** when a problem-relevant event exists: event → own that it is a well-researched cold call (**Arms-Up**, not ashamed) → 30 seconds then *they* decide. Denied permission = swatting the interruption; incentivize a why, re-ask 30s.
- Fallback: **untailored permission** (~11.18%) when you have no thread.

**Never:** “How’s your day going?” (~7.60%), “Did I catch you at a bad time?” (~2.15%), “Is this Bob?” Assume the name.

### Problem Proposition — dump the value prop (Ch 2)
Use a **Problem Proposition** instead of buzzword pitches (Gong: problem language ~16% vs social proof ~12% vs buzzwords ~5.5%). People act on pain; value has no context without a problem.

1. **Triggering problem** (~80%): movie scene — persona, annoyances, scenery, emotion — in *customer* language. Split **at / above / other-department** power line; same product, different movie. “Save time/money/risk” is too generic.
2. **One-sentence solution:** “We do X so the problem goes away.” X = the differentiator that *allows* the fix. Too little = unbelievable; too much = you spawn objections. No categories (“we’re a CRM”) — if they have one, they dismiss you. No random accolades.
3. **Interest-based CTA:** one decision only. Soften, prefer **no-based** asks, **mini push-away** (“my guess is you’re all set”). Then book: **time ranges**, within 1–2 weeks, confirm email live, explicit **accept**, **throw a dart** + backups if they want to “email times.”

Tone: join the ridiculousness of the problem; brush the solution; shrug the CTA.

### Mr. Miyagi — don’t fight the punch (Ch 3–4)
The first objection is almost always a **knee-jerk at the interruption**, not a product verdict. Do not counter with logic.

1. **Agree** with the *specific* objection (micro-agree while you think). Pause ~2s; go slower than you think; laugh on nasty ones.
2. **Incentivize conversation** — “so no one calls again” + **multiple-choice** that piles onto *their* side.
3. **Sell the test drive**, not the car: why meet if they never buy. Generic fallback: not for now, so they know what’s out there.

**Chain it.** Median successful call ~4.8 minutes; expect 2+ objections. Book when they start asking *you* questions.

Gong mix: ~49.5% dismissive / ~42.6% situational / ~7.9% existing-solution. Top 5 ≈ 74%. Do **not** memorize 18 scripts — master buckets:

| Bucket | When | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Dismissive | Fake swat | Disarmingly blunt; reveal the real objection; Miyagi that |
| Pre-pitch dismissive | Before they know what you sell | Blunt humor; pivot to permission opener — no test drive. Hang-up: log date/time/last words, call ~1 week later; don’t fake “disconnected” |
| Situational | Budget, price, bandwidth, hire, fit | Let them win today; get them in the car; don’t lock discounts |
| Existing solution | In-house, competitor, contract | Agree it works → **trap question** → peer social proof. Never badmouth. Contract: no rip-and-replace today |
| Wrong person | “Not my job” | Name the right title; **you** name-drop-email; don’t ask a stranger to intro you |

### Gatekeepers & voicemail (Ch 5)
Act like you belong (Drake’s aunt). **Don’t** befriend or pitch the EA. **Triple Bypass:** **Slide-by** (first name, gruff, question back) → **context** not product → **social proof** + “I sent a note.” No identity lies. GK ≈ **−39%** success; after 1–2 shutdowns use mobile, off-hours, or another channel.

**Double Tap VMs** exist to lift **email** (~2.73% → ~5.87%), not callbacks. Case against is real (time; **−28%** future connect if you out yourself as sales) — still leave **exactly two**, rotate numbers. Name last, never pitch, never “call me back.” VM1 ~15s context; VM2 ~30s + proof. Email 1 = the Problem Prop; later emails/VMs only point back.

### Golden 3 — juice per dial (Ch 6)
Meetings kept = dials × connect × set × show. Same 200 dials/week × 4 weeks, top-quartile Golden 3 vs average ≈ **13×**. Activity-board leaders are often conversion losers.

- **Connect:** mobiles/directs; **mark tracks** (tree codes, dead corporates); **5 dials in 4 weeks** then rest; two-VM cap; register/rotate/test numbers; business hours (8 a.m.–9 p.m. cited); never emergency-double, *67, FaceTime.
- **Set:** disqualify in **30 seconds** → A (problem *now*) / B (problem) / C (ICP) → extra two dials on A/B → whole account, call high → intent (5× opens, pricing page, objection reply, new event) jumps the queue.
- **Show:** day-before **assume-on** + a prepared detail (plus week-before if ≥2 weeks). Late: email 2 / call 5 / call 8. No-show: **punt the invite 3–4 days**, don’t delete.

**Parallel dialer:** huge territory yes (connects compensate); small enterprise no (you lose context-first opens).

### Volume without junk (Ch 7)
Perfect time to call = **when you will actually call**. Consistency beats “Thursday 3 p.m.” studies; one skipped blitz ≈ 40 lost dials. Gong: morning is also the best window.

- **10 Dial No Pee:** hardest thing first. You don’t need to feel good to start; you need to start to feel good.
- **Green 8–12** prospecting (calls at 9, then email, flex ~1) / **Yellow 12–3** customers / **Red 3–6** admin. Inbox **8:30 / 12 / 3** only. If a customer takes a Green slot, **move** the dial block inside Green — never delete it. Night-before: ≥**60 tasks for 40 dials**.
- **40 in 60:** research already done; DND; no linger; never a 30-minute “blitz” (first 5 are the worst 5). AE **~150–200 dials/week (1h/day)**; SDR **~300–400 (2h/day)**. Raise if you need meetings; **never lower** (roller coaster).

### Kill reluctance (Ch 8)
Reluctance is **confidence** = belief that inputs produce outputs. You cannot manufacture it with more books, longer scripts, extra research, or affirmations. Prove it until rejection is funny and “~50 dials ≈ 1 meeting” is felt.

**Contract:** MV tracks + ~50 verbal reps, then (1) calls before anything else, (2) every day — no vote, (3) weekly number + **consequence**. When it sucks, average reps invent excuses (calling is dead, EoQ, Monday/Friday myths, double tomorrow). Club reps feel the same suck and dial — that’s the moat.

Practice off-phone: opener ×100; agree+incentivize top 4 objections ×25 *before* collecting tracks; one objection per week ×~30.
