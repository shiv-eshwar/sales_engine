# Chapter 9: Opening Statements: What to Avoid to Minimize Resistance

## Core Idea
Most prospecting calls die in the first 10 seconds because of what the rep says. Do not screw it up. Strip words that trigger “get rid of this salesperson” and you raise the odds of a receptive mind — the actual job of the opening.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Don’t-say list (first 10 seconds)**: Use as a pre-call edit of every script.
  - How: Delete or rewrite anything on the list below. Goal is minimize chance of resistance, not claim magic words work 100% of the time. Delivery can save a bad line for a rare personality; do not bet your pipeline on that.
  - Why it works: Openings create the same emotion as “this chapter is about HVAC wiring” vs. “this chapter is how you get another week of paid vacation.” Boring/irrelevant → extract. Personal result → tell me more.

- **Name discipline**: Use before you ever greet.
  - How: Confirm pronunciation (pronouncenames.com; ask someone at the company — not “what’s the *correct* pronunciation”). Ask how they prefer to be addressed. Use the name as written and as they answer. Do not rename David to Dave. Do not joke about unusual names unless they go first.

- **HAYT (“How are you today?”)**: Optional icebreaker, not a rule.
  - How: ~30% always, ~30% never, ~40% situational. If you use it: mean it, listen, react. Prefer a fresher variant (“How’s your Tuesday so far?” / “You sound like you’re having a good day”). Skip if it sounds forced. Some teams even name a rough morning they hear in the voice — high risk, sometimes opens talk.

## Key Concepts
- **Don’t say “just”**: Weakens everything after it.
- **Don’t “introduce myself and company”** as the point of the call. Not a mixer; they care about themselves.
- **Don’t name products/services without a result**: Easy “we already have that.”
- **Don’t “you’re a hard guy to reach”**: Wastes seconds; labels you a pest.
- **Don’t sound salesy**: “As you know, we’re the top…” “I’m sure you’d agree…” “If I could show you a way… you’d want that, wouldn’t you?”
- **Don’t “are you the person in charge of…?”**: Announces a sales call. You should already know from intel. Burns the seven-second window.
- **Don’t “can you help me?” to a decision maker**: Their job is not to help you; yours is to help them. That line is for screeners (Ch 8).
- **Don’t apologize for wanting to help** or “I know you’re busy, sorry for interrupting.”
- **Don’t “thanks for taking my call”** in the opening — can signal unworthiness and burns time.
- **Don’t ask or hint at a decision** (appointment, marriage-in-a-bar equivalent) before interest exists.
- **Don’t lead with “do you have a few minutes?”** before value (time issue revisited in Ch 10 — after PVP, as a statement, if at all).

## Mental Models
- Use the **vacation-week vs. ductwork** test on every sentence: does this make them lean in or look for the exit?
- Think of the opening as **resistance minimization**, not persuasion yet.
- Prefer **already knowing the decision maker** over asking them to qualify themselves as a target.
- Treat **small talk as optional seasoning**, never a substitute for PVP.

## Anti-patterns
- **Winging the first 10 seconds** (the highest-leverage words on the call).
- **Copying a line because one charismatic rep can sell with it.**
- **Commenting on unusual names** (“that’s a strange name”).
- **HAYT as a record-needle skip**: they answer; you steamroll with “I’m fine, the reason I’m calling…”
- **Asking the decision maker to help you** find the right person after you have already interrupted them.

## Worked Example
Failure stack: “Hi Pat, Dale with Ace. Can you help me?” → “What do you need?” → “I was just wondering, are you the person responsible for secure storage of paper records?” → “I am, but we’re all set.” Fifteen seconds of suspicion confirmed.

Edit: Drop help-ask, drop “just,” drop “are you the person.” Open with intel + possible result + request to ask questions (Ch 10). If you truly do not know the title, social-engineer it *before* this voice (Ch 4), not in the opening.

HAYT failure: “How are you doing today?” / “Fantastic.” / “I’m doing fine, thank you, the reason I’m calling is…” Listener checked out. If you ask, hear the answer.

## Key Takeaways
1. Edit the first 10 seconds against the don’t-say list before you dial.
2. Results, not products; their world, not your introduction.
3. Never qualify “are you the person?” on a Smart Call opening.
4. Help-asks are for screeners; decision makers need a reason to stay.
5. Do not apologize, self-minimize, or ask for a decision before interest.
6. Get the name right; do not improvise nicknames or jokes.
7. HAYT only if it is real for you — and then listen.
