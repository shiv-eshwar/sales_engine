# Chapter 14: Recommending the Next Step

## Core Idea
Pitching is for sports. On a Smart Call you recommend — and only after they have told you what they need. Objections are created by pitches. A recommendation is on-target because it is built from their answers.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Recommendation vs. pitch**: Use this vocabulary on purpose.
  - How: A pitch is one-way “puking” / “spray and pray.” Words follow thoughts: if you call it a pitch, you will present what *you* want to say. Recommendation requires information; information requires questions.

- **When to recommend**: Use when you feel they are receptive to hearing how you can help — after enough questions, not after a clock. Could be a few questions or an hour. Variables: what you sell, objectives, whether they were already looking.

- **Smart Call recommendation process** (four moves):
  1. **Transition**: Done questioning; spark listen-again interest (“based on what you told me about X, I believe I have something that…”).
  2. **Paraphrase understanding**: Their needs, concerns, desires, values — check “is that right?”
  3. **Recommend results + next step**: Only what they said they care about. Use “what that means to you” / “which means.” Proof for each claim. **Trial close** after chunks: would that work; sound good so far; would that save you money?
  4. **Get commitment**: Seamless into Ch 15 — the next action (meeting, webinar, sale, etc.).

- **Social proof + “people are interested in other people”**: Use when making a claim.
  - How: Cialdini: most people imitate; others’ actions/words beat our claims. Peer stories, counted similar firms, named quotes (with permission/ethics). Amazon’s “customers who bought this also bought” is the same lever.

- **Principle of consistency**: Use their earlier answers as the frame.
  - How: Russert-style: people want to stay consistent with what they already said. Preface benefits with their words; ask commitment in their words (“would that help your reps ask the right questions to build value?”).

- **Positive preconditioning**: Use before price or the “best part.”
  - How: Not “bad news / sitting down / you’re not going to like this.” Do: “here’s the good news”; “keeping in mind the operational savings, the price is low in comparison”; “this is the part people say is best”; “you might want to write this down.” It’s what you say *before* you say it. They evaluate information in the state you put them in.

## Key Concepts
- **Echo their terminology** (Spreda): If they repeat “safety and ergonomics,” recommend in those words.
- **Sensory, concrete proof**: Memorize numbered examples of results others already got.
- **Trial close ≠ the big close**: Micro-agreements while you recommend.

## Mental Models
- Use **question → paraphrase → results → ask**, never **monologue → hope**.
- Think of a pitch as **trying to strike the hitter out**; a recommendation as **building the score they already asked for**.
- Prefer **their quote as the headline** of your recommendation.
- Condition the room **positive** before numbers and asks.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling it a pitch** and behaving like one.
- **Presenting every benefit on the sheet** including ones they never mentioned.
- **Negative preconditioning** before price.
- **Skipping paraphrase** so they do not hear themselves in your plan.
- **No trial closes**, then a shocking big ask.

## Worked Example
Insurance: Transition — “Based on what you said about coverage gaps and added liability now that you manufacture toxic chemicals, I believe we can cover you fully at about the premium you pay now.” Paraphrase — neglected except at renewal; premium feels outrageous for the new exposure; confirm. Recommend — dedicated account rep (addresses neglect), coverage programs mapped to the exposure, each result with proof, trial closes. Commitment — “You’d show a savings from day one. It would make sense to sit down on the details. Would Friday morning work?”

Consistency: They said reps lack confidence to hold price because they cannot ask value questions; they dollarized leakage. Recommendation opens with that sentence, then the workshop mechanics, then “do you feel that would help them ask those questions and keep more of what’s on the table?”

## Key Takeaways
1. Recommend; do not pitch. Questions first, always.
2. Transition, paraphrase, results-only recommendation, then commitment.
3. Translate features with “what that means to you”; trial-close each chunk.
4. Use peer proof and their exact words (social proof + consistency).
5. Precondition positively before price and key claims.
6. Echo their jargon; drop yours if it does not match.
7. The “right time” is receptivity, not a slide number.
