# Chapter 10: Creating Interest with Your Smart Call Opening Statement

## Core Idea
The opening has two jobs only: move them into a positive, receptive frame of mind, and move them into questioning. Script it. The worst time to think of the words is as they leave your mouth.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Jim Furyk Theory**: Use when you worry there is only one “legal” opening.
  - How: Furyk’s swing is ugly; the club is square at impact. You may shape Sobczak’s process to your mouth, but the club-head conditions stay: relevance, possible value, invitation to questions, minimized resistance.

- **Interest-creating opening (four beats)**: Use on every live buyer greeting and as the body of voicemail.
  1. Introduce yourself and organization.
  2. Use Smart Call intelligence (the proof you are not a typical cold caller).
  3. Hint at the Possible Value Proposition, tailored to their world.
  4. Suggest more possible value *contingent on* moving to questions (“I’d like to ask a few questions to see if I could provide some information”).
  - Why it works: Curiosity without a pitch. Weasel words keep you from sounding like a cocky closer.
  - Failure mode: Presenting, asking for a meeting, or dumping products here (Ch 9).

- **Script as professional draft**: Use for opening and voicemail.
  - How: A script is the conversational combination of well-planned words that, delivered naturally, get the response you want. Actors use scripts; winging is submitting a rough draft to the most important publication in your career. Write, edit, practice, deliver.

- **Weasel / contingency words**: Use in beat 4.
  - How: might, maybe, perhaps, depending on — then into questions. Avoid the declarative “we will save you 30%” in the opening (Ch 9).

- **Time mention (optional)**: If you respect time out loud, do it *after* PVP, preferably as a statement: “and if I’ve reached you at a good time, I’d like to ask a few questions.” Not “Do you have a few moments?” first. Optional Jim Furyk line some reps use successfully: “I’ll be brief, if that’s okay with you.”

## Key Concepts
- **Intel stems**: speaking with [name]; congratulations on [news]; following your Twitter/LinkedIn; website; major initiative this year.
- **PVP stems**: we specialize in helping [role] with [issue]; other [peers] we’ve helped to [result]; sounds like you might be experiencing [X].
- **Ending stems**: see if we should talk further; determine if it’s worth a look; find out if it might be of value; see if it might be an option.
- **Length rule**: As much as you can with the fewest words. Every word must add impact. Too long = poorly delivered or irrelevant — not a stopwatch. Laser-targeted detail is not long; one irrelevant sentence is.

## Mental Models
- Use the opening to **earn questions**, not to earn a yes.
- Think of winging it as **publishing the first draft**.
- Prefer **contingent value** (“depending on… we might…”) over premature certainty.
- Treat voicemail as **this script with a callback close** (Ch 7).

## Anti-patterns
- **Unscripted ramble** because “scripts sound canned.” Canned delivery of a bad script sounds canned; preparation does not.
- **Asking for the appointment in sentence four.**
- **Leading with the time-ask** before they know why the minutes matter.
- **Wordy filler** that does not change the picture in their head.

## Worked Example
Pediatric dentist (intel: coupons in the school paper + office manager on marketing): “Dr. Moeller, Stephen with Smile Marketing. I noticed the school-paper coupons and spoke with your office manager about how you’re building the practice. We’ve helped other pediatric dentists cut new-patient acquisition cost about 50 percent while increasing new patients about 25 percent in six months. Depending on your goals, we might have a few options worth a look.”

Construction / failed inspections: “Jack, Zack with Hometown Construction. City permit reports showed properties you manage. We know the inspectors here and specialize in bringing failed properties to pass. If this is a good time, I’d like to ask a few questions.”

Voicemail variant: same body, then “I’ll call Friday morning; if you’d like me sooner, my number is…”

## Key Takeaways
1. Two objectives: receptive mind + permission to question.
2. Four beats: you/company → intel → PVP hint → questions as the next step.
3. Write and rehearse; do not wing the highest-leverage 10 seconds.
4. Weasel words and “depending on” keep you consultative.
5. Mention time only after value, if at all.
6. Length follows relevance; edit words that do not add impact.
7. This script *is* your voicemail; only the last line changes.
