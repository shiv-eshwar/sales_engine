# Chapter 19: More Smart Calling Success Tips

## Core Idea
A toolkit of small, high-leverage habits: find names, map power, follow buyers, protect focus, and respect the medium (especially mobile). None replace the core process; they keep the process from leaking.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Email-the-org to find the buyer**: Use when screeners will not name the person.
  - How: Contact-us or department inbox: you could not find the Director of Finance on the site; please reply with name so you can contact them directly.

- **Start-up referral**: Use when you hit a new company.
  - How: Ask who to talk to at their *previous* employer — often a similar buyer still buying similar items.

- **Decision-making org chart**: Use on every multi-threaded deal.
  - How: “Who else will be involved in the final purchase?” Draw boxes; add tidbits after each call; reread before the next dial so you inhabit their environment.

- **“So Jerry works for you?”**: Use when a name drops instead of “are you the decision maker?”
  - How: They correct you with title and hierarchy. Write every name; “You mentioned Gary — who is he?”

- **Follow the buyer**: Use when an account is shutting down.
  - How: “Where are you going to go?” Open the new company with a known user.

- **Internal referrals once you are in**: Use with multi-location/multi-department customers.
  - How: The hard part (getting in) is done. “Who else uses/does X? Other departments or locations? If I find them, may I use your name?”

- **Said vs. meant notes** (Feil): Use while listening.
  - How: Two columns: what they said / how they said it (tentative, squeamish, something else not discussed). Budget delay may be fear.

## Key Concepts
- **Calling-card / unknown caller ID**: One field tip to reach avoiders. Use ethically; it is a connect tactic, not a personality.
- **Hold discipline**: Do not chat with the next desk. You lose the thread; they hear you mid-sentence with someone else and intimacy dies.
- **Cell phones as sell phones**: Default — call mobile only with permission or if it is the only number (tiny business). Do not trick assistants into handing over the cell. If voicemail offers the cell, treat as last resort. Confirm “is that how she prefers to take calls?” before using a volunteered mobile. You: prefer landline for important money conversations; mobile/Skype audio quality can handicap a deal already missing video. Cheesy “how ya doin’ old buddy” on a harvested cell creates anger.
- **Your voicemail greeting**: Not “unable to come to the phone” (fallen-and-can’t-get-up). Where you are, when you return, who to hit for help now.
- **Don’t invite interruptions**: Email chimes during ritual call blocks (Ch 6). Offline or scheduled checks; kill pop-ups. You cannot hit dial numbers while servicing the inbox alarm.

## Mental Models
- Use **every spoken name** as a node on the map.
- Think of **mobile as their living room**; do not walk in uninvited.
- Prefer **landline intimacy** when asking for real money.
- Protect **call ritual** like a locked door; notifications are picklocks.

## Anti-patterns
- **“What’s your title / are you the decision maker?”** as a blunt instrument.
- **One contact in a company with five plants.**
- **Prospecting cells obtained by pretending you already know them.**
- **Skype-from-a-coffee-shop** for a five-figure close.
- **Empty “I’m unavailable” greetings.**
- **Live email during the calling block.**
- **Notes that capture words but not the wobble in the voice.**

## Worked Example
Name drop: “I’ll run this by Jerry Smith.” → “So Jerry works for you?” → “No, he’s VP of Finance.” Chart updated; next call includes Jerry’s world.

Closing plant: Customer is shutting down. “Where are you going?” New employer, same need, warm thread.

Interrupt audit: Five-minute floor conversation, two email chimes, two whirls to the screen. That is why the dial quota fails. Alarm off; mail at 11:30 and 4:00 only.

## Key Takeaways
1. Hunt names via site email, start-up alumni, and every mentioned human.
2. Draw and update the decision chart; never blunt-force “are you the DM?”
3. Follow buyers to the next company; farm internal referrals after you are in.
4. Confirm mobile permission; you dial from a landline when it matters.
5. Stay present on hold; run a useful personal greeting.
6. Shut email during ritual calling.
7. Note what they meant, not only what they said.
