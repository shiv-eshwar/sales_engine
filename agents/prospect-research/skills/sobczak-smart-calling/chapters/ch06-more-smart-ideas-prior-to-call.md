# Chapter 6: More Smart Ideas for Prior to Your Call

## Core Idea
Ritualize calling, stay in your success zone, and use timing and access tactics that increase live connects — without waiting for a “best day” study to give you permission to avoid the phone.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Ritualized phone time**: Use whenever prospecting is a real job, not a mood.
  - How: Appointment with yourself, set hours, no email, no proposals during that block. Pre-plan when possible so you stay in the zone. Same logic as a 5:30 a.m. gym ritual: missing once becomes missing the week.
  - Failure mode: “I’ll call when I feel like it” or mixing admin into the block so momentum dies.

- **Best time is when you are calling (plus your data)**: Use when someone quotes Thursday 8–9 a.m. as gospel.
  - How: Oldroyd’s large-sample pattern (Thursday; 8–9 a.m. then 4–5 p.m.) is interesting, not a cage. Industry common sense (not restaurants at lunch; contractors at 6 a.m. or after dark; plant-floor people at start, breaks, lunch, quitting time). Measure your own connects. If you are not calling, you are not uncovering opportunities.

- **Stay in the zone / end with a positive**: Use when calls are working — and at day’s end.
  - How: On a hot streak, do not stop to admire it; keep dialing. End the day on a win (objective accomplished, not necessarily a sale) so tomorrow starts high. One client: you cannot leave until the last call is positive.

- **Call when others will not**: Use around holidays, summer, week between Christmas and New Year.
  - How: Some buyers are in, gatekeepers are out, mood is lighter. Empty chairs are no worse than voicemail.

## Key Concepts
- **No universal metric**: Calls-to-appointment averages across unlike sales are meaningless. Do not outsource judgment to a study.
- **Multimedia pre-call**: Short recorded voice + slides + their LinkedIn photo (e.g. PointAcross-style tools) can beat a plain email when you can monitor opens.
- **Gimmick mail**: Coffee/mug, shoe (“foot in the door”), lumpy mail, crumpled catalog “don’t throw this away again,” cheap cell phone with a call-me-at-3-Thursday note. Fun when they work; you will not build a career on trick plays. Use after conventional Smart Calling is exhausted on a must-win account.
- **Direct numbers**: Premium intel tools; ask confidently in social engineering (“And what is her direct number?”); Google wildcard `480-***-****` with name/title; email ↔ number reverse search; Audix-style directory tricks when the system allows (enter last name, get extension).
- **Listen to the whole greeting**: Locations, assistant names, return dates, name pronunciation.

## Mental Models
- Use **ritual** the way athletes use practice time: identity, not inspiration.
- Think of **published “best times”** as someone else’s averages — dangerous if they become an excuse not to call.
- Prefer **direct dial** over navigating trees; invest in getting the number.
- Treat **gimmicks as last-resort differentiation**, not the Smart Calling process.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling only at the “best” hour** and writing off the rest of the week.
- **Breaking a hot streak** to celebrate or do admin.
- **Ending the day on a sour call** and starting tomorrow in that residue.
- **Shy, guilty ask for a direct line.**
- **Building your pipeline on $20 cell phones and garage-sale gimmicks.**
- **Hanging up at the first syllable of voicemail** (you miss “I’m in Boston, call this number”).

## Worked Example
Plant-floor timing: Reception or pattern (break ~two hours after start, lunch two hours later) tells you when industrial buyers are near a phone. Ritual: those windows are on the calendar, not “when I get to it.”

Direct-number stack: LinkedIn gives name/title → Google `Name Title 480-***-****` → dial. If voicemail is Audix, directory by last name yields the 10-digit extension you should have logged the first time.

Must-win gimmick (after Smart Calling stalled): Deutsch-style sequential objects with a message each, or a disposable phone and a specific call time. Use only when the account is existential and normal channels failed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Block ritual calling time and protect it from email and proposals.
2. Best time = when you consistently call, adjusted for industry reality and your own stats.
3. When you are hot, stay in the zone; end the day on a win.
4. Call through holidays and “dead” weeks; someone is working.
5. Get direct numbers confidently (tools, ask, wildcards, voicemail directories).
6. Listen to entire greetings; log the map through their phone tree.
7. Unusual pre-call objects are optional last resorts, not the system.
