# Chapter 15: Getting Commitment for the Next Action

## Core Idea
Stop “closing.” Closing implies an end; you want movement. Commitment is the validation of the call so far — easy if you moved the ball to the one-inch line. Attitude (ask more, ask larger) beats gimmick closes.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Commitment, not close**: Use as the name of this phase.
  - How: Every Smart Call needs the prospect to commit to doing something between now and the next contact (or to buy, meet, or a future pledge). If trainers say “we need closing techniques,” suspect the real gap is not reaching a point where asking is appropriate.

- **Asking attitude over technique**: Use when you hesitate.
  - How: Alternate-choice (“Wednesday at 3:30 or Thursday at 9?”) without value is long-bombing from your own 20. Glengarry “always be closing” is nonsense. Get out of comfort; ask more in sales *and* in life for a week so “just ask” becomes muscle.

- **Four commitment types**:
  1. **Movement**: Never schedule a follow-up until they commit to an action before it (review catalog and mark items, complete a demo, pull sales figures). “What will happen between now and our next contact?” “By when will you have gone through the material?”
  2. **Meet**: After the call has gone far enough that the visit is with a hot prospect. Plain: “It would make sense to get together. Are you available next Wednesday?”
  3. **Buy**: Ask, don’t wish. “I have these in stock” ≠ “The K-100 would cut processing time; I can ship today. May I?”
  4. **Future**: Not “keep us in mind.” “If you change vendors before my next call, will you call me?” “When you send the RFP, may I be included?”

- **Sold-personally chain**: Use when they must take it to someone else.
  - How: “Are you personally sold?” / “If it were only you, would we be the ones?” If yes: “Are you going to recommend it at the committee meeting?” Do not let unsold messengers sell for you.

- **Will you? vs. May I?**: Use at every wrap.
  - How: Sinclair restaurants: “Please call if plans change” vs. “Will you call if plans change?” No-shows 30% → 10%. “May I send information?” is permission. “If I send this, will you review it so we can talk in two weeks?” is commitment. If asking that feels wrong, you have not built enough interest.

## Key Concepts
- **Wishing vs. asking**: Electronics clerk who describes models while a ready buyer waits. “Do you want me to buy this?” should not have to come from the customer.
- **Fax-the-contract false close**: “I’ll fax the contract, okay?” does not get a signed contract. Add “When will you sign it and send it back with the deposit?”
- **Nonthreatening words**: “Own” vs. “buy” when purchase language creates friction; “why not try the deluxe” (trying, not buying).
- **Fear = False Evidence Appearing Real**: “What’s the worst thing that happens if you do this?” “If you do nothing, then where are you?”
- **Theory of contrast**: Ask for more than you expect (or start at full price if you can discount). Raises the standard; other side feels a win. Always start high if discounting is allowed; trade concessions for larger orders.

## Mental Models
- Use football field position: **do not throw the Hail Mary from your 20**; earn the inch, then score.
- Think of every callback you accept **without homework** as unpaid intern work for a maybe.
- Prefer **Will you?** which creates obligation, over **May I?** which leaves the ball in your court only.
- Ask big; contrast makes the landing feel small.

## Anti-patterns
- **Technique catalogs** (which day is better?) before value.
- **“Always be closing”** as a substitute for process.
- **Announcing availability** and hoping they self-checkout.
- **Keep us in mind.**
- **Permission asks** that they forget the moment they hang up.
- **Letting a maybe-champion present you without confirming they are sold.**

## Worked Example
Weak: “I just want you to know we have several models in stock.”
Strong: “Based on what you said, the K-100 would cut processing time. I have one in stock and could ship today. May I do that?”

Internal seller: “Are you personally sold on this?” → “Yes.” → “Will you recommend our proposal at Thursday’s committee?” If no, stay in questions/recommendation; do not book a hope-call.

Restaurant rule applied: “If I email the comparison, will you review the highlighted page before Friday at 11:15 when I call?” Calendar both sides; they own an action.

## Key Takeaways
1. Seek commitment and movement, not a theatrical close.
2. Asking attitude > cute techniques; practice asking in the rest of life too.
3. Get a between-call action before you agree to call again.
4. Confirm the internal champion is personally sold and will recommend.
5. Ask for the business in a specific sentence; do not describe inventory and wait.
6. Replace May I / please with Will you.
7. Use contrast, worst-case questions, and nonthreatening verbs to lower irrational fear.
