# Chapter 8: Working with Screeners, Gatekeepers, and Assistants

## Core Idea
Do not get past, around, over, or under screeners. Treat the screener as you would the buyer. Their job is to protect the decision maker’s time; your job is to help them see you as worth an audience — and to gather intel while you do it.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Screener-as-buyer**: Use on every first live reach through an assistant.
  - How: Same social-engineering beats (identify, help, justification, questions). You are helping them do their job. Top answer to “who gets through?” in Sobczak’s interviews: **people I like**.
  - Why it works: They can often buy, influence, or research vendors. They can always make sure nobody buys from you. Elusiveness lowers your value in their eyes.
  - Failure mode: Strong-arm scripts from old literature. They spot scams instantly; higher-level assistants are better at it.

- **Sell the screener when probed**: Use when you hear “What is this in reference to?”
  - How: Immediate, complete, results-based justification — a cousin of your opening. Not product (“postage meters”) but outcome (cut cost of renewal statements and mailings). Invite them to help with information.
  - Failure mode: Shadowboxing. If a bozo wastes the boss, the screener gets “why did you let that through?”

- **Pretty-ugly literature via the gatekeeper** (Domanski): Use when they truly must see material first.
  - How: Address the package to the gatekeeper (less mail, opens sooner); handwritten plain envelope; highlight, margin notes, arrows, flags; Post-it instead of “further to our conversation of…” Confirm the phone appointment on the note. Follow up: “the brochure probably looked pretty ugly — I wanted to save you time.”

## Key Concepts
- **Rejected tactics**: Give no information; answer abruptly and demand a transfer; “it’s a personal business matter”; “would Ms. Bigg approve of you denying her this opportunity?”
- **Tone**: Conversational and confident, not sloppy, pushy, or condescending.
- **Weak availability questions**: “Is she available or busy / in a meeting / doing something important?” Minimizes you.
- **Get their name**; use it next time.
- **Buyer preferences**: How they like to be addressed, small talk vs. blunt, topics to avoid.
- **Names you hear**: Jot, then “You mentioned Steve — what does he do?” Ask who the boss reports to.
- **“Is this a sales call?”**: “I don’t know yet. If there’s a fit it might be; otherwise not.” Then PVP + questions to see if there is a basis for conversation.
- **Send-info brush-off**: “One purpose of the call is to see if information is worthwhile — that’s why I’d like to speak with him first.”
- **Message channel**: “How does she like to get messages?” Voice mail vs. screener vs. page.
- **Will you see her?** Ask them to tell her personally you want to speak today; you have an idea she might want to know.
- **Start high**: Begin a level above typical buyers; get referred down with “I was speaking with Karen at Mr. McNabb’s office…”
- **Vacation**: Learn return date; do not add to the pile; call a couple of days after they are back; ask about the trip.
- **Visit the screener** if you are ever on site; thank them.

## Mental Models
- Use **like + value** as the through-filter: rapport and a reason the boss’s time is protected, not stolen.
- Think of the assistant as **CEO of access**.
- Prefer **results language** with screeners; product names make “we’re all set” easy.
- When in doubt, **go a level higher** and get introduced downward.

## Anti-patterns
- **Minimizing phrases** that invite a brush-off.
- **Ignoring the screener to “save time”** and missing the best intel in the building.
- **Formal cover letters** and logo envelopes that look like every other vendor.
- **Leaving voicemail on a vacation pile** instead of timing the live call.
- **Implying the executive ordered them to buy** — only that you came from above.

## Worked Example
First call: “Hi, I’m Jason with National Systems. I hope you can help me. I’d like to speak with Ms. Peterson, and to be sure what I’d discuss is relevant, there are a couple of questions you could probably answer.”

If probed: “We help parts manufacturers in your industry increase short-term cash flow while they wait on longer-term receivables. I want to ask him a few questions to see how big that problem is and whether it’s worth speaking further. In fact, you could probably help me with some of that.”

Literature path: Package to Shelly, handwritten, highlighted ugly brochure, sticky: “Here’s the projector brochure — key areas marked to save you time. Looking forward to the 6th at 8:15.” Follow-up names the ugly brochure so it is memorable.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat screeners as buyers: respect, help-ask, justify, question.
2. People they like get through; people who slime them get the door bolted.
3. Prepare a results answer to “what is this about?” — do not freeze or dodge.
4. Collect names, preferences, hierarchy, and message-channel habits.
5. If you must send info, send it through the gatekeeper in a way that serves *their* job.
6. Start high; time vacation returns; thank them in person when you can.
7. Never use intimidation scripts from old “get past the gatekeeper” lore.
