# Chapter 20: A Smart Call Case Study and Makeovers

## Core Idea
Smart Calling is a built, practiced, recorded system — not smile-and-dial. The HUMAN Healthy Vending case shows the full stack. The makeovers show how missing PVP in the first 10 seconds creates the resistance reps then blame on “prospecting.”

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Build-the-system loop**: Use when a company has no sales process.
  - How: Define motivators → write PVPs and test them → research + social engineering → openings and voicemail → question trees with anticipated answers and follow-ups → listen to recordings → refine. Chad Vanags did this and then taught the rest of the team.

- **First-call objective with trial closes**: Use in multi-step motions.
  - How: Interest + needs on call one; brief results; recommend the next step (online presentation). Trial-close so that agreeing to the meeting means they are already ~70% sold.

- **Prepared RRR, not “overcoming”**: Use when they have an incumbent.
  - How: “We already have vending” → acknowledge most schools do; you are not *just* vending; that is why you want questions. Differentiate without fighting.

- **Three-bucket question frame**: Use when you need them to rank value.
  - How: Empathize that they get vendor calls all day (get a yes). Then: “I find principals usually care about (1) revenue (2) health/wellness/education (3) hassle-free. Accurate? Which is most important?” If none, ask their three. **Write answers word for word** for the close and the later presentation (consistency, Ch 14). Branch questions by bucket (why that one; how they use commission; guideline losses; White House/New York Times-scale impact as a prompt; what they would improve; parent reaction; etc.). Salvage: “How would you determine if it’s worth looking?” “What results must a program produce?” Satisfied → “What would it take to be *more* than satisfied?” (Ch 11).

- **Opening-makeover rule**: Use on any struggling opener.
  - How: If the first sentences are “we are X and I want time to explain what we do,” you created the resistance. Add intel + possible result + questions. Missing “why should they give you a moment?” is why generic “do you have a moment for two questions?” fails even when an “expert” publishes it.

## Key Concepts
- **Motivators are role-specific**: Principals — vending revenue crushed by restrictions (example district: hundreds of thousands down to tens of thousands); nutrition matters but revenue is measurable; vending is low on a hundred-duty list.
- **Social engineering into schools**: District vs. school decision rights; existing machines; food service; wellness initiatives.
- **Close language**: “If there were a way to see options that help you [their benefit], [benefit], and [benefit] while keeping it hassle-free, would you be open to a conversation about ideas?” Echo their words.
- **Freight makeover**: Outside rep hated calling because the opener asked for time to blab. Same as Ch 1 dumb insurance call.
- **Video-duplication makeover**: “I finally called, so I guess you can start using us” — dialing does not entitle you to time. Reconstruction: you produce training DVDs and may be paying a premium on short runs; we specialize in quality duplication and quick small orders; depending on price and satisfaction, it might be worth a bid; questions. If still blocked: “What plans for a backup if you needed a large quantity fast and your supplier couldn’t?”
- **Recycling makeover**: Add the green-initiative blog intel + trash-hauling and paper-goods cost results + “if I’ve caught you at a good time, I’d like to ask about the new policy to see if this is worth a look.”

## Mental Models
- Use **Chad as the poster child**: hours of construction, then calls — not talent as a substitute for a script.
- Think of makeovers as **resistance you were manufacturing**.
- Prefer **ranked buckets + verbatim notes** over a generic discovery laundry list.
- Trial-close the first call so the “demo” is not a cold presentation.

## Anti-patterns
- **Smile and dial without a question tree.**
- **Fighting “we already have a vendor”** instead of acknowledging and differentiating.
- **Opening with company history and a request for time.**
- **“Do you have a moment for a couple of quick questions?”** with no payoff.
- **Not writing their words** — then closing with *your* benefits.
- **Believing free internet “expert” openers** that skip possible value.

## Worked Example
Chad opening (paraphrase, specific account): “Dr. Cook, Chad with HUMAN Healthy Vending. I read your Turnaround Plan, and Renee said you’re considering pulling all vending at Lawson. I help schools replace junk with healthier options, add nutrition education, and can aim at *increasing* revenue instead of eliminating it. I’d like to ask some questions to see if I could provide information.”

Then yes-ladder on vendor-call fatigue → three concerns → rank → branched questions in their language → recommendation of a web session using their ranked outcomes.

Freight before: “We’re a freight shipper; I’d like to come take time to explain what we do.” After: intel about their shipping pattern + possible on-time/cost result + questions to see if a look is worth it — no unearned appointment.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build PVPs, intel paths, openings, RRR lines, and question trees *before* volume.
2. Record and coach the whole call; promote what works to the team.
3. First call: interest, their words, trial closes, next-step recommendation.
4. Frame and rank their world (revenue / wellness / ease — or *their* three).
5. Makeovers always add intel + possible result + a request to question.
6. Dialing, or “a moment for questions,” does not earn time; hinted payoff does.
7. Ignore opening advice that has no why-for-them.
