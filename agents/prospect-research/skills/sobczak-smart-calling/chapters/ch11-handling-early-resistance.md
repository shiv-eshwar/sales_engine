# Chapter 11: Handling Early Resistance on Your Smart Calls

## Core Idea
Even a perfect opening will draw “not interested” / “we’re happy.” Expect Resistant Reflex Responses (RRRs). Do not argue. Interrupt the pattern, soften, and get them talking so the reflex has something real to attach to — or so you can salvage a secondary.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Resistant Reflex Response (RRR)**: Use as your name for instant blow-offs.
  - How: Treat them as ducking a flying object — little thought, high success against unprepared reps. You cannot logic against a reflex that has no content yet. Do not use “of course you’re not interested — I haven’t given you anything to be interested in!”

- **Pattern interrupt**: Use immediately after an RRR.
  - How: Soften, then a question that forces a new pattern (NLP idea, simplified). Soft, almost surprised or disappointed tone — never confrontational.
    - “I’m not interested.” → “I see. Where are you getting your compressors now?”
    - “We’re all set.” → “I understand. When is your next project coming up?”
    - “We wouldn’t need that.” → “Oh. How are you handling written-off receivables?”
  - Softeners: Oh / I see / I understand. Galper: “That’s not a problem,” then “I’m not trying to replace your vendor. Would you be open to some different ideas you might not be using now?”

- **Sound-dumb / explain**: Use when the reason does not parse.
  - How: “I’m not following. Could you explain?” “I heard you but I’m not following the reasoning.” More talk = more to work with.

- **Happy-with-supplier branch**: Use when that is the RRR.
  - How (pick a fit): stay-in-touch via webinars/white papers then “a few questions so we tailor”; “teach me what it would take to feel *better than* satisfied”; request a comparison quote as a value-check; “if something changes, may I stay in touch?” then what would need to change; offer to be **second source** during shortages; “when will you next evaluate?” + “under what circumstances would you consider switching?”
  - Do **not** ask “what do you like best about them?” (Ch 12) — you may get a love letter.

- **Early literature, “why you,” rush, price**: Use the matching reply, then questions.
  - Literature-as-brush-off: happy to send; to tailor it I need a few questions.
  - “Why should I consider you?”: trap if you list reasons. “There might be a few; I need to learn your situation.” / “That’s why I’m calling — a few questions.”
  - “You’ve got two minutes”: don’t spray the pitch; often ignore the clock if interest holds; or offer to call when they have time because you need details to see how much you could save.
  - Quick not-interested: “Does that mean never, or just not now?”
  - Early price: defer — depends on variables; range if they insist; “you want the best price — we have to look at your situation.”

## Key Concepts
- **Fourth-grader persistence**: After “I already bought tickets,” “That’s okay. You didn’t get any from me yet.” Prepared for no; ask again without combat.
- **Catalog-buyer case**: Not looking for more suppliers → process questions: how do you evaluate new products, who is involved, criteria, how current was chosen, under what circumstances would you look, can we be involved, stay-in-touch system. Those questions won a detailed reply and an invitation on-site.

## Mental Models
- Use **soften + question**, never **rebuttal + pitch**.
- Think of an RRR as a **closed pattern**; your job is to start a different conversation.
- Prefer **second source / future eval / better-than-satisfied** over “switch to us now.”
- When they demand a pitch reason, **sell the need for questions**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Clever comebacks** that insult the reflex.
- **Dumping the presentation** because they gave you two minutes.
- **Answering “why you” with a feature list.**
- **Quoting price before needs.**
- **Accepting “send literature” as the end** on an early call.
- **Arguing that they *are* interested.**

## Worked Example
Safety-eyewear into a catalog: instant “not looking for additional suppliers.” Instead of a speech, ask evaluation process, committee, criteria, origin of current line, circumstances for a new look, permission to be in that process, plus a stay-in-touch cadence. Result in the field: detailed answers and a corporate-office invitation.

School vending RRR (Ch 20 preview): “We already have vending.” → “That’s fine, most schools do. We’re actually quite a bit different and not concerned *just* about vending, which is why I wanted to ask a few questions.” Acknowledge + differentiate + questions — not “overcoming.”

## Key Takeaways
1. Script your common RRRs with softener + interrupt question; practice tone.
2. Get them talking; the reflex fades when content appears.
3. Never fight “not interested” with logic about your opening.
4. For incumbent-happy, aim at better-than-satisfied, next eval, or second source.
5. Early send-info / why-you / price / two-minutes are invitations to question, not to pitch.
6. “Never or not now?” keeps future doors honest.
7. Stay-in-touch with tailored education can turn today’s RRR into tomorrow’s inbound.
