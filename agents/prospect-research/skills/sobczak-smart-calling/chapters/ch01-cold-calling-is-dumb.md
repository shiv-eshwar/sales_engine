# Chapter 1: Cold Calling Is Dumb, but Prospecting Is Necessary: Smart Calling Is the Answer

## Core Idea
Never place a cold call. Prospect by phone using intelligence, so the first 10 seconds sound like you already belong in the buyer’s world. Telephone prospecting is essential; robotic pitching to strangers is not.

## Frameworks Introduced (When / How)
- **Smart Calling (three steps)**: Use when you must reach someone who does not know you.
  - How: (1) Acquire intelligence on the person, company, and industry before the decision maker comes on the line. (2) Plug that intelligence into a conversational, consultative process. (3) Help them take an action they feel good about and gain value from.
  - Why it works: Gatekeepers become sources, not walls. Buyers hear relevance in the first 10–20 seconds—the window where most calls die.
  - Failure mode: Skipping step 1 and “winging” a pitch. You sound like every other vendor.

- **Quality game vs. numbers game**: Use when a manager or inner voice says “just dial more.”
  - How: Measure successful conversations, not dials. Activity without the right words does not move you closer to a yes. The previous no has no bearing on the next call.

- **Take the call as far as possible**: Use when your model includes a face-to-face visit.
  - How: Do not treat the phone as appointment-only. Qualify, create interest, and advance on the call so the later meeting is with a hot, informed prospect.

## Key Concepts
- **Cold call**: Dialing someone you do not know, who does not know you, with little or no information, delivering the same pitch every time.
- **Telephone prospecting**: Necessary hunting to replace lost customers and create opportunities that inbound marketing will not produce on your timeline.
- **Sales is a verb** (Weinberg): Top performers do not wait for inbound; they act.
- **Social intelligence**: Research plus people-sourced facts that turn a stranger call into a Smart Call.
- **Social engineering** (preview): Speaking with others inside the company to learn current issues before the buyer call.
- **First 10 seconds**: The make-or-break window. Talk results and their situation, not products and your quota.
- **Screener-as-buyer**: Assistants may decide, influence, or kill the deal. Treat them as the buyer.
- **Win on every call**: Rejection is a reaction, not an event. Secondary objectives (Ch 5) make every call a win.

## Mental Models
- Use **Smart Calling** when you have identified a high-fit prospect who will not respond to mail, social, or waiting.
- Think of **cold calling** as interviewing Brad Pitt with “So, what is it you do?” — unprepared and insulting.
- Prefer **quality attempts** over swinging at every pitch. Casino games are numbers; prospecting is skill.
- Treat **marketing busywork** (letters, door hangers, social posting during selling hours) as avoidance when your job is talking to people.

## Anti-patterns
- **Same pitch, zero research**: “We sell X; I’d like 10 minutes to tell you what we do and save you time and money.” Triggers “we already have that.”
- **Nickname guessing**: Calling Michael “Mike” when the list said Michael.
- **Taking time without giving value**: “I’d like to take 10 minutes of your time.”
- **Appointment close in the opening**: Asking Tuesday or Wednesday before they have a reason to meet.
- **“Never give the screener any information”**: Makes you look shady and burns the person who can help you.
- **Loving rejection**: You want to avoid the feeling of rejection by designing wins, not by enjoying no’s.
- **Waiting for inbound only**: Ideal as a mix; fatal as the only motion for a named, high-value target.

## Worked Example
Dumb opening (insurance): “Hi Mike, I’m Dale with Insurance Partners. We provide employee benefits. I’d like 10 minutes to tell you what we do and how we save time and money. I’ll be in your area next week — Tuesday or Wednesday?” Errors: wrong name, product dump, taking time, “tell you what we do,” empty “time and money,” travel as a benefit, asking for a meeting too soon.

Smart reconstruction (same company, same buyer): “Hi Michael, I’m Pat with Insurance Partners. Hope you enjoyed the golf trip. Suzanne mentioned you’re evaluating how to attract and keep talent across locations. We’ve helped similar companies lower recruiting cost and raise managerial retention. I’d like to ask a few questions to see if I could provide some information.”

What Pat did before the call: researched Michael (hates “Mike,” golf, article, Cubs, prior competitor), social-engineered Suzanne on hiring losses and benefits dissatisfaction, and opened on *their* current initiative — not insurance. Later he asked questions he already largely knew the answers to, which made him sound prepared rather than nosy.

## Key Takeaways
1. Ban “cold call” from professional prospecting vocabulary; keep the phone.
2. Smart Calling = intelligence + consultative dialogue + buyer-felt action.
3. Quality of the attempt beats volume of dials.
4. In the first 10 seconds, state their situation and possible results — never a product catalog.
5. Treat screeners as part of the sales team.
6. Take every live conversation as far as the prospect’s interest allows.
7. Two reasons people still cold-call: laziness, or not knowing how. This skill covers the second.
