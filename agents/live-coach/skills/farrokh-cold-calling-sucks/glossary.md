# Glossary

**30-Second Rule** — Disqualify a bad account in the first 30 seconds of research; save time for buyers. (Ch 6)

**A/B/C Tiers** — A = problem *now*; B = has the problem; C = rest of ICP. Extra dials on A/B. (Ch 6)

**Above / at / below the power line** — Exec money-risk vs day-to-day owner vs end user; each gets a different triggering problem. (Ch 2, Ch 6)

**Arms-Up tone** — Tailored Permission delivery: honest, slightly cheeky, nothing to hide — not chipper, not ashamed. (Ch 1)

**Banned openers** — “How’s your day going?” (~7.6% Gong) and “Did I catch you at a bad time?” (~2.15%). Also “Is this [Name]?” (Ch 1)

**Chain objection handling** — Run Miyagi on each successive objection until they ask *you* questions, then book. (Ch 3)

**Commitments and Consequences** — Public weekly dial goal plus a stick for missing it. (Ch 7, Ch 8)

**Confidence (Farrokh/Cegelski)** — Belief that inputs produce outputs (enough dials → a meeting). (Ch 8)

**Context-first opener** — Lead with a unique thread about *them* before your name or product. (Ch 1)

**Disarmingly blunt** — Meet abrasive dismissals with extreme transparency/humor instead of apology. (Ch 4)

**Double Tap Voicemails** — Exactly two VMs: #1 ~15s context-only; #2 ~30s context + social proof; both point to email. (Ch 5)

**Feet-Up tone** — Heard The Name delivery: assumptive, downtone, referral-casual. (Ch 1)

**First 60 seconds** — Win/loss window; goal is to *earn* the next 60. (Intro, Ch 1–2)

**Gatekeeper Triple Bypass** — Slide-by → Context → Social proof; dose info only when pressed. (Ch 5)

**Golden 3** — Connect rate × set rate × show rate; same dials, top quartile ≈ 13× kept meetings vs average. (Ch 6)

**Green / Yellow / Red Hours** — 8–12 prospecting, 12–3 customers, 3–6 admin. (Ch 7)

**Heard The Name Tossed Around** — Context → intro → “Heard the name tossed around?” Gong ~11.24%. (Ch 1)

**Incentive (no more cold calls)** — Reason a stranger talks: so you *know* not to call again. (Ch 3)

**Interest-based CTA** — Validate interest before calendaring; softeners, no-based asks, mini push-aways. (Ch 2)

**Knee-jerk objection** — Interruption swat, not a product verdict (“I don’t like animals”). (Ch 3)

**Law of diminishing returns** — Pre-commit: ~5 dials in 4 weeks then rest; two VMs max; stop impassable GKs. (Ch 6)

**Micro-agree** — Generic agree that buys thinking time before the specific agree. (Ch 3)

**Mini push-away** — Lower-pressure preface (“my guess is you’re all set”). (Ch 2–4)

**Minimum viable talk tracks** — Opener + Problem Proposition + Miyagi; enough to start the contract. (Intro, Ch 8)

**Mr. Miyagi Method** — Agree → incentivize conversation → sell the test drive. (Ch 3)

**Name-drop referral** — Wrong-person path: permission to mention them when *you* email the real owner. (Ch 4)

**No-based question** — Ask them to reject a small ask (“opposed to a peek?”) so yes is easier. (Ch 2)

**One decision at a time** — Never stack care + belief + meet-this-stranger + calendar slot. (Ch 2)

**One-sentence solution** — “We do X so the problem goes away”; X = the differentiator that *allows* the fix. (Ch 2)

**Parallel dialer rule** — OK for huge territories; dangerous for small enterprise (no context, pickup lag). (Ch 6)

**Permission-based opener (untailored)** — Own the cold call + 30-second ask, no research hook. Gong ~11.18%. (Ch 1)

**Pre-pitch dismissive** — Swat before they know what you sell; pivot to permission opener, don’t test-drive. (Ch 4)

**Problem Proposition** — Triggering problem + one-sentence solution + interest CTA; ~80% problem. (Ch 2)

**Sell the test drive** — Why meet even if they never buy; do not sell the car on the cold call. (Ch 3)

**Sit-up moment** — Instant sense you work with their peers. (Ch 1)

**Slide-by** — First-name, no company, ask for the transfer. (Ch 5)

**Tailored Permission Opener** — Problem-tied context → own the cold call → 30 seconds then they decide. (Ch 1)

**10 Dial No Pee Rule** — No morning ritual until 10 dials are done. (Ch 7)

**30-for-40 / 60-for-40** — Night-before inventory: ≥60 tasks to complete 40 dials in 60 minutes. (Ch 7)

**Throw a dart** — Placeholder calendar invite + backups instead of email ping-pong. (Ch 2)

**Trap question** — Agreeing, curious question that lets *them* notice a gap in the current solution. (Ch 4)

**Triggering problem** — Movie-scene pain: persona, annoyances, scenery, emotion, in their words. (Ch 2)

**Value proposition (rejected)** — Buzzword/benefit pitch without a problem; Gong buzzwords ~5.5% vs problem language ~16%. (Ch 2)

**Witness protection** — Verbal “later/email/callback” then they vanish. (Ch 4)
