# Chapter 6: How to Maximize Your Dial Conversion

## Core Idea
#1 on the activity board is almost never #1 on the leaderboard. Meetings kept = dials × **Golden 3**: connect rate × set rate × show rate. Same 200 dials/week × 4 weeks, top-quartile Golden 3 vs average ≈ **13×** kept meetings. Work *off* the phone so every dial has juice.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Golden 3 Conversion Metrics**:
  - **Connect %**: picked up.
  - **Set %**: conversations → meetings.
  - **Show %**: meetings that occur.
  - When to use: Diagnose before “make more dials.” Kenny: 400 dials, 10 connects (2.5%), junk accounts/people/numbers — 4,000 more dials would not save him.

- **Four levers for connect rate**:
  1. **Prioritize mobile and direct**. Skip trees/GKs. US cell is generally acceptable; 20 answers per 1 annoyed beat corp-only.
  2. **Mark your tracks** on the first pass: red/yellow/green numbers. Log phone-tree keycodes, dead corporates *at company level*, front-desk (don’t ask for four people in one blitz), GK notes. Tag/skip bad numbers in the SEP.
  3. **Law of diminishing returns (pre-commit)**: ~**5 dials in 4 weeks** then rest a month; **stop after two VMs**; avoid impassable GKs (cell / other channel / off-hours). Decade-long hunters lose to people who spent those dials on pickups.
  4. **Don’t get spam-tagged**: Register the number (e.g. Free Caller Registry); rotate lines (carriers watch volume spikes); test new/recycled numbers against your own phone first; call **business hours** (FTC window cited 8 a.m.–9 p.m.; 8 a.m. / 5 p.m. OK to beat GKs, 5 a.m. / 10 p.m. not); stop hammering bad numbers (that *is* a spam signal).

- **Unethical connect hacks — never**: Double/triple-dial to fake an emergency, *67 hide, cold FaceTime. They answer pissed, spam-report you, and will not buy.

- **Maximize set rate — call people who have the problem** (priority):
  1. Problem **now**
  2. Have a problem
  3. Everyone else in ICP
  - Build the list **in reverse**: big list → cut junk → rank remainder.

  **Step 1 — Qualified companies (or people in B2C)**: List disqualifiers first (The **30-Second Rule**: spot a bad account in 30s; save research for buyers).

  **Step 2 — Tier A/B/C**: Top ~5 buy-signals, **timing at the top**. Example (comp software): upcoming review / recent fundraise / hiring a comp person = now; fast growth / “generous equity” in jobs = problem. **A** = now, **B** = problem, **C** = ICP. Add **two extra dials** to A/B.

  **Step 3 — Right people, whole account**: If you work the account, work it. Above-the-line execs (VP/Chief HR + Finance), at-the-line champions (Heads of Comp/HR Ops), some below-the-line users. Don’t burn an A-account on one junior analyst. Extra person-level notes for A-tier. Cold call still reaches old-school execs whose inboxes EAs guard.

  **Step 4 — Prioritize within reason**: Don’t cherry-pick so hard you stall. Stack A/B with extra dials so the queue is naturally better. Jump the line for **intent**: 5× email opens, pricing-page visit, email objection reply, new compelling event.

- **Show rate**: Gong on confirmation-email *count* was inconclusive; still send **good** confirms.
  - Same-week meeting: **day-before**, assume it’s on, add a prepared human detail (their news). Do not “are we still on?”
  - 2+ weeks out: week-before **and** day-before (second can be a one-line reply).
  - If late: email at **2:00**, call at **5:00**, call again at **8:00** (only acceptable double-dial).
  - No-show: **never delete the invite**. Punt 3–4 days, backup times, light guilt (“we prep for these”). Easier to keep a meeting than find a new one.

- **Parallel dialer (preface to Ch 7)**: More connects/time; dropped-VM option. Cost: you often cannot lead with context (generic PBO), plus pickup lag = call-center vibe. Use if **territory is huge** (conversion dip < 3× connects). Skip / single-thread if **small enterprise territory** where each connect is precious.

## Key Concepts
- **No Crappy Dials punch list**: Disqualifiers, tier signals, titles, intent alerts — defined *before* research so accounts take <5 minutes.
- **Green/yellow/red numbers**: Quality tags from pass one.
- **Intent signals**: Behavior that jumps the queue.

## Mental Models
- Use **Golden 3 math** before adding volume — one lever compounds on every dial.
- Use **disqualify-first research** when the CRM is a junk drawer.
- Use **A-tier extra dials + whole-account coverage** when a logo actually has the problem *now*.
- Use **punt-don’t-delete** whenever a meeting no-shows.

## Anti-patterns
- **Redialing the same dead corp / fax / busy** because you didn’t tag.
- **Junior-only or wrong-department** on good accounts.
- **Spam tricks** for pickup.
- **“Are we still on?”** confirmations (invites doubt).
- **Deleting no-show holds**.

## Worked Example
*Conversion triage (reconstructed)*

Kenny: 400 dials, 10 conversations. Unmarked bad corporates ×3, busy ×3, 10-person company, lost deal last week, wrong department, analyst. Fix: tag numbers, 30-second DQ, A/B/C on timing signals, call CHRO *and* CFO *and* Head of Comp, extra two dials on A/B, day-before confirm with a specific news line.

A-tier trigger: “Fundraised 6 weeks ago + job post mentions equity refresh.” That’s two extra dials and person-level notes — not 400 more random corporates.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose Golden 3 before grinding more activity.
2. Connect: mobiles/directs, mark tracks, 5-in-4-weeks + 2-VM cap, spam hygiene, no trick dials.
3. Set: DQ junk in 30s, tier timing-first, call high and wide, extra dials on A/B, honor intent.
4. Show: assume-on personalized confirms; 2/5/8 minute chase; punt the invite on no-show.
5. Parallel dialers: volume territories yes, tiny enterprise maybe no.
