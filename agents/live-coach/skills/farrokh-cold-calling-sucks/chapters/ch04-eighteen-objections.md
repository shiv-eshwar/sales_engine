# Chapter 4: 18 Objections and How to Handle Them

## Core Idea
Apply Miyagi *specifically* by bucket, not by memorized script. Gong mix: ~49.5% dismissive, ~42.6% situational, ~7.9% existing-solution. Master the category strategy first; the top 5 objections cover ~74% of volume.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three buckets, three strategies**:
  - **Dismissive**: Fake/knee-jerk. Agree hard, be **disarmingly blunt**, surface the *real* objection, then Miyagi that. Pre-pitch subset: do **not** test-drive — get back to the Problem Proposition via a permission pivot.
  - **Situational**: Transition cost > benefit (budget, price, bandwidth, hire, “not for us”). Let them “win” today; sell the car later. Mini push-aways. Get them in the car.
  - **Existing solution**: Never throw mud. Agree the current way works → **trap question** that lets *them* notice a gap → test drive with **peer social proof**, not your opinion.

- **Disarmingly blunt + humor**: The more ridiculous their reaction, the more opposite yours. Transparency and leaning into misery pull the chair out. Do not stammer-apologize.

- **Script rules (mandatory)**: Focus on delivery, use this chapter as a reference, practice *then* read the track. Word-for-word scripts sound telemarketer, miss nuance, and fail on objection 19.

- **Priority order**: (1) Learn bucket approach. (2) The objections *you* actually get. (3) Global top 5. (4) The rest. One objection per week × ~30 reps.

### Dismissive (post-pitch): reveal, then Miyagi
1. **Not interested** — Fake. Blame your pitch, name the cold-call hate, force a real reason. No test drive until the real objection appears; then restart Miyagi.
2. **Call me in 6 months** — Nice “not interested.” Multiple-choice: real trigger vs “busy.” If real, test-drive *today* so later is easier. If busy (9/10), treat like send-info bluntness.
3. **Send me some information** — Nobody wants the PDF. Call the lie, get them to stand by it, then over-invest in a useful follow-up + placeholder meeting.
4. **Not my responsibility** — Drop the name/title who *should* own it. If they were lying, they fess the real objection. If truly wrong person: do not ask *them* to refer (strangers don’t). Ask permission to **name-drop** when *you* reach out; email subject like “Spoke with [Name].”

### Pre-pitch dismissive: blunt, then permission opener
Goal: 30 more seconds for the prop, not a meeting yet.
5. **Where’d you get my number?** — Name the database. Offer to screenshot their row. Double incentive: last call from you *and* maybe from this list.
6. **I’m in a meeting!** — Unlikely. Match absurdity (speakerphone joke). Do **not** “I’ll call back” (witness protection). Two options: calendar placeholder *now* or talk now. 50/50 invite > callback to a blocked number.
7. **Is this a cold call!?** — Irate pattern. Be the opposite: name that this is going badly, give them the right to slam the door, recreate face-to-face courtesy in words.
8. **I thought you were someone else** — Lean into the awkward (wrong-house energy). Joke, then permission ask, and explicitly allow hang-up if the real person calls back.
9. **\*Hang up\*** — Do **not** redial “we got disconnected” (success ≈ 0). Log date, time, *exact* last words. Call back ~1 week later using those three as Tailored Permission context. Second hang-up → email/LinkedIn.

### Situational: remove purchase pressure
10. **No budget** — Agree that asking for money burns political capital. Multiple-choice: timing (cycle) vs value (must create budget). Test drive = help them win funds / directional sense, not “buy now.”
11. **Too Expensive** — External sticker vs internal budget. Multiple-choice: prior rep failed to sell value vs true cheap. Hint flexibility without locking a discount. Prove value or skinny-package *on the meeting*.
12. **No resources / bandwidth** — Forever-busy posture. Ask now-vs-forever with humor, then test-drive “how other teams do more with less.”
13. **Need to hire first** — Searches take months. If start date is real, placeholder then. If not, test-drive a punch list the new boss can inherit.
14. **It’s not made for XYZ** — Product/fit objection, often expert ego. Multiple-choice on *why* unfit. Test drive via peer customers *they* can talk to. **Ego boost**: don’t fight the expert; invite them to see for themselves.

### Existing solution: trap, then social proof
15. **We do it in-house** — Agree spreadsheets/process work at their stage. Trap: the later-stage pain that process cannot absorb. Test drive the gap they named.
16. **Competitor (known)** — Never badmouth. Trap on the problem *your* differentiator uniquely hits. Peers who switched > your pitch.
17. **Competitor (unknown)** — You need the name for a trap. Don’t ask “who?” cold. Multiple-choice the usual suspects, incentivize twice with *different* incentives, then Miyagi the named tool.
18. **Stuck in a contract** — Unknown competitor + timing. Handle competitor first; test drive with **no rip-and-replace today** (options for the renewal). Buyouts later, on the meeting.

## Key Concepts
- **Witness protection**: They say callback/email and disappear.
- **Trap question**: Curious, agreeing setup that lets *them* surface the hole (“you must not get sick often?” energy — not a gotcha).
- **Name-drop referral**: You do the outreach; they only allow the mention.
- **Placeholder meeting**: Calendar now for a later window (hire start, budget cycle) instead of a verbal “later.”

## Mental Models
- Use **bucket strategy** when you cannot recall a track — 50% of the way there.
- Use **pre-pitch rules** when they object before they know what you do.
- Use **trap + social proof** when they already have a way — never vegetables-on-a-protein-diet lectures.
- Master **one objection a week**; do not cram 18.

## Anti-patterns
- **Test-driving a pre-pitch swat**: They have no idea what you sell.
- **Immediate callback after hang-up**.
- **Criticizing the incumbent / diet**.
- **Same incentive twice** on unknown competitor — sounds canned.
- **Locking discounts or buyouts on the cold call**.

## Worked Example
*Not interested → competitor (chain, reconstructed)*

Prospect: “Not interested.”
You: “Totally fair — you probably would’ve reached out if this was on your list. Just so nobody hits you again: is it that you’ve got something in place, you’re doing it in-house, or you just hate these calls?”
Prospect: “We’re on SalesGoon.”
You: “Should’ve assumed you’d be set — usually doesn’t make sense to rip that out. Just so I don’t bother you: are you hitting [differentiator-gap A], or is it working fine?”
Prospect: “It’s fine enough.”
You: “Rarely makes sense to replace it. A few teams in your world switched because of X. Completely against a look — not to rip it out, just to know the tradeoffs?”

## Key Takeaways
1. Category first: dismissive = reveal; pre-pitch = blunt + PBO; situational = car later; existing = trap + proof.
2. Top 5 ≈ 74% — start there plus *your* local list.
3. Do not memorize 18 scripts; memorize Miyagi and bucket tactics.
4. Hang-ups: log three facts, wait a week, context-open.
5. Wrong person: name-drop permission, you send the email.
6. Practice one objection weekly (~30 reps).
