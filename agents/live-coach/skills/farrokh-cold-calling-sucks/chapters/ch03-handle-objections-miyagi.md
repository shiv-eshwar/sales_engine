# Chapter 3: How to Handle Objections Like Mr. Miyagi

## Core Idea
The first objection is almost never the real one — it is a fight-or-flight swat at the interruption. Do not counter-punch with logic. Absorb the momentum: agree, get them talking, then sell a no-pressure test drive, not the product.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Mr. Miyagi Method**: Three steps for *any* objection after the first 60 seconds.
  - When to use: Live objection after opener + Problem Proposition (and again on every follow-on objection).
  - How: (1) **Agree** with the *specific* objection. (2) **Incentivize conversation** so they air the real reason. (3) **Sell the test drive** — why meet even if they never buy.
  - Why it works: You cannot beat emotion with logic. Agreement is a pattern break (“wait, you’re not pitching?”). Talk time correlates with meetings (Gong); median successful call is ~4.8 minutes — expect 2+ objections.
  - Failure mode: Agree then immediately re-pitch. Trust dies; they clamp shut.

- **Micro-agree (buy time)**: Short universal lead-in while you load the specific agree.
  - When to use: The instant they object — especially if you feel rattled.
  - How: “I totally get that…” / “That one’s on me…” / “I should’ve assumed you’d be all set…” Then complete the specific agree. Pause ~2 seconds. Speak slower than you think (0.5x). Tell yourself “pause, slow down.”

- **Incentive: no more cold calls**: Strangers will not dump business details. Give a reason to talk — so you *really* know they are not worth calling again.
  - How: Multiple-choice questions that jump on their side of the objection and pile on (“out of budget this year, or you get put through the wringer every time you buy?”). Multiple-choice also marks you as an insider.

- **Sell the Test Drive (not the car)**: Answer “why meet if they don’t buy?”
  - When to use: After they have talked. Never as the first move.
  - How: Mini push-away (“you’re probably not gonna buy this now”) + a directional benefit of looking (ideas for an in-house process, wishlist if budget frees, tradeoffs vs current tool).
  - Generic fallback: “My guess is you’re not gonna buy this, but open to taking a look — not for now, but so you know what’s out there?”

- **Chain Objection Handling**: Run Miyagi again on each new objection until they ask *you* questions — then book (Ch 2 calendar rules).
  - When to use: Always. One handled objection is not enough.

## Key Concepts
- **Knee-jerk vs real objection**: “Not interested,” “call me in 6 months,” “send info” = interruption reactions (the “I don’t like animals” lie).
- **No-pressure sale zone**: Agree + incentivize before any ask. Recommendations land only after they feel understood.
- **Pace as status**: Speeding up after an objection marks you as junior. Slow + raised eyebrow = peer; they feel like the jerk.
- **Laugh on nasty objections**: Comfortable peer, not victim. Rebalances power.
- **Shrug on the test drive**: Mall-kiosk eagerness kills a “just for kicks” ask.

## Mental Models
- Use **agree first** whenever they snap — they sat down and you pulled the chair.
- Think **wax on, wax off**: redirect the punch; do not counter.
- Use **multiple-choice + “so no one calls again”** when a stranger won’t explain.
- Book the meeting when they **start asking you questions**, not after the first “no.”

## Anti-patterns
- **Logic-on-emotion**: Re-pitch, pile value, push the meeting into a swat. They push harder.
- **Unsolicited advice before understanding**: Classic resistance trigger.
- **Open questions with no incentive**: “What tool are you on?” → clam-up.
- **Fighting the objection on the phone**: You will not win budget, beat a competitor, or prove ROI on a cold call. Get them in the car.
- **Memorizing scripts as the method**: Rigid tone, slight-off replies, freeze on objection #19. Master the three steps (see Ch 4 disclaimer).

## Worked Example
*No budget — full Miyagi (reconstructed)*

Prospect: “Sorry, we don’t have any budget.”
You *(pause, slow)*: “I hear you. These days it’s hard enough to keep what’s already in the budget, let alone add something.”
You: “Just so no one calls you again — is it that there’s no budget this cycle, or that any spend means you have to do a triple backflip internally?”
Prospect: “A bit of both. We’re tight.”
You: “Got it. You’re probably not buying this now. But if budget ever frees up, the people who’ve seen it at least have a directional sense of what they’d want. Open to taking a look so you know what’s out there?”

If they then say “we’re on Competitor X”: restart at Agree — do not skip to a feature fight.

## Key Takeaways
1. First objection = interruption, not a verdict on the product.
2. Agree specifically (micro-agree while you think), then incentivize talk, then test-drive.
3. Incentive that works: last cold call + multiple-choice.
4. Sell looking, not buying. Mini push-aways make the test drive safe.
5. Slow down, pause two seconds, laugh, shrug. Tone is the method.
6. Chain it. Median win is ~4.8 minutes and 2+ objections.
7. Drill agree+incentivize on your top 4 objections (25 reps each) *before* collecting talk tracks.
