# Chapter 7: How to Make More Cold Calls in Less Time

## Core Idea
The first dial of the day is the hardest. Do not wait to “feel ready” or hunt the mythical best hour. Front-load calling, time-block it, and run a distraction-free 60 minutes at 40 dials. Consistency of effort beats micro-optimized timing; missing one blitz costs ~40 dials — a 5% connect bump from “best hour” cannot pay that back.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **10 Dial No Pee Rule**: No other morning ritual until 10 dials are done (literal bladder-as-commitment device in the origin story).
  - When to use: Any day you will otherwise warm up with inbox, Slack, and Netflix recaps until the phone feels heavier.
  - How: Hardest thing first. “You don’t need to feel good to get started, but you do need to get started to feel good.” After 10, momentum finishes the block.
  - Gong: morning is the best connect window anyway — so the “I’m not a morning person / Thursday 3 p.m. study” excuse dies twice.

- **Calendar of a Cold Calling Machine** — two requirements: **Front-loading** (hardest revenue work first) and **Time blocking** (in a dial block you *only* dial; inbox closed).
  - **Green Hours (8–12)**: Hit the daily prospecting goal; customer meetings only in gaps.
  - **Yellow Hours (12–3)**: Most customer meetings; leftover slots = extra prospecting.
  - **Red Hours (3–6)**: All admin.
  - Color-code: prospecting green, customer yellow, admin red. Don’t Skittle the calendar on day one.

  **Step 1 — Inbox at 8:30, 12:00, 3:00 only.** Close it (and chat) between. Recaps, proposals, non-signature work can wait. 2-minute response time is how middle reps ride the pipeline roller coaster (close everything → empty pipe).

  **Step 2 — Three prospecting blocks (full-cycle)**: 9 a.m. **calls first** (hardest); 10 or 11 **emails**; 1 p.m. **flex** (ahead / afternoon dials / leftovers). First two blocks = quota; third = President’s Club. SDRs: extra late-morning dials, extra early-afternoon email, 1–2 hours late-day research inventory.

  **Step 3 — Customer calls afternoon or morning gaps.** Never delete a Green Hour dial block; if a prospect can only do morning, **move the blitz to another Green slot**, don’t exile it to 4 p.m.

  **Step 4 — Admin at end of day**: Push 1:1s later when you can; 3 p.m. batch recaps/CRM/tomorrow prep; last two hours = **list build**. Night-before inventory or you cannot hit 40 at 9 a.m.: accounts researched, people, numbers, **≥60 tasks for 40 dials** (bad-number buffer).

- **40 dials in 60 minutes — 5 ground rules** (quality intact because research was last night):
  1. 60 tasks locked; research on one screen, dial on the other; punch numbers while you skim the card.
  2. **Never a 30-minute blitz.** First 5 dials are the worst 5; an hour forces you through warm-up.
  3. Goal = 40 (fast without cutting live conversations short).
  4. Close *everything* else: no email, chat, extra tabs, phone on DND. No multitask except the research pane.
  5. Don’t linger: next number immediately; booked? send invite, next; rejected? start anyway. Each no is toward a yes.

  If you cannot hit 40, you are mostly screwing around, not “being thorough.”

- **Weekly dial load**:
  - Math way: dials to *keep* one meeting (your Golden 3) × meetings needed for quota (SDRs easy; AEs messy).
  - **Practical default** (authors’ coaching pattern always collapsed here): **full-cycle 1 hour/day (~150–200 dials/week)**; **SDRs 2 hours/day (~300–400/week)**. Raise if you need more meetings. **Never lower.** A skipped day makes the phone heavier; one bad day → bad week → hit/miss roller coaster.
  - Exception: ultra-tight enterprise lists.

- **Commitments and Consequences**: Public weekly dial number + a *stick* if you miss (mustache, only-left-turns, Greek yogurt day, $100 to a cause you hate). Start with sticks until carrots work. Four weeks locked in is the habit.

## Key Concepts
- **Virtuous vs vicious morning**: Hard thing first vs admin-first inertia.
- **Pipeline roller coaster**: Inbox-as-job → feast/famine.
- **Green/Yellow/Red Hours**: Energy and task taxonomy.
- **60-for-40**: Task inventory vs completed dials.

## Mental Models
- Use **calls before pee/inbox** when reluctance is strongest (morning).
- Use **three inbox windows** when “I’m closing” is eating prospecting.
- Use **move-not-delete** on dial blocks the way Ch 6 punts no-shows.
- Use a **consequence** when willpower will lose to a busy calendar.

## Anti-patterns
- **Warm-up admin** until 11 a.m. with zero dials.
- **Timing studies as permission to skip** today’s block.
- **30-minute “blitzes”** that never leave warm-up.
- **Research during the dial hour** (that belongs in Red Hours / last night).
- **Lowering the weekly floor** after a good week.

## Worked Example
*Machine morning (reconstructed)*

8:28 — bathroom after? No. Laptop open.
8:30–8:40 — inbox window #1, then closed.
9:00–10:00 — DND, 60 tasks, 40 dials. First 10 before anything human. Meeting booked at dial 22: invite sent, next number. Hard no at 31: immediate next.
10:00 — email block.
Customer wants 9:30 tomorrow: move Thursday’s dial block to 11:00 Thursday (still Green), don’t cancel the habit.
3:00 — recaps + CRM. 4:00–6:00 — pull 60 tasks for tomorrow.
Weekly: 200 dials. Miss → stated consequence. Do not “make it up tomorrow.”

## Key Takeaways
1. Perfect time to call = the time you will actually call; morning is also empirically strong.
2. Green prospecting / yellow customers / red admin; inbox 3×/day.
3. Calls first, then emails, then flex; never exile the dial block out of Green Hours.
4. 60 prepped tasks, 60-minute block, 40 dials, zero lingering, zero extra windows.
5. 1–2 hours daily forever; raise, don’t cut. Public commitment + consequence.
