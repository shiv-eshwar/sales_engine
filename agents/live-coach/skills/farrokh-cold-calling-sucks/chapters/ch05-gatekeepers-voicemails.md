# Chapter 5: Gatekeepers and Voicemails

## Core Idea
Gatekeepers are trained to close on strangers and open for people who belong. Do not befriend them or pitch them. Dose information only when pressed (Triple Bypass). Voicemails are not for callbacks — they exist to **double email reply rate**. Leave exactly two, short, and point to the email.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Act like Drake’s aunt / walk in like you own the place**: Gatekeeper job #1 close strangers; job #2 open for important people. Remind them of #2. Tone that makes questioning you feel ridiculous.
  - When to use: Any live EA/front desk.
  - How: Minimum viable info. Never lie about identity. Never disrespect.
  - Why: Befriending fails (they’ve heard every sob story). Pitching fails (competent EAs reject solicitors on sight — even “cure world hunger”).
  - Gong: a gatekeeper cuts cold-call success ~**39%**. After 1–2 full shutdowns, stop fighting: mobile, off-hours, or another channel.

- **Gatekeeper Triple Bypass** — three tries, escalating only when pressed:
  1. **The Slide-by**: “Could you get me over to [First]? It’s [Your first name].” No company, no small talk, gruff. Full name raises “what company?” If they ask “Nick who?”: “Would you let him know it’s Nick Cegelski?” Returning a question reapplies pressure on *them*. The one who asks the questions controls the conversation.
  2. **The Context**: If “what’s this regarding?” — context, not product (other partners in the office, press release, merit cycle). End on a question: “Would you let him know it’s [Name]?”
  3. **The Social Proof**: If they still demand what you do — peer proof + company, plus “I sent him a note the other day.” Not a Problem Proposition. Then: if still stonewalled, ask to leave a voicemail.

- **Double Tap Voicemails**:
  - When to use: Sequence work. Not every dead air. Max **two**.
  - Why leave them despite the case against (rare callbacks; ~25% fewer dials if you VM everything; Gong: VMs cut **future connect rate ~28%** because you outed yourself as sales):
    - Email reply **2.73% → 5.87%** after a VM (roughly 2x) on *every* subsequent email.
    - Faster yes *and* faster no, so you stop calling ghosts.
    - Protect connect rate by **rotating numbers**.
  - Rules: Lead with context (never pitch on VM — they delete). Direct 100% of action to the **email**, never “call me back.” ≤30s. Two only — 3+ means they were unlikely to reply anyway.
  - **VM #1 (~15s, context only)**: Curiosity context → look for the email → Miyagi-style incentive to reply → name **last** so they can find the thread (name-first = insta-delete).
  - **VM #2 (~30s, context + social proof)**: Same, plus how you help peers; still no solution — that’s the email. Tone = Heard The Name / calling a referral, not gatekeeper-gruff.

- **Tie emails to calls (sequence)**: Typical 10–14 touches / ~30 days. Early pattern: Day 1 email; Day 3 call+follow-up; Day 5 call+follow-up.
  - **Email 1** = first 60 seconds in writing: context, triggering problem, one-sentence solution, interest CTA.
  - Later emails/VMs **do not re-pitch**. They pull attention back to email 1 (short “left a VM / any thoughts?” then a bit more: case study / “mind telling me if it’s not a fit?”).
  - Pro tip: Keep email 1 open while dialing — it *is* your research card.

## Key Concepts
- **Slide-by**: First-name, assume the transfer.
- **Dose information**: Reveal only under pressure; extra reasons increase suspicion.
- **Voicemail goal = email reply**, not callback.
- **Number rotation**: Capture VM email-lift without smoking future connect rate.

## Mental Models
- Use **Slide-by first** the way a building owner walks to the elevator — ID only if stopped twice.
- Use **context then social proof** for both gatekeepers and VMs — same staircase, different tone.
- Use **one pitched email, two VMs** when running a sequence; more pitching is noise.
- After impassable GK: **cell, before 8–9 / lunch / after 5, or email/LinkedIn/direct mail** — not a fourth verbal fight.

## Anti-patterns
- **Schmooze the EA** or **pitch the EA**.
- **Lying about identity** (unethical; blows up).
- **Full name + company on the first breath**.
- **Infomercial voicemails** / asking for a callback / three-plus VMs.
- **Parallel redial “we got disconnected”** energy on GKs — fight 1–2 times, then bypass the channel.

## Worked Example
*Triple Bypass (reconstructed)*

GK: “Aubrey Graham’s office.”
You: “Hey — could you get me over to Aubrey? It’s Nick.”
GK: “Nick who? What’s this regarding?”
You: “It’s about the press release on the Rochester office. Would you let him know it’s Nick Cegelski?”
GK: “And what company?”
You: “We work with a few other firms in the region on insurer billing — Banana Billing. I sent him a note the other day; mind letting him know it’s Nick?”

*VM #1 (reconstructed, ~15s)*: “Hey Jane — calling about the Sequoia raise and how other portfolio CHROs are handling the merit cycle. No need to play tag; I just emailed you. If it’s a miss, a quick ‘not a fit’ keeps us off your line. It’s Armand from Pave.”

## Key Takeaways
1. Belong; don’t befriend or pitch gatekeepers.
2. Slide-by → context → social proof; questions back; no lies.
3. Impassable GK: mobile, off-hours, other channel. Don’t grind a 39% penalty.
4. Leave two VMs to lift email replies, not to get callbacks; rotate numbers.
5. VM: context first, name last, point to email, 15s then 30s with proof.
6. One problem-prop email; everything else is a pointer back to it.
