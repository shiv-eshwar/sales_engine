# Chapter 12: The Law of Familiarity

## Core Idea
**Law of Familiarity**: the more familiar they are with you, your brand, and/or company, the more likely they accept/return calls, open email, connect socially, reply to texts, attend events, download, engage, and buy. Unfamiliarity is why you get so many time objections. Familiarity **lubricates** prospecting by making the yes to time feel less risky. Balance familiarity work with selling today or you empty the pipe.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Law of Familiarity** + touch-count evidence (see also Ch 9): ~20–50 touches if little/no familiarity vs. ~1–10 for inactive customers, warm inbound, or high familiarity.

- **Familiarity Threshold** (Sean Burke / KiteDesk): Point where they communicate on a first-name basis even when not buying now — including social inboxes and text without feeling intrusive. You will not cross it with most names; it costs serious time/intellect/emotion/energy/tech. Use **conquest lists + strategic plans** so investment lands on most valuable prospects.

- **Five Levers of Familiarity**:
  1. **Persistent, consistent daily prospecting** — each call, email, face-to-face, card, VM increases name recognition (including with gatekeepers).
  2. **Referrals and introductions** — instant credibility on someone else’s trust. Types: **customer** (happy clients; systematic ask), **personal** (friends/family; educate on ideal prospect and remind), **professional** (non-competing related sellers; mutual investment). Secret common to referral books: legendary customer experience + **ask**.
  3. **Networking** — the real social prospecting. Chambers, civic calendars, events your buyers attend. Go. Do not sell or brochure. Ask, listen, interest in them (Angelou: they remember how you made them feel). Follow up: handwritten note (prestamped kit in the car), optional thank-you text + LinkedIn, CRM by next morning, promised actions within 24 hours, then regular follow-up into the pipe.
  4. **Company and brand familiarity** — big brand makes interrupting easier (sometimes the name is enough). Start-up/small/unknown: sales *is* brand-building (blogs, shows, social, white papers, podcasts, webinars). Big-brand reps: stay out of marketing’s gears and let the machine run.
  5. **Personal branding** — they buy you as the person who can solve their unique problem. Content is easy now; **secret weapon: speak in public regularly**. Chambers, Rotary, trade groups, meeting planners want speakers; webinars/live streams are weaker but usable. After talks, prospects seek you out and hand over issues and contact info.

## Key Concepts
- **Prospecting lubrication**: Familiarity reduces perceived risk of giving time.
- **Senior-rep ease**: Years on the five levers, not magic.
- **Ask script pattern**: Thank them, they’re happy, you’re adding customers like them — would they introduce others in their network?
- **No quid pro quo at events**: Connections first; appointments and leads after.
- **Warning**: Familiarity work is easy to hide in. A month of only branding → empty pipe and an angry boss. Balance sales today vs. investment in the future.

## Mental Models
- Use **conquest-only threshold investment** when time is scarce — not every name deserves first-name texting rights.
- Use **referral ask as a system**, not a mood: if everyone claims great service but nobody asked last quarter, the lever is unused.
- Use **networking = listen** when you tend to pitch in the room.
- Use **public speaking** when competitors all look the same on LinkedIn.
- Prefer **daily touches** as the first lever — branding without interrupts is not prospecting.

## Anti-patterns
- **Familiarity as avoidance**: Content, social, and events instead of Power Hours.
- **Never asking for referrals**: Fear of no, or simply forgetting — even with “great service.”
- **Networking as a booth brochure**: Quid pro quo, talking about you.
- **No follow-up** after events (notes, CRM, 24-hour promises).
- **Start-up waiting for marketing** instead of all-hands awareness.
- **Big-brand rep fighting the marketing machine** instead of riding it.

## Worked Example
**Introduction lever.** With a happy training client: “You still have ties to your old company. Who decides sales training there?” He names Mary Walker, former direct report, and offers the number. Ask for the call, not just the number. He dials: “I’m with Jeb Blount, he’s been helping our curriculum. You two need to meet. He’ll call you.” Instant familiarity.

**Referral gap check.** Room of ~100 B2B reps: all hands up for “great service.” Zero asked last week or last month. One in the last quarter. Three in the last year. The missing discipline is the ask, not a clever script.

**Networking close-the-loop.** Meet someone at chamber; ask about their world; write a handwritten note in the car referencing one specific topic; send a short text; LinkedIn connect; log the lead next morning; if you promised an intro or asset, do it within 24 hours; keep touching until they enter the pipe.

**Speaking.** Volunteer at Rotary as subject-matter expert. After the talk, three attendees bring problems to you. That is inbound created by personal brand — still log it and book real appointments (Ch 9), don’t confuse applause with a full pipe.

## Key Takeaways
1. Familiarity drives engagement rates; cold unknown names can take 20–50 touches.
2. Lubricate interrupts by investing in the five levers — especially daily prospecting and referrals.
3. Cross the Familiarity Threshold only on conquest-level names.
4. Referrals: legendary experience + ask (customer, personal, professional).
5. Network to connect, not to sell; follow up within 24 hours.
6. Personal brand secret weapon: speak in public.
7. Balance: familiarity without today’s interrupts empties the pipe.
