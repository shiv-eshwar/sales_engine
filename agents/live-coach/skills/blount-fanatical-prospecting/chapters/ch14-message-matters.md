# Chapter 14: Message Matters

## Core Idea
There is no magic pitch. Prospecting messages exist for one job: **quickly persuade them to give you time**. Prospects meet for their reasons (WIIFM), not yours. Words plus nonverbal delivery either raise resistance or pull the wall down. Ask. Assume yes. Shut up. Handle RBOs (Ch 16).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **What you say + how you say it**: Seconds to earn attention. Delivery (tone, body, dress, written grammar) transfers emotion; people respond in kind. Horses dump riders who smell fear — prospects do the same.

- **Confidence and enthusiasm as primary nonverbals**: You can manufacture them via posture, face, self-talk, clothes, assumptive language. Cuddy power posing (testosterone/cortisol); smile in the voice (mirror by the phone); “I’m awesome” as mood lever. Act enthusiastic → feel enthusiastic.

- **WIIFM + because (Langer copier study)**: Ask with no reason ~60% yes; “because I’m in a hurry” ~94%; even a nonsense because (“because I have to make copies”) ~93%. The word **because** matters more than a perfect reason. Do **not** use nonsense in sales — use a simple, 10-second, good-enough because. C-level/conquest: specific, germane case. SMB + known brand: “learn more about you” can be enough (low time-risk).

- **Possible Value Propositions** (Art Sobczak): For each class of DM/role, pre-define likely WIIFMs. Jill Konrath VP: measured business objective + disrupt status quo + proof. Mike Weinberg Power Statement: issues + offerings that address them + differentiators (why they choose *you*).

- **Two bridge types**:
  - **Targeted bridge**: Common to a large similar group (role, vertical, application). Use when research-per-name is not worth it (e.g. 10k SMBs). Infer from trends/peers/competitors; iterate from live calls.
  - **Strategic bridge**: Unique to one high-value name/role. Research (alerts, CRM, site, social, trade press); use their language; Platinum Hour work. One shot at C-level — make the because count.

- **Three value types in the because**:
  1. **Emotional** — name painful states (stress, worry, fear, frustration) and offer peace of mind, options, less worry, hope.
  2. **Insight/curiosity** — power/leverage, competitive unknown, best practices they may lack.
  3. **Tangible/logic** — how much/how many/results; technical and exec buyers.

- **People decide emotion first, justify with logic**: Feature pitches and marketing scripts fail because they skip empathy. Stand in their shoes. Kill any line that earns “So what?”

- **The only technique that works: Ask** — for appointment, info, DM, next step, sale. 9/10 “can’t get X” problems are failure to ask (fear of no).

- **Three steps of the ask**: (1) Ask with confidence and assume you will get it, (2) **Shut up**, (3) Be ready for RBOs.

- **Assumptive ask stats**: Confident/assertive ask ~**70% yes**; nonassertive ~**30%**; because on top raises further. Gitomer: assumptive position is the strongest selling strategy.

- **Rule of thirds after you shut up**: ~1/3 yes (don’t talk them out of it), ~1/3 real no (blessing — next name), ~1/3 maybe/RBO (where turnaround skill lives).

- **Risk of time**: 15-minute discovery is easier than a 1-hour demo ask. Lower the time-risk.

## Key Concepts
- **Time is the hardest ask in the whole sales process** — harder than the buy — because everyone is crazy-busy.
- **Pitch resentment**: “A few minutes to tell you about my company” = “waste an hour on me.” No features dump, #1 claims, generic stats, brochures, irrelevance.
- **Congruence**: Words, tone, and body must match or they won’t trust you. Written words *are* body language on email/text/social.
- **Amygdala / fight-or-flight**: No feels like a rattlesnake; teach the rational brain that no won’t kill you. Courage is a muscle. Duck-on-water: calm above, paddling below.
- **Passive vs assumptive language**: “Is this a good time / I was wondering / whole day open / what do you think / kinda sorta” vs “The reason I’m calling / tell me who / slot at 11 / why don’t we / lunch Monday / how about 2 PM.”

## Mental Models
- Use a **10-second because** for most names; **strategic research** only when the account is conquest/C-level.
- Use **emotion → curiosity → proof** when building the bridge, not a feature tour.
- Use **ask + silence** as efficiency: get yes/no/maybe fast.
- Prefer **good enough because** over **perfect unique VP** that eats the Power Hour.

## Anti-patterns
- **Magic-words fantasy** and long marketing scripts.
- **Me-talk**: talk/tell/show my product; “love to get together”; “I’ll be in the area.”
- **Passive ask** that begs for no.
- **Filling silence** after the ask — overexplain, pre-overcome imaginary objections, feature dump until a yes becomes a no.
- **High-pitch, too-fast, too-loud/soft, ums, no eye contact, hands in pockets, face-touching, hunched, weak handshake**.
- **Hour-long demo as the first ask**.

## Worked Example
**Targeted bridge (appointment).** “Hi Candace, Jeb Blount, Sales Gravy. Reason I’m calling is to schedule an appointment to show our sales onboarding automation. Many clients are frustrated it takes too long to ramp new sellers — that holds growth back. We typically cut onboarding time and cost ~50% and make ramp easy so new hires sell fast. I have Thursday 2 PM. Short meeting to learn about you and see if a demo makes sense?” Educated guess at her emotion (anxiety about unproductive new reps), logic proof (50%), low-risk next step, assumptive time, then silence.

**Strategic bridge (C-level).** Research: Fast Company says they’re adding 100 reps. “Hi Windsor… reason I’m calling is to set an appointment. You’re adding another hundred reps — that has to be stressful to get them producing. We cut ramp-to-ROI 50% at Xjam. I don’t know if we’re a fit; I have ideas that work for companies like yours. Short meeting Thursday 2 PM?” Their language, named stress, proof, humility, specific ask.

**Ask contrast.** Weak: “I kinda was wondering if maybe you have time if that’s okay?” Assumptive: “How about we meet at 2 PM?” Tracked pattern: assertive + because dramatically outperforms limp language. After the ask: shut up. If they say yes, book both calendars (Ch 9) and get off the phone.

## Key Takeaways
1. Messages exist to win time, not to pitch the company.
2. WIIFM + a because; Langer shows the reason-structure itself is powerful — keep it simple and 10 seconds.
3. Targeted bridges for volume lists; strategic bridges for conquest/C-level (Platinum research).
4. Emotion first, then insight, then logic; never lead with features.
5. Ask assumptively (~70% vs ~30%); then shut up (rule of thirds).
6. Manufacture confidence/enthusiasm in body and voice; congruence builds trust.
7. If you are not getting in, 9/10 you are not asking.
