# Chapter 8: Time: The Great Equalizer of Sales

## Core Idea
Everyone gets 24 hours; only a handful are **Golden Hours** for selling. Top performers block time, concentrate power, protect those hours from busywork, and push non-selling work into **Platinum Hours**. Most “I don’t have time to prospect” problems are self-inflicted. Master time and you lower stress and raise income.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **CEO Mindset (CEO of You, Inc.)**: You own results and scarce resources; scarcest resource is time. Scratch the title on your card and write CEO.
  - When to use: Anytime you blame company, CRM, or “all the stuff they pile on.”
  - How: Accountable for time, territory, CRM, and resources. Adapt around curveballs; do not use them as permission to stop filling the pipe.

- **Golden Hours vs. Platinum Hours**:
  - **Golden Hours**: Prime selling time — prospecting, qualifying, gathering information, presenting, closing. If you are not doing those, you are not doing the job.
  - **Platinum Hours**: Before/after prime selling — lists, research, precall planning, proposals, contracts, social/email prospecting (as scheduled), admin, reports, CRM, calendar, inbox.
  - When to use: Building the daily calendar. Sales work in gold; setup work in platinum (early morning, late afternoon, weekends if you want max income).

- **Time blocking / Power Hours / Concentrate Your Power**: Distinct blocks with one activity, outcome goal, and no multitasking.
  - When to use: Phone, email, social, proposals, follow-up — each in its own block.
  - How: Treat blocks as sacred appointments with yourself (you would not skip a 9 AM demo for coffee). Do Not Disturb. Lists researched in advance. Notes on the list during the block; CRM log after. Phones and email off.
  - Sales Gravy pattern: ~**120 outbound calls/day in three Power Hours** (morning, midday, afternoon). Many reps can pack the pipe with **1 hour of 25–50 teleprospecting calls plus 1 hour email/social**, results inside **60 days**.
  - Why it works: **Horstman’s Corollary** to Parkinson’s Law — work contracts to fit the time allowed. Anyone can focus for an hour. Multitasking is cycling, not parallel skill; efficiency falls as simultaneous tasks rise.

- **Protect the Golden Hours — four moves**: Delusion is not an option; **just say no** to non-mission-critical dumps; **prioritize** (you get harassed for admin, fired for missing number); **do nonsales before/after**; **delegate**.

- **Fine Art of Delegation**: Scale yourself. Clear instructions in advance, then “In God we trust; everyone else we follow up on.” Invest in support relationships (VoloMetrix: internal network correlates with sales success). Hire VA/assistant if independent.

- **Measure Your Worth**: Annual income goal ÷ (working weeks × Golden Hours) = **dollars per Golden Hour**. Use it to refuse $10/hour work (data entry, amateur plumbing) during $50/hour selling time.

- **Law of Triviality**: Tendency to waste time on unimportant work while mission-critical tasks wait. Salespeople commonly waste **50%+** of time on low-value activity.

## Key Concepts
- **80% time-management pain**: In 10,000+ workshop asks, ~80% name time as the greatest sales challenge.
- **Parkinson’s Law**: Work expands to fill allotted time (all-day “50 dials” becomes two appointments a week).
- **Horstman’s Corollary**: Shrink the window, output compresses upward (30 minutes → ~22 dials and ≥1 appointment vs. an entire idle day).
- **Beware of the Ding**: Email and mobile are the two biggest derailers; average person checks phone ~every 7 minutes; each ding costs restart time.
- **Prospecting rule number one (Iannarino)**: Do not check email first thing. First 1–2 hours = telephone prospecting block. Important ≠ urgent; if truly urgent they will call or text.
- **Worth math example**: $75k, 48 weeks, 6 Golden Hours/day → ~**$52/hour**. 12 hours of DIY repairs “to save $150” cost ~**$624** of selling time.

## Mental Models
- Use **CEO of You, Inc.** when resources and interruptions feel unfair — generate max ROI from scarce hours.
- Use **Golden vs Platinum** to sort any task: selling contact vs. everything else.
- Use **Power Hour** when daily dial standards are missed despite “being busy.”
- Use **worth per hour** when tempted to do support work “because specialists are expensive.”
- Prefer **single-focus blocks** over **mythical multitasking**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Busywork-as-prospecting-avoidance**: Admin feels important and is the perfect excuse.
- **All-day prospecting quota**: Target expands into unfocused wandering (~2 calls/hour).
- **Multitasking block**: CRM log + research + email + social + calls → ~4 calls/hour or 7 dials in 60 minutes (insurance team) vs. 47 after concentration.
- **Open inbox at dawn**: Three hours of FYI/CYA/HR/billing; almost never a 2 AM close.
- **Skipping a prospecting block for coffee** while you would never skip a customer demo.
- **Control-freak failure to delegate**: Cannot scale; prospecting dies under account babysitting.
- **Disrespecting support staff**: Destroys the internal network that high performers invest in.

## Worked Example
**Inside team, 50 dials/day standard, 3 discovery appointments goal.** 90-day actual: < half the dials, **2 appointments per week**. Excuses: meetings, CRM, voicemail, “people don’t answer mornings/Wednesdays/full moons.” Pipe empty, so “follow-up” was fiction.

Intervention: 10 minutes to pull 50 names; **30-minute block** to call 25 with a goal of 2 appointments. Result: ~**22 dials and ≥1 appointment** in 30 minutes — more than a prior full day. After coaching, ~**29 dials / 30 minutes / 2 appointments**. Same people, Horstman’s Corollary plus concentrated power.

**Ding audit:** Laura’s two-hour block: phone buzz, texts, Facebook, YouTube; email dings; 11+ focus losses; 7 minutes gone per episode. She concludes call targets are “ridiculously unreasonable.” Diagnosis: derailers, not the standard.

**Worth conversation:** DIY home repair on two selling days to avoid a $150 plumber. 12 hours × $52/hour ≈ $624. Specialist was a bargain. Apply the same math to CRM data entry in Golden Hours.

## Key Takeaways
1. Golden Hours are for selling interactions only; Platinum Hours absorb lists, research, admin, CRM, inbox.
2. Block time; treat prospecting blocks as sacred appointments; concentrate on one activity.
3. Horstman’s Corollary: shorter focused windows beat all-day wandering quotas.
4. Power Hours (e.g. 25–50 calls in an hour; three hours → ~120 dials) pack the pipe; many need only 1–2 hours/day.
5. Do not open email first; turn off dings; log CRM after the block.
6. Know your dollars per Golden Hour; delegate and say no to trivial work.
7. Time is the equalizer — same 24 hours; winners protect them.
