# Chapter 18: In-Person Prospecting

## Core Idea
Nothing replaces the same room — but **IPP is the least efficient channel**. Use it to supplement phone/email/social, not as the only motion. Plan IPPs around preset appointments (**hub-and-spoke**), add **T-Calls**, wear **sales goggles**, and walk in ready to close. “I’m better in person” is usually phone fear.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **When IPP fits**: Outside reps, local territory, transactional to semi-complex, SMB/residential, walk-in without a security wall. At large plants: mostly intel from guards/GKs. Random windshield touring burns a day and gas.

- **Kelly pattern (top uniform-rental rep)**: 1 hour phone → 2–3 qualified appointments. CRM + map: 3–5 nearby names around each appointment. LinkedIn photos of key players. **T-Call**: at the site, look right, left, and behind for doors not in the database. Result: high-value meetings **plus 10–20 face-to-face touches**. ~**30% of IPPs → future appointments**, intel on most, 1–2×/week a DM ready to talk now. Face-to-face also bypasses phone GKs.

- **Five-Step Hub-and-Spoke**:
  1. Plan IPPs around phone-block appointments (or existing-account visits).
  2. CRM list nearby (zip search).
  3. Plot 3–5 prospects around each hub.
  4. Efficient driving route — minimize windshield.
  5. Use time before/after the appointment; don’t stop until the IPP goal.
  - Kasey (restaurant supplies): 4 existing accounts/day + 3–5 spokes each = **15–20 new IPPs/day**; neighbor relationships as credibility.

- **Phone vs weather math**: Honest winter/summer IPP day often **10–20** calls then they quit. Same list, one hour phone: **25–50**. Use both.

- **IPP objectives**: Qualify (more info + visual competitor/product clues); book a return appointment; convert to a sales conversation/close if timing is right; familiarity with GK and DM; maximize the day; learn/own the territory. **Primary**: qualifying intel to feed phone/email/social lists.

- **Five-step IPP planning**: Research (T-Calls: 30-second phone scan of site/social); personalize (questions, compliments, “Billy next door has used us five years”); define the ask; **be prepared to close** (sheets, orders, contracts, deck — you may get one chance); log CRM notes/tasks same day or on the spot.

- **Five-Step IPP Call** (slower, more dialogue than phone):
  1. Approach with confidence — expect to win; questions planned.
  2. Identify + why you’re here — transparent, no cheese. Neighbor customer, park-wide service, or a specific insight (e.g. Facebook promos).
  3. Gather information — conversation not interrogation; ~80% of communication is visual; listen; **no pitch**. Attention on them.
  4. Ask for the pre-decided thing; bridge to close if the window is open.
  5. Turn around RBOs (Ch 16).

- **Sales goggles**: Look left/right/behind every appointment. New construction, trucks, uniforms, bulletin-board cards, cracked-phone users. Voice-memo/photo if you can’t stop. Awareness without action is useless. Blount: >$500k over five years from uniform/line/waiting-room/airplane conversations.

## Key Concepts
- **T-Call**: Opportunistic doors in a T around the planned stop.
- **Hub-and-spoke**: Appointments are hubs; nearby names are spokes.
- **Managers who only trust invisible reps**: Bad reason to live in the car.
- **One-chance close**: If they just fired your competitor, you must have materials.

## Mental Models
- Use **phone to buy the right to be in person** — appointments first, IPP as density around the hub.
- Use **T-Calls and goggles** so you never drive past the next sale.
- Prefer **listen/qualify** over **pitch**; pitching makes you identical to every other walk-in.
- Carry close-kit always: timing luck is useless if you’re empty-handed.

## Anti-patterns
- **Aimless driving** as “working the territory.”
- **IPP-only** because of phone fear or “better in person.”
- **Walk out of the complex** without the other suites (Madison).
- **Unprepared close**: owner fired competitor, wants a presentation now; “I’ll come back in two weeks” → they sign someone faster.
- **Pitch dump** the moment you’re inside.
- **Gather intel and never log it**.
- **Notice cracked phones / new signs and never stop**.

## Worked Example
**Hub day:** Morning Power Hour books two appointments. CRM zip plot: five spokes around #1, four around #2. Map the loop. Before appointment 1, two IPPs: qualify DMs and competitor. After, T-Call the rest of the park.

**Third door (Carl):** Owner overheard, came out — just fired the competitor, “close me” signals, wants a presentation. Carl has no materials. Owner leaves for conference then vacation; “call in two weeks.” Two weeks later: already signed the faster vendor. Rule: close-kit in the trunk, every IPP.

**Transparent openers:** “ABC next door is a customer and said I should introduce myself to Mary.” / “I serve several businesses in this park and want to learn whether we’d be a fit.” / “I’ve been following your Facebook promos — we have a tool that might lift them; is Jerry in so I can ask a few questions?”

**Goggles:** Delivery truck parked — quiz the driver on DM, window, competitor failure. Uniform in line — who decides. Card on the diner board — photo, CRM tonight, call tomorrow.

## Key Takeaways
1. IPP supplements a balanced mix; it is the slowest channel — plan it or it wastes the day.
2. Hub-and-spoke around phone-set appointments; T-Call the T.
3. Primary job is qualify + familiarity; be ready to close on the spot.
4. Five-step call: confident, transparent, listen, ask, RBO.
5. Sales goggles + action; awareness without a stop is theater.
6. Log everything the same day.
