# Chapter 20: Text Messaging

## Core Idea
Text is the third rail of prospecting: impersonal as a medium, extremely personal as a domain. That tension makes it powerful *and* easy to feel like spam. **Familiarity is everything.** Use text as an integrated follow-on after phone, email, social, or in-person — not as a cold standalone. Lead360: text alone **4.8%** conversion; same text after a phone contact **+112.6%**. Random texts to strangers = annoying spam.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **When to text**: After familiarity exists. Gambit to a hard-to-reach name only when the window is slamming shut and you have nothing to lose — still low probability.

- **Networking anchor sequence**:
  1. In the conversation, when they vaguely agree to meet: “Sounds good — I’ll text you and we’ll get together.”
  2. Walk away → personalized LinkedIn request (anchors the name).
  3. Within 24 hours (or +2 days if travel): thank-you text + meeting ask, personalized with something from the talk.
  4. No response → one more text a day later (unknown number often ignored).
  5. Still nothing → shift to phone and email. Do not keep texting (ill will).
  - Bonus: handwritten note within a week.

- **Trigger-event texts**: Status-quo disruption + urgency of SMS. Soft: send a relevant article/resource link, or “saw X — how are things?” Only if they already know you. Value first → phone conversation.

- **Nurture texts** (qualified, not in window): Mix with quarterly calls, monthly email, social. Reserve *best* value for SMS. Matt (BI software): demo loved, no buy (integration hours + capex vs status quo). He kept need + means, no urgency. Texts of relevant articles (often *not* about his product), sports chat, “enjoyed your podcast.” Stay top of mind without a pitch. When the window opens, he is the vendor.

- **Engagement texts**: Congratulate promotion, press, award; sincere comment on their content. No solicitation. Make them feel important → they engage.

- **Seven rules**:
  1. Identify yourself (name + company) — they don’t have your number saved.
  2. Message matters — complete sentences; tone is easy to misread.
  3. Direct and brief — 1–4 short sentences, **<250 characters** when possible; no emoticons.
  4. No abbreviations/acronyms/slang (LOL/OMG/WTF).
  5. Transparent full URLs, never shortened.
  6. Pause and reread before send.
  7. Know your numbers — texts sent, response, conversion.
  - **DO NOT TEXT WHILE DRIVING.**

## Key Concepts
- **Personal-haven inbox**: Family/friends/coworkers; spam rarely belongs. That’s why reactions to “text prospecting” go nuclear.
- **Compelled to read immediately** — power and danger.
- **Mobile/VoIP/BYOD**: Text is now a first-class business channel technically; socially it still requires permission via familiarity.

## Mental Models
- Use text to **anchor a live moment** or **nudge a known relationship**, never to interrupt a stranger.
- Use **value-only SMS** in nurture; pitch belongs on phone/meeting.
- Prefer **one retry then channel-shift** over SMS persistence.
- Think **Law of Familiarity, compressed to 250 characters**.

## Anti-patterns
- Cold texts to unknown mobiles.
- Pitch in the first nurture text.
- Unidentified number, emojis, abbreviations, shortened links.
- Third, fourth, fifth texts after silence.
- Texting while driving.
- Treating 4.8% standalone conversion as “text doesn’t work” instead of “text without familiarity doesn’t work.”

## Worked Example
**Event:** Positive 8-minute talk about their hiring surge. “I’ll text you and we’ll get together.” In the hallway: LinkedIn with a one-line reference to hiring. Next morning: “Jeb Blount, Sales Gravy — great talking last night about the 30-rep ramp. How about Thursday 10 AM to see if we can help you staff faster?” Silence. One more text 24 hours later. Still silence → phone + AMMO email. Handwritten note Friday.

**Trigger (known contact):** Competitor launched a threatening product. Text a full-URL article plus “thought of your team — curious how you’re seeing this.” They reply; you call.

**Nurture (Matt pattern):** After a no-for-now, quarterly check-in calls; monthly email; Twitter engagement; SMS reserved for the one article they’ll actually read. They thank you; three-line sports chat; relationship stays warm until the upgrade project ends and budget appears.

## Key Takeaways
1. Text is personal; familiarity first or you are spam.
2. After phone, conversion can more than double vs. text alone.
3. Best uses: post-event appointment, trigger resource, nurture value, sincere congratulations.
4. Identify, brief, professional, full URLs, one retry, then other channels.
5. Track it like any other channel. Never text and drive.
