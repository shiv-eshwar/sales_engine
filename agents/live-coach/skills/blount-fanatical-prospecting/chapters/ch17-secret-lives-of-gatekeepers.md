# Chapter 17: The Secret Lives of Gatekeepers

## Core Idea
There is **no secret technique** past gatekeepers. They exist to protect the boss’s time and are people with a job, a boss, and emotions. Edge comes from manners, likeability, and people-savvy — plus persistence. High-value conquests are walled for a reason; persistence always wins.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Seven Keys for Dealing with Gatekeepers**:
  1. **Be likable** — cheerful, polite, respectful; never rude/pushy. Leave a positive impression of you and the company.
  2. **Use please, please** (Mike Brooks): “Would you please connect me to Mike Brooks, please?”
  3. **Be transparent** — full name + company. Disclosure sounds professional and pass-worthy.
  4. **Connect** — if you speak often, ask how they are; listen to tone; family/interests. Some GKs you know better than the boss; they put you on calendars.
  5. **Hold the cheese** — no tricks. Tricks = do-not-talk list (“snow at the equator”). Honesty is remembered and opens later.
  6. **Ask for help** — authentic plea + light humor can flip an adversary into an ally.
  7. **Change the game** — sidestep: call early/late (boss in, GK out); social inbox (GK rarely has it); in-person at conferences/clubs/shows; email; **handwritten note** with real value or congratulations (a brochure is not value).

- **Three hacks when you cannot get a name**:
  - **Calling-Other-Extensions**: Random internal extensions until a friendly human gives a clue; then use that name with reception (“Dale in purchasing was transferring me to Jack in IT…”). Confirm the real name, grab the extension “in case we disconnect.”
  - **Salespeople-Help-Salespeople**: Press 1 for sales. Honest: you’re a fellow seller hitting a wall, looking for who buys X. They pick up, know who’s who, empathize, often hand mobile numbers and intel.
  - **Go-Around-Back** (in person): Loading docks, smokers, parking lot — transparent, nonaggressive. **Do not** if security is there to keep you out.

## Key Concepts
- **Tug-of-war**: Sellers experiment with foolish schemes; GKs learn to hate the category (would rather have teeth pulled).
- **No avoidance**: You will deal with GKs; design for it.
- **Elusive high-value DMs**: Don’t answer, don’t return, don’t connect, don’t fill forms — that’s the real world. Hard work + persistence.

## Mental Models
- Use **please ×2 + transparency** as the default opener.
- Use **relationship with the GK** as a long game on accounts you will call for years.
- Use **channel sidestep** when the front door is a wall — social, handwriting, early/late, events.
- Use **fellow-seller empathy** when the switchboard is brutal.

## Anti-patterns
- **Tricks and cheese** that get you blacklisted.
- **Rudeness** born of frustration.
- **Treating GKs as obstacles, not people**.
- **Quitting because reception said they don’t give out names**.
- **Around-back with security present**.

## Worked Example
**Humor + help:** Receptionist: “Are you back again? I told you we’re not interested.” Smile: “I came to see you — I hadn’t gotten quite enough rejection today to fill my quota.” Laugh → conversation → “I really need help” → she calls the DM → meeting.

**Extensions hack (Evan, grocery HQ):** No name, no title, only “someone in corporate” for broadband. Reception refuses. He strings clues, then dials random extensions. Friendly voice: “Jack in IT?” Reception: “I’m not seeing Jack — did you mean Zack Freedman?” “Yes, Zack — and his extension in case we disconnect? 5642.” VM, then relationship, then largest customer.

**Sales-help-sales:** Brutal GK hangs up mid-question. Dial again, press 1 for sales. “Hi Mike, Jeb Blount. Trying to reach whoever buys training; switchboard isn’t helping; figured a fellow salesperson might.” Mike: Jean, VP Sales, mobile number; plus why they’re hiring 30 reps and that training is nonexistent. Next call: Jean answers ring two; 15 minutes → appointment including the president → proposal.

## Key Takeaways
1. No magic past GKs — likeability, manners, transparency, persistence.
2. Please twice; never trick; connect as humans.
3. Sidestep: hours, social inbox, events, email, handwritten value notes.
4. Hacks: other extensions, fellow salespeople, around back (no security).
5. The more valuable the account, the thicker the wall; persistence always wins.
