# Chapter 19: E-Mail Prospecting

## Core Idea
Email, used intelligently, opens doors and gets **10–20× more engagement than social**. It extends the prospecting window (write in Platinum Hours, send in Golden). Done wrong — spam, bulk, pitch dumps — you get blocked and look like a chump. Three cardinal rules: **delivered, opened, convert**. Plan with **AMMO**; write **Hook → Relate → Bridge → Ask**. Same rules for LinkedIn InMail, Facebook Messenger, Twitter DM.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three Cardinal Rules**:
  1. **Delivered** — one-to-one from your address (bulk from personal mail is the fastest blacklist). Avoid images, hyperlinks (if needed: full visible URL, never shortened, max one including signature), attachments. No ALL CAPS, “free,” “buy now,” spam lexicon in subject. Drip multiple people at one company across the day. Don’t over-mail one person (they mark spam → enterprise blacklist). Scrub bounces; find the real address. Sensitive industries (finance, defense, health): text only.
  2. **Opened** — execs get 200+ emails/day; scan-and-triage. **Familiarity** (Law of Familiarity) plus a subject that screams open. **Triple threat**: VM + LinkedIn ping + email (any order). Layer channels on purpose, not randomly.
  3. **Convert** — response that is an appointment, info, intro, forward, download/webinar, or conversation. Conquest/C-level/SPC: personalized. Vertical templates OK only if research makes each feel unique.

- **Subject line**: 3–6 words or **40–50 characters**; >50 kills mobile opens (~50%+ of opens). No questions (delete bait). Action/directive/list/testimonial/referral (“Jeb Blount Said We Should Talk”) / issue-or-pride (“Loved Your Book!”). About them, not you. Humor when relevant (“Keep Those Kegs Rolling”). **Test** with Yesware/Tout/Signals/Tellwise — no one-size formula.

- **AMMO plan**: Audience (role, style, consume-when, familiarity) → Method (short/detailed, hard/soft, standalone vs SPC, cross-platform) → Message (what gets attention, what’s important, what earns the ask) → Outcome (the one action).

- **Four elements of the email**:
  1. **Hook** — subject + first sentence (~3-second “glimpse factor”). Name only — no Hi/Hello/Dear (salesperson tell). Opening about *them*.
  2. **Relate** — emotion; you get their problem (or cite peers in the role if you are not them).
  3. **Bridge** — WIIFM in *their* language (growth, risk, resources — not your INC 5000 brag).
  4. **Ask** — clear, easy, assumptive time. Disrupt (“might not be a fit”) + “learn about your unique challenges” + short/low-risk + “How about Thursday 3 PM?” Do not dump calendar work on them or offer a “free consultation” pitch.

- **Send timing**: When they will open *and act*. Typical B2B: first thing to mid-morning. B2C: when they act. Test; schedule from Platinum Hours. Timing articles are mostly noise.

- **Pause before send**: Proof twice; walk away 10 minutes; print the important ones. Typos are brand damage.

## Key Concepts
- **Social inbox as email proxy**: skip GK, skip spam box, no address required — still not a license to spam.
- **Address sourcing**: ask, Google/social, scrape/guess tools — then still write one-to-one.
- **Worst emails**: jargon novels, feature dumps, cheerleading, wrong name, mosquito-attention-span length. Blount deletes 99.9%.
- **Research fail as brand fail**: sales trainer InMailing a sales trainer without reading the profile.

## Mental Models
- Use **email for scale-with-craft**: more response than social, less live than phone; never bulk.
- Use **triple threat** when a name must open.
- Use **AMMO then four beats** so you don’t write from your product out.
- Prefer **short + their language + one ask** over **impressive pitch**.

## Anti-patterns
- Bulk, images, links, attachments, spam words, ALL CAPS.
- Question-mark subjects; long subjects; “Hi Jeb”; “I was browsing LinkedIn.”
- “I’d love to…” + “pick a time that works” + free pitch.
- Templates with zero research.
- Persistence that earns a spam click.
- Sending unproofed.

## Worked Example
**Converts (COO / bank):**  
Subject: `COO — The Toughest Job in the Bank`  
Lawrence, Ernst & Young says the COO has the toughest C-suite role. COOs I work with say banking complexity made the job harder and more stressful. We help COOs reduce complexity/stress: growth and profit, credit risk, resource allocation, fewer regulatory surprises. I don’t know if we’re a fit; short call to learn your unique challenges, then we decide on a deeper conversation. How about next Thursday at 3 PM?

**Bombs (Brandon, cloud software):** Subject `Cloud Based Software`. “Hi Jeb, I was browsing Linked-in and wanted to reach out.” Features dump. INC 5000 brag. “I’d love to schedule… pick a time for a free consultation.” All about him; burden on the buyer; expected salesperson pattern.

**Triple threat:** Leave the five-step VM (Ch 15) → personalized LI comment on their post → AMMO email the next morning. Name is already familiar when the subject hits.

## Key Takeaways
1. Email >> social for response; still one-to-one, never bulk spam.
2. Delivered / opened / convert — miss one and the rest is wasted time.
3. Short, non-question, them-centered subjects; test.
4. Familiarity layers (phone + social + email) raise opens.
5. Hook, Relate, Bridge, Ask; assumptive time; disrupt “might not be a fit.”
6. Write Platinum, send when they act; proof before send.
7. Same craft on social inboxes.
