# Chapter 21: Developing Mental Toughness

## Core Idea
Salespeople are the elite athletes of the company: no you, no customers, no company. Superstars get more rejection before 9 AM than most people get in a year. **Mental toughness (grit)** matters more than talent, experience, education, skill, or technique — and unlike talent, it can be learned. Change your mindset, change your game. **Grind to shine.** You control only actions, reactions, and mindset. Losing and mediocrity are choices.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Loehr’s seven dimensions of mental toughness**: self-confidence; attention control; minimize negative energy; increase positive energy; maintain motivation; attitude control; visual/imagery control.

- **Croner/Abraham three dimensions (sales grit)**:
  - **Optimism** — mother of perseverance; if you can look up, you can get up.
  - **Competitiveness** — hate-to-lose drives longer/harder work; mother of persistence.
  - **Need for achievement** (Murray) — prolonged effort at something difficult; mother of self-motivation.

- **Four Pillars of Mental Toughness in Sales**:
  1. **Desire** — Hill: not hope/wish but pulsating desire. Stanton: mother of sales activity. Answer: What do you want? How will you get it? How bad do you want it? **Write the plan** or you become part of someone else’s. Written goals create unstoppable action past 3Ps.
  2. **Mental resilience** — sharpen the saw (Covey image via chainsaw story): slow down to speed up. **Outlearn = outearn.** 15 minutes/day professional reading × ~250 weekdays ≈ **62.5 hours ≈ 21 books/year**. Drive time = Automobile University (Ziglar). Podcasts/audio. Zero sympathy for salespeople who won’t read.
  3. **Physical resilience** — mental energy is capped by body. Exercise **30–60 min/day** (or 10+10+10); standing call blocks; 7–9 hours sleep; eat for a race car; **cardinal rule: eat breakfast** before the prospecting block. Sitting all day slows the brain.
  4. **Feed your attitude** — you are what you believe. Two shared beliefs of positive people: **expect to win**; **everything happens for a reason** (learn, don’t “why me?”). Self-awareness when altitude drops. Change: company you keep, self-talk (“cannot afford a negative thought”), input (news/talk radio off), focus (stop the defeat tape), **gratitude** as cornerstone.

- **When you’re on top, attack yourself**: Second place, attack the leader. First place, attack yourself. Celebrate, then raise the bar — 30-Day Rule still applies. Steve Young: compete against yesterday. Winners pick apart brilliant performances for small gains.

- **Churchill**: When going through hell, keep going. Faith that daily right things compound even with no evidence yet. Persistence is the fuel of winners. Embrace the suck of prospecting.

## Key Concepts
- **It’s what you sell today**, not what you sold.
- **Grit vs. DNA**: talent helps; washouts usually chose to quit.
- **Cusp-of-success quit**: new-job darkness; things look bleaker just before breakthrough.
- **Mediocrity magnet**: mediocre behaviors attract bad luck.

## Mental Models
- Use **written desire** when 3Ps and rejection hit — the plan is the counterweight.
- Use **15-minute reading + car university** as the cheapest resilience program.
- Use **body as the battery** for Power Hours: sleep, breakfast, movement.
- Use **attack yourself** after a win so the 30-Day Rule doesn’t empty next quarter.
- Prefer **lean into the wall** over **job-hopping for a new toolkit**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Quit at day 29** of a new role (worked example: $7,000 of commissions closed on *her* pipe after she left — she got zero).
- **Born-not-made fatalism**.
- **Contentment on the mountain** after President’s Club.
- **Misery company**, negative self-talk, news diet, defeat-tape loops.
- **Won’t read / won’t train**.
- **Sleep-deprivation badge**; skip breakfast; desk-drawer candy for fuel.
- **Sharpen-later**: new saw bogging on the trunk because ego won’t pause.

## Worked Example
**Quit-too-soon:** New advertising rep. Heart-to-heart: first 60 days will be the hardest — rejection, mistakes, embarrassment on an unfamiliar product. She works 29 days, then quits: overwhelming, no success, maybe not a fit. Team follows her pipe and closes almost all of it. **$7,000** she would have earned. Churchill/faith would have carried the last mile.

**Sharpen the saw:** Fallen tree, new chainsaw heroics, hour of easy limbs, then the trunk wins. Son: sharpen it. Ego: it’s new. After a rasp, butter again. Parallel: business doubling after a seminar/books/blogs — slow down to speed up.

**15-minute math:** 250 days × 15 min = 21 professional books. Lunch, waiting rooms, commute. Same person, different mind.

**On top:** Trophy week. Temptation: downhill from here. Counter: new number, new challenge, dissect the winning quarter for leaks (skipped Friday blocks, CRM trash). Attack yourself.

## Key Takeaways
1. Grit > talent; it is learnable via mindset.
2. You control actions, reactions, mindset; quitting is a choice, often on the cusp.
3. Four pillars: desire (written), mental resilience (outlearn), physical resilience (move/sleep/food/breakfast), attitude (expect to win + meaning + gratitude).
4. 15 minutes of reading a day and Automobile University compound.
5. After winning, attack yourself; the 30-Day Rule does not pause for trophies.
6. Grind to shine — success is paid in advance with prospecting.
