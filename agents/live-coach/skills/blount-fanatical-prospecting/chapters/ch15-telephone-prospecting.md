# Chapter 15: Telephone Prospecting Excellence

## Core Idea
The telephone is the most powerful prospecting tool — most efficient (more touches per hour than doors or even email) and more effective than email/social/text because you are live. “Nobody answers” is a myth told by people who won’t dial. Nobody likes it; get over it. **Eat the frog**: first-thing daily phone block. Five-step call, five-step voicemail, no call-timing cop-out.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Nobody answers a phone that doesn’t ring**: Boot camp example — 19 agents, **1,311 dials**, **51% live contact**. Typical contact **15–80%** by industry/role; business services often **25–40%**. Phone contact **~5 points higher** than 1990s (mobiles follow people; fewer peers calling so you stand out; inbox burnout). Far above email response; light-years above social.

- **Phone vs. street math**: Even on a busy street, ~10–20 face-to-face qualifies/appointments in 8 hours (weather/parking). One hour on a targeted list: **25–50 calls** at 1–2 minutes each — twice the touches in ~1/10 the time, indoors.

- **Sacred phone block**: 1–2 hours, devices/email off, DND, dial goal. Chunk into 10-call or 30-minute bites; countdown 50→1; gamify nos. Sales Gravy **Power Hours / Power Half Hours**. First **1–2 hours of the day** (peak energy; fewer prospect fires).

- **Five-Step Simple Telephone Prospecting Framework** (no pauses — pause loses control; 10 seconds to the point):
  1. **Get attention with their name**: “Hi, Julie.” Do **not** ask “How are you doing?” — that pause triggers the salesperson-escape reflex (“not interested” / “who is this?”).
  2. **Identify yourself**: Name + company. Transparency = professional + lowers their stress.
  3. **Tell why you’re calling**: “The reason I’m calling is to set an appointment…”
  4. **Bridge / because**: Their problems, not talk/tell/show my stuff. Emotional + relevant language from Ch 14.
  5. **Ask + shut up**: Specific time, or qualifying question, or open question into a sales conversation.

- **Five-Step Voicemail (doubles callbacks)** when it **matters** (conquest, in-window). Skip VM on unqualified spray lists — 20–30 seconds each can burn 10–15 minutes of an hour; callback rates are **single digits**.
  1. Identify yourself (name + company).
  2. **Say phone number twice, slowly**, up front.
  3. Reason/purpose of the call — honest.
  4. Reason to call back (curiosity: insight, resources, pricing, problem).
  5. Repeat name; **number twice again** (four times total — awkward for you, easy for them).
  - Cap **30 seconds**. Auto-delete: no number, long-winded, garbled number.

- **Eat That Frog (Tracy) / swallow a toad (Chamfort)**: Phone is the hardest, highest-leverage, ugliest task. Staring doesn’t help. Timing the market loses to dollar-cost averaging — **daily scheduled dials beat “best time” hunting**. Insight Squared-type studies (e.g. Tuesday 10–4) often show **~1.3%** day-to-day gaps — noise that becomes an excuse. Monday/Friday/morning/lunch/afternoon/EOD are all used as cop-outs until the pipe is dead.

## Key Concepts
- **Skinny kids**: Salespeople who won’t interrupt don’t eat.
- **Most never taught**: Training is missing, or written by marketing/HR who have never interrupted a live prospect. Scripts that would make you slit a wrist.
- **Stipulations**: Lots of rejection (more live interactions than other channels); most calls hit VM (connect often **20–50%**); frustration comes from overcomplicating a simple process; you will still hate it and still must master it.
- **Tonya/outside-rep trap**: Of course you’re better in person — that’s why you were hired. Time is money; phone covers ground so in-person time is spent on qualified meetings (Ch 18).
- **Avoid in the bridge**: “Talk about my product / love to show you / tell you about our new service / I’ll be in the area.” Prefer: learn, share insights/best practices, understand unique situation, fit, options, peace of mind, save, frustrated, stressed, time, money.

## Mental Models
- Use the **phone as default interrupt** when you need efficiency and a live answer.
- Use **framework not script**: same five beats, swapped because and ask by objective (appointment vs. info vs. instant conversation).
- Use **VM only on names that justify the time tax**.
- Prefer **eat the frog at 8 AM** over **perfect-timing mythology**.

## Anti-patterns
- **“Nobody answers / cold calling is dead”** from non-callers (including the exec who hired phone training).
- **How are you today? + pause** — instant RBO.
- **Cheeseball long scripts**.
- **Talking after the ask**.
- **VM on every unqualified dial**.
- **Call-timing religion** (can’t call Monday… Friday… mornings…).
- **I’m better in person** as phone avoidance.
- **Admin/social/email after one brush-off**.

## Worked Example
**Appointment call (no pauses):** “Hi Julie. My name is Jeb Blount, Sales Gravy. The reason I’m calling is to set up an appointment. I read you’re adding 200 sales seats this year. Several companies in your industry already source exclusively through us and are happy with results. Best place to start is a short meeting on your recruiting challenges. How about Wednesday around 3 PM?” [silence]

**Information objective:** New restaurant on the bypass — ask how they buy kitchen equipment and when selection starts; because: design team early → more options, lower construction/labor via layout.

**Inbound white-paper, convert to conversation:** You downloaded the landing-page paper — what triggered that? Other CMOs struggling for quality leads; happy to share practices. “Can you tell me more about your situation?”

**Voicemail:** “Hi Rick, Jeb Blount, Sales Gravy. 1-888-360-2249, that’s 1-888-360-2249. Reason I’m calling: you downloaded our cold-calling paper; I want to learn what triggered that. I also have extra resources on VM and phone prospecting you may be curious about. Let’s talk this week. 1-888-360-2249, 1-888-360-2249.”

**Eat-the-frog day:** Block 8–10 AM, 50 dials in 10-call chunks, list already ranked (Ch 10), CRM notes after the hour. Ignore the “Tuesday at 10:12” article.

## Key Takeaways
1. Phone is the highest-leverage prospecting tool; contact rates beat email/social; “nobody answers” is usually “nobody dials.”
2. One hour of targeted dials beats a day of random knocking for coverage.
3. Sacred first-thing 1–2 hour block; Power Hours; chunk goals.
4. Five steps, no “how are you,” no pauses, 10-second because, ask, shut up.
5. VM when it matters; five-step, 30 seconds, number four times; skip on junk lists.
6. Do not time the market; eat the frog every morning.
7. You will hate it and still must do it if you want peak income.
